# Get Rid Of — homeowner buyer persona (working brief)

Status: a working brief written from the homeowner stories and objections already on the mouse-pouches sales page.
It is not survey data. Replace it with the owner's own persona document when there is one.

## Who

- Owns a house (often an older one, with a basement, garage or crawl space), 35 to 65, family at home: kids, a dog or a cat.
- Found droppings in the kitchen or pantry, or hears scratching in the walls at night. Usually as the weather turns cold.
- Has already spent money: snap traps, glue traps, poison, an ultrasonic plug-in, peppermint spray, cotton balls, cheap pouches.

## What it feels like

- Disgust and embarrassment ("we're clean people"), and a worry about food, wiring and the kids' health.
- Tired of emptying traps and of finding the next one a week later.
- Does not want poison near children or pets, and dreads a dead mouse in a wall.
- Skeptical. Every product so far promised results.

## What they want

- It handled once, without killing anything in the house and without a monthly exterminator bill.
- Something they can put down in five minutes and stop thinking about.
- Proof from people like them, and their money back if it does not work.

## Objections to answer

1. "I tried peppermint / pouches. They did nothing." → why weak formulas fade, what is different here.
2. "Why do they keep coming back?" → the scent trail and the entry gap (root cause), then the pouch at the gap (root solution).
3. "Is it safe around my dog and kids?" → no poison, no rodenticide, four plant oils; keep pouches where pets and children cannot chew them.
4. "What if it doesn't work?" → 90-day money-back guarantee.
5. "How many do I need?" → one pack is 12 pouches; most houses take 3 packs (kitchen, basement, garage).

## Words they use

"scratching in the walls", "droppings", "every winter", "tried everything", "exterminator wanted $400", "don't want poison around the dog".
