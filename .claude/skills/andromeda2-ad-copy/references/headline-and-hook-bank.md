## 1. Anatomy of the first ~125 characters

Feed shows roughly 125 characters before "…See more"; Reels and Stories show one or two lines over the video. Most
readers stop there, and Andromeda reads every phrase, so this line both routes the ad and earns the click.

`[side signal] + [recognisable moment] + [one number or sensory detail] + [open loop or desire]`

- Side signal: role/identity, behaviour, situation, aspiration, or the pain/ceiling (§2).
- Moment: a time, place or gesture the reader has lived this week ("6:40 p.m. walk", "hour 9 of a shift").
- One concrete number or sensory image (C6 rule) — not three; the line must stay easy.
- Loop or desire: a question it raises, or the life moment it promises.
- Not: the brand name, "Introducing", a greeting, a floating claim ("100% natural"), the creative's on-screen text again.
- Compliance: first person or the situation; never "Are you [attribute]?" (copy-compliance.md §2).
- Count it: `python3 -c "import sys; print(len(sys.argv[1]))" "<line>"`.

Before/after on one line:
- Generic: "Introducing Glide Chews, the vet-approved joint supplement your dog will love!" (no side, no moment, floating)
- Specific: "Every evening at 6:40 Bruno and I walk to the park. This spring he started stopping at the curb." (side by situation,
  moment, number, loop)

## 2. Avatar call-outs: seven ways to signal the side (Blueprint)

"An avatar is not a demographic … it's an identity, a situation, an aspiration, or a behavior." Self-test: can you say
who the ad is for in one sentence, and does the first line signal that person?

| Way | Brand example (Blueprint's swipe list) | Template | Glide Chews line |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Call it out directly | "Serious cookware for serious meat eaters" (HexClad); "my favorite way to start my mornings as a busy mom" (AG1) | `[Product] for [identity]` / `As a [identity], …` | "For the dog who's family and just turned 10." |
| 2. One product, many avatars | HexClad: meat eaters / "worth every single penny" / "the last pans they'll ever need" | a different first line per avatar = a different concept | runners' dogs / senior dogs / big breeds |
| 3. Imply it, don't name it | "You may not know how important it is for your dog to play with toys" (BarkBox) | `You may not know how [habit only this side has]…` | "You may not know why old dogs pause at curbs." |
| 4. Name the symptom or situation | "Crush your day without the 2 p.m. crash" | `[Moment] without [the moment's problem]` | "The full park loop without the curb stop." |
| 5. Name the behaviour | "Why switch from pills to gummies" | `Why [people doing X] switch to [Y]` | "Why owners are swapping pill pockets for chews." |
| 6. Name the aspiration | Tailored Athlete: look strong | `[The self they want to be seen as]` | "Give him the walks he still wants." |
| 7. Name the pain or ceiling | "If you've hit a revenue ceiling…" (Hormozi) | `If [situation/ceiling], [promise]` | "If walks got shorter this year, read this." |

Copy can do the filtering: naming a behaviour or a known product type lets Meta find the people who respond to those
words (Blanchard's "competitor awareness" method; Blueprint's pills-to-gummies). In telehealth keep medication names out of
headlines (copy-compliance.md §5).

## 3. Opener bank

### 3a. Sixteen advertorial openers under 125 characters (C6, character counts from the source)

Rules distilled from the pages: start mid-scene or mid-identity, one concrete number, one sensory image, no brand, no
product, use the avatar's job or role so the right person self-selects. First-person openers must come from a real
person (copy-compliance.md §6).

| # | Pattern | Source opener (chars) | Template |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | End-of-shift, present tense | "I just finished work for today. My swollen legs are killing me." (63) | `I just finished [shift/task]. My [body part] is [sensory pain].` |
| 2 | Job-load + number | "I just finished work for today and my feet and knees are killing me. I'm on my feet for at least 9 hours a day." (111) | `… I'm [doing X] for at least [N] hours a day.` |
| 3 | Identity + load | "I work as a Registered Nurse for up to 16 hours a day, no less than 5 days a week." (82) | `I work as a [role] for up to [N] hours a day.` |
| 4 | Scene + dialogue + secret | "'We're at the beach Patricia, take your shoes off!' I nervously laughed... Little did she know that was a lie." (110) | `"[Line someone said]." I [reaction]. Little did they know [secret].` |
| 5 | Shock line (real survivor) | "IN 20 SECONDS, OVER 50% OF MY BODY WAS BURNED." (46) | `In [N seconds], [real event].` — sentence case, not caps, in ads |
| 6 | Myth-buster dare | "If you still think house fires only happen to other people, think again." (72) | `If you still think [risk] only happens to [others], think again.` |
| 7 | Shame first | "At work my friends started to laugh every time I took my shoes off." (67) | `At [place], [people] started to [reaction] every time I [act].` |
| 8 | Identity loss | "I felt like an 80-year-old woman, even though I was still in my 40s." (68) | `I felt like a [N]-year-old, even though I was [age].` |
| 9 | Age + number | "I am 65 and usually do not sleep more than three hours without having to get up." (80) | `I'm [age] and [specific limit with a number].` |
| 10 | Functional limit | "I can't pick up anything that weighs more than 15 pounds without shooting pain down my hip, thigh, calf and foot." (113) | `I couldn't [action] more than [N] without [sensory chain].` |
| 11 | Money pain | "Gas prices skyrocketed and it hit me hard. It got so bad I considered taking the bus to work." (93) | `[Cost] went up and [consequence]. It got so bad I [extreme step].` |
| 12 | Protector fear | "It was a call no father wants to get. My daughter had found a hidden camera in her vacation room." (97) | `It was a [call/moment] no [role] wants. [Event].` |
| 13 | Habit enemy | "Nobody actually reads the label on Q-tip boxes that says 'Do not insert into ear canal'." (88) | `Nobody reads the [label/rule] on [common object] that says [fact].` |
| 14 | Bandwagon | "These insoles are being hyped by nurses around the world. Here's why." (69) | `[Product type] being [talked about] by [avatar group]. Here's why.` (only if true) |
| 15 | Stop the old way | "Before spending hours searching for hidden cameras in your hotel room, you need to try this." (92) | `Before you [waste time/money on old way], try this.` |
| 16 | Reframe | "I didn't know how abnormal my 'normal' was until I started using this." (70) | `I didn't know how [bad] my "normal" was until [change].` |

Plus the comment-style offer hook "I bought mine for the full price and now they're 50% off? That's not fair!" (74) —
use only as a quote of a real comment and only with a real discount.

### 3b. Hook-line templates (Kell)

- `Can't [root-cause problem]? Do this [your solution]!`
- `STOP doing [the thing they're trying] that doesn't [solve the problem].` / `Hey, stop using these for [X]…`
- `[Problem moment]? Do this for 2 seconds tonight.` (rewrite as a situation, not "Still losing hair?" — copy-compliance.md §2)
- `The hidden [cause] behind [problem].` (avoid "poison" in health copy)
- `Did you know [surprising root-cause stat]? Seems crazy, right?`
- `People think [getting benefit] takes a long time. But that's false.`
- `After [time] my [benefit] is the best it's ever been. Thanks to [product].` (real customer only)
- `Your [daily routine] sucks. Sorry I had to say it.` (PROVEN; not about a body attribute)
- `Why do [group A] have [benefit] while [group B] have [problem]?`
- `I laughed when I heard [weird method] helps [problem]… then I looked at the studies.` (studies must exist)
- `This is a [common object]. Think it's good for you? Think again…`
- `[Authority] discover [problem] caused by [surprising mechanism].` (real, named authority only)
- `If you struggle with [situation], nothing works if you have [root cause].`
- `Who do you want to copy? [People with benefit] or [people with problem]?`

### 3c. Seven hook types (Oliver) and C7 hook lines

| Type | Oliver's example / template | Glide Chews line |
|---|---|---|
| Curiosity | something weird, the answer is about to come | "Why do old dogs stop at curbs and not on grass?" |
| Shock | something big: a hoax, a scandal, a crisis | (rarely brand-safe; skip in health) |
| Negative emotional trigger | the market's key fear/pain in their words | "The walk that used to be 40 minutes is now 15." |
| Benefit promise | "Thousands of people are getting rid of back pain with this new smart-foam device" | "[N] owners got the full park loop back." |
| Mini-pitch | who + mechanism + root cause + timeframe + "going viral" in one sentence | see copy-frameworks F14 |
| Story of victory | "How I got rid of 5 years of neck pain in 14 days with this genius new device" | "How Bruno got his trot back at 11." (real customer) |
| Story of suffering | "My foot pain made me feel like an old woman, like I was a prisoner in my own body" | "He looks at the curb, then at me, and waits." |

C7 lines that transfer to text (adapt brackets): "What really causes [X]? Not [A], not [B], not even your age." ·
"Before you [habit] again, read this." · "This is a bag of 'premium' [product type]. Think it's [good]? Think again." ·
"When I first heard about this [method], I laughed. Then I looked at the studies." · "Most people think you need [usual
fix]. The real reason [problem] won't go away is [named cause]." · "Don't blame yourself. Nobody told you about [hidden
cause]." · "If you've tried [fix 1], [fix 2] and [fix 3] and nothing stuck, it's not your fault." Rule from C7: one villain or
contradiction plus one concrete detail in the first sentence.

### 3d. One customer sentiment → four hook forms (Evolve CRO)

Score candidate pain statements with the hook discovery score (toolkit §11), then write all four forms of the winner and
test the two most different.

| Form | Evolve example | Glide Chews version |
|---|---|---|
| Sentiment | "No matter what I do, I can't grow my business." | "He used to drag me to the park. Now I wait for him." |
| Obvious-yes question | "Are you tired of feeling like no matter what you do, you can't grow your business?" | "Tired of waiting for your old dog at every curb?" |
| Desired future state | "How would you like to know the exact next steps…?" | "What if he beat you to the door again tomorrow morning?" |
| First-person direct | "What do I have to do to finally grow my business…?" | "What do I give an 11-year-old dog who still wants the long walk?" |
| If/then | "If you're wondering what on Earth you have to do…, then you've stumbled upon the right ad." | "If walks got shorter this year, this is the 2-minute read for you." |

### 3e. Openers by awareness stage (toolkit §6, Evolve's positioning list) and sophistication (Oliver)

| Stage | What to say (Evolve, from Schwartz) | Glide Chews opener |
|---|---|---|
| Unaware | give words to a hidden dream, exploit a hidden fear, common resentment, ultimate win, take away the work, lead from a universally accepted image into the problem | "Most dogs slow down so gradually you only notice at the curb." |
| Problem aware | problem → solution | "If he stops at curbs now, the stiffness has a cause." |
| Solution aware | benefit → solution; other solutions that cost more, are slower, or failed | "Tried two joint chews already? Here's what both skip." |
| Product aware | reinforce desire, sharpen and extend the image of when/where it works, show ease, testimonials and experts, announce a new mechanism, remove a former limitation | "Same Glide Chews, now in a soft chew for dogs with few teeth." |
| Most aware | the promotion | "The 90-day bundle is [$89.99] until Monday." |

Sophistication ladder (Oliver's knee example) — what the claim must carry: 1 "Stop your knee pain" → 2 "Stop bone-on-bone
knee pain in 4 weeks" → 3 add a solution mechanism ("…with red light therapy, which boosts collagen") → 4 add a problem
mechanism ("…caused by 'Cartilage Drought'") → 5 story, identity, indirect lead. Read the stage from competitors' ads:
claims and product-first stories = 1-2; mechanisms = 3-5. (Health claims in these examples are Oliver's; on Meta, soften
per copy-compliance.md §3.)

### 3f. Openers by zone (toolkit §8) and self-concept (toolkit §7)

| Zone | Pellegrini's line | Glide Chews line |
|---|---|---|
| COZY (+, low) | "A friend told me about these tablets. Thicker hair after 3 months. I'm sticking with it." | "Our neighbour's lab is 13 and still does the hill. We asked what she feeds him." |
| JOY (+, high) | "REGROW YOUR HAIRLINE. 10,000 MEN SAW THE DIFFERENCE IN 60 DAYS." (drop the caps in ads) | "[N] senior dogs back on the long walk this year." |
| FATIGUE (−, low) | "Sick of checking your temples in every shop window?" → compliant: "I was sick of checking my temples in every shop window." | "Tired of the 15-minute walk that used to be 40?" |
| PANIC (−, high) | "You lose 100 hairs a day. After 40 they don't grow back on their own." | rarely right; never for product-aware readers |

| Self | Line that fits | Glide Chews line |
|---|---|---|
| Actual | "That's me." | "You already do everything right for your old dog." |
| Ideal | "What if it were finally possible?" | "Picture him beating you to the door again." |
| Ought | "I really should…" | "He gave you 11 good years. He's owed easy walks." |

## 4. Headlines

### 4a. Rules and categories

- Sits under the creative beside the button. Placements cut it at roughly 27-40 characters; Reels/Stories may hide it.
  Put the meaning in the first ~27 characters; keep it ≤ 40.
- Each headline on an ad is a different category: **desire/outcome · named mechanism · proof/count · offer/risk reversal
  · avatar call-out · question/curiosity · "what they get"**. "Shop now" vs "Shop today" is one headline (Blueprint).
- Lead with what they get (Oliver): not "This viral product helped 12,700 Americans with back pain" but "Get relief … with
  the product 12,700 Americans swear by".
- Name the product or brand in at least one headline or the first lines (transparency; copy-compliance.md §7).
- No ALL CAPS, no "!!!", no "secret / miracle / breakthrough" in health (copy-compliance.md §4). Prices end in .99.
- A winning headline is the next creative's hook or on-image line (Blueprint Q&A: copy as a leading indicator).

### 4c. Advertorial headline types → Meta headline + landing-page headline (C6)

Use the long form on the advertorial or static and the compressed form in the Meta headline field; keep both on the same
angle so the click lands where the ad promised (message match). Authority must be real and named on the page.

| Type (C6) | Long form (page / static) | Meta headline (≤ 40) |
|---|---|---|
| Insider reveals (confession) | "[Role] Reveals the [Truth]: How I [Fixed] My [Problem]" | "[Role]'s fix for [problem]" |
| Professional recommended + regret | "My [Professional] Recommended This… I Only Wish I Knew Sooner" | "Wish I'd started these sooner" |
| Before → until → after | "I Was [Suffering] Until [Someone] Told Me About This [Category]. I Haven't [X] Since." | "Haven't [X] once since" |
| Everyone's talking (bandwagon) | "These [Products] Are Being Hyped by [Group]. Here's Why" | "Why [group] are switching" |
| Finally, cause-niched | "Finally! A Simple Way to Combat [Problem] Caused by [Cause]" | "Finally, help for [situation]" |
| How-to + story | "How to [Result]: A [Avatar]'s Win Over [Problem]" | "How [avatar] [got result]" |
| Stop the old way | "Read This Before You [Habit] Ever Again" | "Read before your next [habit]" |
| Non-negotiable (personal stake) | "Why This [Product] Is Now Non-Negotiable In My [Place] (As a [Credential])" | "Why it's in every room now" |
| Listicle + expert | "[5] Reasons Why [You Need X]… Advice From a [25]-Year [Expert]" | "5 reasons [avatar] swear by it" |
| Benefit command | "Say Goodbye to [Sensory Pain Words] in Your [Place]" | "Say goodbye to [situation]" |
| Hidden cause / industry ignores | "[Real expert] Reveals [Problem] Cause the [Industry] Ignores" | "The cause most [products] miss" |

Mechanics from the pages: first person is the most common voice; the category noun plus "This" beats the brand name in
the long headline; every headline is followed by a second line (social count, question, negation stack, or "after
reading you'll…") — on Meta that second line is the primary text's first line.

## 5. Description lines

Default: blank (Blueprint: it "doesn't even show up all the time"). When used: one bonus line ≤ ~30 characters that adds a
reason, never essential information.
- "+ free shipping over $[50]"
- "60-day empty-tub guarantee"
- "Code auto-applied at checkout" (only if true)
- "Ships in [2-4] days"
- "[4.8]★ from [4,212] owners"
- "+ book a call today" (lead gen, Blueprint)

## 6. In-text CTA lines and the CTA button

Name the button the ad actually uses (Oliver: "tell them EXACTLY which button to click and what to do after that"), give a
real reason to act now, and make the line specific to this ad.

| Button | In-text line pattern |
|---|---|
| Shop Now | "Tap Shop Now and pick your [size/colour/bundle]." · "Tap Shop Now — the 3-tub bundle ships free." |
| Learn More | "Tap Learn More to see how the [mechanism] works (2-minute read)." · "Tap Learn More to check if it's in stock." (only if stock really varies) |
| Get Offer | "Tap Get Offer — the discount is already applied." |
| Order Now | "Tap Order Now to get it before [real date]." |
| Apply Now / Get Started | "Tap Apply Now to see if you qualify — free consultation." |

Kell's CTA types: identity ("for every owner who wants one more good year of walks"), gift ("or send one to the friend
whose dog is slowing down"), challenge ("take the 30-day curb test"), no-pressure ("not ready? Save this for after
tonight's walk"). The advertorial pages' "Check availability" works on the page, not as a fake stock claim in the ad.

## 7. Display link and site-link labels

- **Display link** (Blueprint: "shorten/clean it so it is relevant to the ad"): `brand.com/[angle-word]` — e.g.
  tamsinandhound.com/curb. Same domain as the destination; no tracking strings.
- **Site links**: at least four (Blueprint: best sellers, new arrivals, two key collections; or hero product, FAQs, VSL,
  homepage + product page). Labels short (about 25 characters), written for the side, each a different reason to click:

| Slot | Generic label | Side-specific label |
|---|---|---|
| Hero product / bundle | Shop Glide Chews | Senior-dog 90-day bundle |
| Mechanism / VSL | How it works | How the cushion works |
| Proof | Reviews | 4,212 owner reviews |
| Risk | FAQs | Guarantee & FAQs |
| Collection | Best sellers | For big breeds 60 lb+ |

- **Promotions extension**: a real code only (Blueprint: "we have seen this be helpful").

## 8. Emotional language bank (curated from C6 and C7)

Use these as patterns for a real person's voice — swap in the side's own words from research. In health, weight and
aging categories keep to relief, pride and restored-life phrases; drop fear and body-shaming ones.

- **Sensory pain**: "a rock lodged in the arch of my foot" · "like a pebble stuck in my shoe" · "stiff as a board" ·
  "look like little balloons by the end of the day" · "every step on hard ground".
- **Futility**: "They all just mask it." · "That was another $[N] down the drain." · "I started to believe I was stuck with it."
  · "Shot in the dark."
- **Social pain**: "I laughed too, but deep inside I was embarrassed." · "I'd put on a happy face and pretend." · "I
  became the weakest link." · "I stopped looking people in the eye."
- **Identity loss**: "I felt like an 80-year-old, even though I was in my 40s." · "existing, not living" · "life in slow
  motion".
- **Empathy / blame removal**: "If you're like me, I really FEEL you. It's not your fault." · "Nobody chooses this. But
  some people choose not to do anything about it."
- **Skepticism voiced, then answered**: "I was skeptical at first." · "It sounded too good to be true, so I read the
  comments." · "Will it actually work, or is this another internet gimmick?"
- **Turning point**: "I decided I'd had enough." · "That's when someone like me changed everything."
- **First-use relief**: "Instant relief." · "Like letting the air out of a balloon." · "Like a weight lifted off my feet." ·
  "I didn't realise how much low-grade pain I'd been living with."
- **Pride / social reversal**: "Now my colleagues ask me where I got it." · "These weren't tears of shame. They were tears
  of victory." · "I'm walking proudly again."
- **Restored life**: "I look forward to playing baseball with my son this weekend." · "the best nurse and mother I can
  be" · "the kind of morning I'd forgotten".
- **Future moments (C7)**: a named moment ("the morning he beats you to the door") · compliments from strangers ·
  "clicking my seat belt without asking for an extender" · a day-1 / week-3 / month-2 ladder (real data only).
- **Risk reversal**: "If it doesn't [work], it's free." · "We won't ask why, and you don't have to send it back." · "You have
  nothing to lose and [desire] to gain."
