# Emotion and retention devices — what makes a script deep, and what keeps people watching

The craft layer for step 5-6 of the workflow. Sources: Pellegrini (audit, fixes, self-concept, Mood × Intensity), Kell
(VSL Masterclass and VSL Bible), Oliver (DR Ecom), "VSL swipes" (an analysis of 14 proven long-form VSL scripts, tagged S1-S14 below), Blueprint,
Denney. Everything here is a device to carry real research; none of it replaces real proof.

## 1. The 5-question audit and the 3 fixes

Run every draft through Pellegrini's five questions:
1. **Who are we talking to?** A named side with a belief and a story — not "you".
2. **Mood × Intensity?** Which zone (COZY / JOY / FATIGUE / PANIC), on purpose.
3. **Which awareness stage?** Does the script know if she has tried 4 products or is discovering the problem?
4. **Which self-concept?** Actual, ideal or ought — the version she wants to become again.
5. **How do we prove it?** Named mechanism, anchored numbers, names, timeframes.

The losing script he audits ("a script any brand could sign"):
> HOOK "I finally relieved my back pain." LEAD "Thanks to this new turmeric supplement, it helped relieve my joint pain. I
> take one a day with my coffee. The results are incredible." MECHANISM "Thanks to its anti-inflammatory properties. The
> formula is 100% natural, approved by experts." CTA "Try our product today."

Audit result: nobody; COZY with "a pharma-brochure tone, zero complicity"; awareness blurred; actual self by default;
"turmeric, anti-inflammatory: the claims of 500 brands", no named mechanism.

The three fixes:
- **Fix 1 — desire, not feature.** "Relieves joint pain" → bending down to tie a grandson's laces without wincing; golf all
  weekend without ending on the couch Monday; sleeping through without the 3 a.m. knee; gardening two hours straight.
  "They buy the moments they miss because of it. Sell the moment."
- **Fix 2 — name the cause, name the solution.** UMP "Joint Drought: the 3 layers of cartilage dehydrate"; UMS "Triple-Zone
  Rehydration: rehydrates the 3 layers in 14 days". Link rule: A causes B, so C solves B, obvious in 2 seconds.
- **Fix 3 — floating claims → anchors.** Numbers (7 days, 2,847 people, 83%), names (a named doctor, a named university
  study), timeframes (visible at day 7, peak at day 30). "The bigger the promise, the more specific the proof. Every number
  must be true and provable."

The rewritten script (translated; Pellegrini marks it a fictional exercise with example brands and numbers):
> HOOK "I burst out laughing when my daughter told me 'hydrating my back' would make my chronic pain go away."
> LIVED EXPERIENCE "Yet I'd been fighting terrible back pain for months and nothing helped. Painkillers, physio, creams: I'd
> tried everything. And at the end of every day, that horrible bar across my back kept me from standing up."
> THE CAUSE "My daughter, who studies medicine, told me my cartilage was short of water. It has a name: Joint Drought."
> THE SOLUTION "She recommended Hydraflex. Its pure aloe vera extract applies Triple-Zone Rehydration: it rehydrates the 3
> layers of cartilage deeply."
> THE RESULT "[Dated personal timeline: first noticed change → day 7 → day 14.]"

For Meta: drop his "or I'd end up in a wheelchair" fear line and the "no more pain at 14 days" result; keep the structure
(surprising quote from a loved one → lived moment → named cause from a credible person → named solution → dated personal
timeline with "results vary" and real data), and use "supports/helps" language.

The 522-day lesson: two ads, same brand, product and format; the winner changed only the tension — "a clear, visible
result from the first seconds", a hook that "opens a loop: we want to see what comes next"; in the loser "the reveal
disappoints, the promise falls flat". Promise only what the payoff shows.

## 2. Pain in one sensory moment

Formula (VSL swipes): **a specific place + a physical action + an object in hand + a body sensation or visual + what other people
say or think + the inner thought, in quotes.**
- "On the day before her late husband's birthday, she stopped at a store to buy balloons for his grave. As she bent down to
  get back into her car, her knee gave out… 'I'm still holding onto the balloons. I'm thinking, how did I get here?'" [S6]
- "I got tired of asking for a seat extender, thinking like everybody is just staring at me." [S6]
- "I wake up and it's like I never went to bed." [S6]
- "Linda's smile instantly disappeared. 'You thought she was my daughter, didn't you?'" [S5]
- "I overheard my mom crying her eyes out on the phone… how ashamed she was of letting me down." [S10]

Dimensionalise in three layers (toolkit §9; Oliver): physical (the symptom) → life (what it stops: the lake loop, lifting
the grandchild, paying for treatments that don't hold, being told what you can't do) → emotional (trapped, old before your
time, a burden, invisible, guilty). Kell's research question pushes one level deeper: "What pain is this causing them
emotionally? How do they feel about it? **What's even worse than that?**"
Rules: use their vocabulary from reviews; "clearly paint the problem in clear graphic detail" (Blueprint) — graphic means
specific, not gross; on Meta the pain is told in first person or as the situation, never asserted about the viewer.
Shoot it: every element of the formula is a shot (the place, the action, the object, the face, the onlooker).

## 3. Social and status pain

What others see often hurts more than the symptom: "what unflattering photos are going to appear online" [S2]; "her
sister's youthful appearance only added insult to injury" [S5]; "burying crepey forearms under your long sleeves" [S2];
"how embarrassing foggy thinking and forgetfulness can be" [S13]. Pellegrini's emotional element in the hook includes "the
gaze of others". Use it as the inner thought in the sensory moment, not as shame aimed at the viewer.

## 4. Absolution, relief and the enemy

- **It's not your fault**: "Don't blame yourself… all you need is a friendly reminder that you matter" [S2]; "no one can
  blame her for that. And if you feel the same way, no one can blame you either" [S10]; "Remember, no amount of diet,
  exercise or anything you've tried up until now has even had a chance at working, and now you know why" [S9].
- **Adding, not removing**: "it's not about taking anything away. It's about adding something" [S6, S14].
- **Relief as a felt moment**: "it was like a switch got flipped on inside me" [S14]; "I'm just not the last anymore" [S6].
- **The enemy** (toolkit §1 Q7): name a real, checkable culprit — the method that fails, the shrug ("he's just getting
  old"; "doctors told me the way I felt was normal" [S8]), the category's shortcut ("most chews only add glucosamine").
  Never invent conspiracies, lawsuits or suppressed studies.
- **Permission**: "You deserve it" [S2, S8]; "do something positive for yourself. Not for anyone else. For you" [S7].
- **The expert's moral motive**: "I couldn't just keep this life-changing information to myself" [S6]; "I made this in the
  hopes that your dog never has to visit the veterinarian with a health emergency" [S4].

## 5. The mechanism in one breath

Templates (Oliver, toolkit §4): "Your problem is NOT caused by [common reasons]; it's caused by [UMP], and [UMP] can be
solved with [UMS]." · "[UMP] is like a poison that leads to the problem; [UMS] is the antidote." Kell: "Simple unique
problem mechanism + simple unique solution = problem solved. 2 sentences." "The simpler it is, the higher you can scale."

How proven scripts build it (VSL swipes):
1. **Coin a name** — "melanin metabolism", "fibroblast failure", "four pillars", "digestive jumpstarter", "dry hinge".
2. **Map it onto an everyday system** — a chef in a kitchen, interior designers, a furnace, a boat with a hole, a hinge
   without oil, a highway with a roadblock (Oliver/RemedyCup), a bowling ball on the neck (Kenko).
3. **Concede, then subordinate** — "Sure, aging and sun can make it worse, but if you fix this at the source…" [S1].
4. **Paradox → resolution** — sisters with the same lifestyle and different faces [S5].
5. **Why it's hidden** — a real reason only ("research was funded elsewhere", "most products only add X").
6. **Threes** — three ingredients, pillars or destroyers (11 of 14 scripts number their solution).
7. **The do-it-yourself flip** — "you'd need a liter of olive oil a week" [S3], "bushels of artichokes" [S13], "10
   supplements a day" [S14] → so we made it easy.
8. **Ingredients as characters** — "the queen bee of stopping new dark spots" [S1]; "gut heroes" [S14].
9. **Plain words** — Kell coaching: "instead of inflammation maybe stress, strain, damage".
10. **Promises stay; mechanisms change** — "Promises stay the same > mechanisms change and set you apart" (Kell). A tired
    mechanism can be refreshed with a new messenger: "New expert/authority figure + same mechanism!"
In ads under 60 s the mechanism is one sentence for the cause + one for the fix + one analogy, carried by one visual
(the prop, a 2D animation, the side-by-side). Truth rule: the cause is real or a fair simplification, and the product
actually does it (toolkit §4).

## 6. Proof right after the claim

"The best time to add proof elements is anytime you introduce new information, like the dream outcome, the mechanisms,
and the benefits" (Oliver). Anchor with numbers, names, timeframes (Pellegrini Fix 3). Menu:
- A real customer with a number or scene: "I'm 56 and don't have that old lady skin anymore" [S2]; "I lost 26 pounds and had
  to buy all new pants" [S6] (weight numbers are not usable on Meta — keep the scene, drop the pounds).
- Handover lines: "You don't have to take my word for it. Here are some reviews taken directly from our website." [S2]
- A skeptic inside the story: "I know it looks like a gimmick, and that's what I was thinking too, but it really works" (Kenko).
- Candour: "I've got to be honest, even I struggle to get enough every week" [S3].
- Demonstration on screen; the expert testing on herself; a user group of strangers with percentages [S14] — real only.
- Scale: customers, reviews, rating — on screen in big type with a red circle (Oliver).
- Process and certification shown (logos on the box, third-party testing) [S3, S5].
- Third-party facts that are public and checkable (a regulator's test, a settlement) — never fabricate.
In short ads one proof per claim is enough: a number for the mechanism, a face for the result.

## 7. Dream outcome as scenes

Convert each feature with **"What that REALLY means is…"** (Kell/Mindvalley): "The Hypnotic Trance… What that REALLY means
is that you start to ENJOY life more often… The first sip of a cup of tea in the morning. The chirp of the birds out of
your window." Small, sensory, everyday — and each scene is a shot.
Devices (VSL swipes):
- **Named moment**: "something I like to call the youthful moment… you catch your reflection in your rear-view mirror, you
  suddenly see a you that you hardly recognize" [S5].
- **"Imagine…" stack**: "Imagine waking up and seeing a fresh, healthy face looking back at you. Imagine hearing your
  friends ask if you just got back from a vacation" [S7].
- **"I want you to feel…"** refrain [S14].
- **Ideal day**: "wake up extra early, take my dogs out, go on a run… and do it all over again" [S8].
- **Small moment of triumph**: "Not having to ask for a belt extender, but instead clicking my seatbelt" [S6].
- **Other people's reactions**: "Someone at the office even asked if I'd had any work done" [S7]; "They'll probably have to
  do a double-take" (Kell, Mindvalley upsell).
- **Concrete clothes and places**: "finally wearing sleeveless dresses again after years of covering up" [S7].
- **Timeline ladder**: "after just a few weeks… after two months… by the third month" [S5] (real, typical timelines only).
- **First-use moment**: "Imagine the feeling of taking it for the first time" [S14].
- **Ripple-out**: body → mornings → relationships → decisions → freedom (Resurge "Reality #2").
- **Heaven without hell**: the outcome *without* the effort they dread (no diets, no gym, no salon chair).
Pair every physical win with an emotional win (Kell: "Physical Wins / Emotional Wins" in every pitch).
Payoff on screen (Blueprint): show the result happening — the strangers reacting, the dog jumping into the car.

## 8. Future pacing that shrinks the distance

"The brain treats the future self as a stranger" (Pellegrini). Show how it changes things now:
- Shrink the distance: not "in 3 months" but "this afternoon", "tonight", "this Saturday".
- Block the "tomorrow" escape: a 2-minute quiz, a one-tap add to cart, a guarantee that removes the risk of trying today.
- Show the product solving today's problem. "Sell to the person who is tired, busy and hurting now."
- Self-monitoring questions turn the trial into a game: "Are you feeling more energized? Is your brain fog blowing away?" [S13].

## 9. Identity and self-concept lines

| Self | Line pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Actual | Validate, no pressure | "Your wife says your hair looks fine. She's right. But imagine if it were even better." (Pellegrini) |
| Ideal | Projected version + shortcut | "Get back the hairline you had at 25, without a transplant." |
| Ought | Standards and duty | "You take care of your business, your family, your fitness. Time to take care of your hair too." |

Unsure which self? Write the same message three times, one per self (Pellegrini). Other identity moves (VSL swipes): praise the
viewer for watching ("you're a person who refuses to be stuck. I can tell. You're here, aren't you?" [S6]); democratic not
elite ("you don't need to be rich and famous" [S2]); the true self ("she's in there just waiting to shine through" [S7]);
the flaw as a gift ("your body isn't broken — it's a metabolic marvel nobody taught you to switch on" [S10]); taking a stand
("make today the day…" [S10]). Identity decides the visuals (Evolve): show who they want to be seen as.

## 10. Open loops and retention devices

Structure:
- **Loop inside loop** (Blueprint): open A; before A closes, open B; close A; B continues; open C… The hook's loop pays off at
  ~60-70% of runtime (VSL swipes).
- Don't let tension simmer too long or "there will be drop-off" (Oliver). Give "a reason in every sentence to keep watching"
  after the first 10-25%.
- Hold them at least 10 s; once they've watched 30 s, odds of finishing "go up exponentially" (Oliver, Gundry rule).
- Start the story at the climax (Oliver's lead options); myth-bust or agitate in the lead when hold is weak.

Verbal devices (VSL swipes, verbatim patterns):
- Promised reveal: "In just a moment, I'll reveal the [toxic protein] that causes this problem."
- "But first…": "In just a minute I'll share [X]. But first I need to show you [Y]."
- Asking for attention: "Stick with me for a few minutes and I promise…"; "listen to this next bit closely".
- Guess / quiz: "Any guesses where I found it? It wasn't Europe." "73% got this wrong. Here we go."
- Rhetorical check-ins: "Does that sound familiar?" "Pretty incredible, right? Well, it doesn't stop there."
- Numbered structure on screen: "Number one…", "Pillar two…".
- Suspense lines: "But that wasn't the worst of it." "There was only one catch." "And you won't believe what happened."
- Gating the payoff: "But not all [olive oils] are created equal." "Before you run out and buy [X], there's something you
  need to know."
- Callbacks: "If you're thinking that [villain] is the one I told you about at the beginning, you're right." [S7]
- Pattern interrupts: a taboo ("we need to talk about poop" [S8]), a one-liner ("show me one tooth in a dog's mouth that is
  flat for chewing grains" [S4]), a voiced objection ("licorice root, right? Sounds like a dessert" [S14]), a prop, a shift
  of register ("Take a minute to let that sink in." "Yes, I said over 50%.").
Visual devices: a new shot every 1-3 s, movement in every shot, one footage per statement (Pellegrini); on-screen numbers
and progress markers; the open loop written on screen ("Why he stopped at the curb ↓").

## 11. Crossroads and closes (brand-safe)

- **Two paths, short** (default for ads): "You can carry on as you've always been, or you can try [product] risk-free for
  [15] days. You have nothing to lose and [the dream] to gain." [S11] · "You can keep [the small daily compromise], or…" [S13]
- **Three paths** (90 s+): do nothing / figure it out yourself / the product — "If you do what you've always done, you'll get
  what you've always got." [S10]
- **Recap the pain, turn the page**: "Think of all the time you spent focusing on these negatives… Now is your chance to make
  new memories." [S2]
- **Doing nothing is a decision**: "we both know they're not going to disappear if you do nothing." [S1]
- **Honour the viewer**: "I just want to honor you… You're here, aren't you?… Seize this moment." [S6]
- **Kell's crossroads** (Resurge): Reality #1 the nightmare vs Reality #2 "heaven without hell". On Meta keep Reality #2
  (freedom, mornings, relationships) and turn Reality #1 into one calm, first-person line about the status quo — the
  nightmare body language ("a shell of what it once was") is the scare tactic Kell's own compliance deck bans.
- CTA rule (Oliver): "Be clear and direct. Tell them exactly what you want them to do." Name the button; call back to the
  hook image; one guarantee line.
- Sign-offs that assume success: "I look forward to hearing your success story." [S1] "When you see the results, let me know." [S11]

## 12. Objections to pre-empt, and where

Kell's 10 + 1 universal objections ("we devastate their entire existence" — Fladlien) mapped to beats:

| Objection | Answer it in |
|---|---|
| "How do I know this is really for me?" / "Will it work for ME?" | Hook call-out, casting the side, a testimonial from someone like them |
| "What unique benefits do you bring?" / "How is this different from everything I've tried?" | UMP + UMS, the analogy, the side-by-side |
| "Why should I believe your claims?" | Proof right after the claim |
| "What makes you think I'll get results this time?" | Absolution + the mechanism ("now you know why nothing worked") |
| "Why haven't I heard of this?" / "Why didn't my [expert] tell me?" | The real reason it's overlooked; "it's not your fault" |
| "Is this really that good a deal?" | Offer line, per-day reframe, DIY-cost comparison (landing page for the full stack) |
| "What if I'm not ready today?" | Future pacing to tonight/this week; guarantee; one-tap step |
| "I don't have the money" | Cost of the alternatives (Oliver: per-session price stack), per-day framing |
Bottom-funnel statics and 1-of-1 content can each kill one objection (Blueprint).

## 13. Transitions bank

"You see…" · "That means…" · "So why does this matter?" (Oliver) · "And then what happened was…" · "The best part was…" ·
"What happened next was…" · "Here's what it is. Here's what it does." · "And the best part is…" · "Why?" · "But the real
issue isn't [X]. There's something bigger going on." · "Turns out…" · "Okay, here's exactly what I did." · "So let's get
into it." (Kell, VSL swipes). Every change of direction gets a quick transition (Oliver).

## 14. Fear dosage and brand safety

- Kell for brands: "Nightmare stories evoke fear and negativity… Hook people in with positivity, fascination, and dream
  comparison scenarios, that way people associate positive emotions with your brand." Snow pivoted away from ads about
  people "embarrassed with their yellow teeth": "the problem is not yellow teeth. It's that the user doesn't have white
  teeth. Always focusing on the end outcome."
- Kell on the blackhat VSL (Meticore): "the definition of 'stabbing with a knife'. This kills the brand."
- Pellegrini: "Intensity buys attention, not the sale." PANIC fits the problem-aware person who needs a shock (David, sudden
  loss); it is a red flag on product-aware audiences. FATIGUE ("sick of checking your temples in every shop window") fits
  problem-aware people who want to be understood without drama.
- Kell's editing playbook: Facebook "doesn't want to make their users feel bad" — no zooming in on problem areas, no
  before/after pictures for health, weight, aging.
- Kell's "if you don't make them cry, they ain't going to buy" (Charity Water) is about moving stories — real ones —
  not about fear.
