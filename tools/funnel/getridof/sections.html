The real reason they come back

The Scent Trail Cycle

Mice barely use their eyes to get around your home. They follow their noses.

Every mouse that gets in leaves a trail of pheromones and urine behind it, an invisible highway from the gap under your door to the food in your pantry. Every new mouse picks up that trail and follows it straight in.

When a trap catches one mouse, the highway stays open. The next one arrives, follows the same trail, and the cycle starts over. That's why "it worked for a week" is the most common story with traps.

To stop mice for good, break the trail at the entry points. Catching whichever mouse walks down it won't do it.
