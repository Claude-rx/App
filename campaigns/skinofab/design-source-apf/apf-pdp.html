The problem

Foundation wasn’t made for skin after 45

You’re not doing it wrong. Most foundations are designed around younger, oilier skin. Then your skin changed, and they stopped working.

  • Dozens of shades to guess online, for a skin tone that has shifted since you last bought one
  • Matte, long-wear formulas that cling to dry patches and crack by lunch
  • Pigment and powder that settle into fine lines and make them look deeper
  • A color that oxidizes orange or turns ashy as the day goes on
  • Primer, foundation, concealer, setting powder: four steps just to look like yourself

The result: a drawer full of bottles that didn’t work, and the feeling that makeup isn’t for you anymore.

The root of it

Your skin changed. Your foundation didn’t.

1

Less collagen, less moisture

In the years around menopause, skin makes less collagen and holds less water. It gets thinner, drier and more textured, so a formula built to control oil grips every dry patch and line.

2

A tone that keeps moving

Sun spots, redness and an undertone that shifts with age and seasons mean the shade that matched at 35 rarely matches now. A fixed shade can't follow you.

3

Formulas made for 25-year-old skin

Many foundations chase oil control and long wear with mattifying powders, drying alcohol and fragrance: the opposite of what drier, more sensitive skin needs.

So the problem was never your skin or your technique. It’s a formula built for someone else.
