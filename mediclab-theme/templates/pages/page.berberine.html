The root cause

The Slow Door: insulin knocks after every meal, and with age your cells answer more slowly.

After you eat, glucose from your meal enters your blood. Insulin then knocks on the doors of your muscle and liver cells to let it in, where it is burned or stored. When the cells answer quickly, the level comes back down smoothly.

Over the years, with less muscle, long hours of sitting, short sleep and stress, the cells answer more slowly. The body makes up for it by knocking louder, releasing more insulin, and for a long time the fasting number still looks fine. Then it starts to drift, a little higher at each checkup. Muscle is the biggest destination for glucose after a meal, so the hours you sit through after lunch and dinner are when the Slow Door shows most.

  1. The knock. After a meal, glucose enters your blood and insulin signals your muscle and liver cells to take it in.
  2. The slow answer. With age, less muscle, more sitting and short sleep, cells respond more slowly. The body knocks louder, and for years the numbers still look normal.

What the research says

What the research says about berberine, and what it does not

These are published studies on berberine alone and on walking after meals, not on this formula. No trial of this product has been run. Every dose is on the card.

Research

11

meta-analyses of randomized trials were pooled in an umbrella review. Berberine had significant effects on blood glucose, insulin measures and blood lipids compared with controls.

Li et al., Phytotherapy Research, 2023

The authors say the quality of these meta-analyses needs to improve. Doses are set by each pooled trial: the Liang review below covers 0.3 to 3 g of berberine a day. Our serving holds about 73 mg.

Research

28 trials

with 2,313 adults: berberine lowered fasting and after-meal glucose against control. The effect was not significant in trials longer than 90 days, above 2 g a day, or in people over 60.

Liang et al., Endocrine Journal, 2019

Adults with a diagnosed blood sugar condition, often alongside medicine. Trials gave 0.3 to 3 g of berberine a day, most 0.9 to 1.8 g. Our serving holds about 73 mg.

Research

3 months

of 500 mg of berberine three times a day before meals improved an insulin sensitivity score against placebo.

Perez-Rubio et al., Metabolic Syndrome and Related Disorders, 2013

24 adults with raised waist, triglyceride and blood sugar readings, 12 per group. 1,500 mg a day of berberine HCl, about 20 times the berberine in our serving.

Safety

34.5%

of patients had passing stomach and gut side effects in a 3-month berberine pilot study. No liver or kidney problems were seen.

Yin, Xing and Ye, Metabolism, 2008

500 mg three times a day, 1,500 mg in all, about 20 times the berberine in our serving, in adults with a diagnosed blood sugar condition. It is why the label says with meals, and why we suggest starting at one capsule.

Safety

3 enzymes

the liver uses to clear many medicines (CYP2D6, CYP2C9 and CYP3A4) were slowed after two weeks of berberine. The authors say drug interactions should be considered.

Guo et al., European Journal of Clinical Pharmacology, 2012

Healthy men, 300 mg three times a day for 2 weeks, 900 mg in all, about 12 times our serving. It is why the medicine list sits next to the price.

Research

7 trials

Light walking breaks during long sitting lowered after-meal glucose and insulin more than sitting still, and more than standing breaks.

Buffey et al., Sports Medicine, 2022

Single-day crossover trials in adults, mostly with overweight. No berberine was given, so our 73 mg plays no part in this result. This is the free half of the routine.
