## 1. Concept, iteration, imitation

**Concept** (Blueprint): "a repeatable ad idea that can generate many iterations… a format, pattern, or storytelling
structure that can be reused across offers, mechanisms, education, avatars." It answers "What is the structural idea that
makes this ad work?" A true concept is structurally repeatable (swap the variables and the frame holds) and can be
written as an SOP. Operationally, **concept = avatar + template**. "Concept = the show. Iteration = the cast."

**Iteration** (Blueprint): "a controlled variation of something already proven to work… not a new idea… not a new
structure… a meaningful adjustment inside an existing winning concept." "An iteration changes what is emphasized, not
how the ad fundamentally works." It asks "What happens if we tell the same story, but through a slightly different lens?"
Its job: "Extend the life of winners; Unlock new pockets of the same audience; Reduce creative fatigue; Improve
efficiency without raising risk. They are your highest ROI, lowest variance creative output."

**Hit rates** (Blueprint live calls): iterations hit often but "are very unlikely to unlock growth… they are sustaining
stability"; new concepts hit rarely ("it might take 100 ads to have a hit") but "could unlock significant spend". "They
just serve two different purposes. That's why we do both." "Hit rate is what matters and volume makes up for bad hits."

**Imitation** (Evolve): rebuilding a specific outside ad (competitor, another niche, inspiration) for our product,
starting from "what we're imitating and why". Competitor concepts seen running many variations are probably winning for
them and tend to have decent hit rates for you; your own winners have the best hit rates (Blueprint).

**Labelling test** (answer in order; the first "yes" decides):
1. Would a different person see themselves in it (a new side: dad David vs gym Tom; GLP-1 user vs new mum)? → **N** (new avatar), own pack.
2. Is the form factor different (UGC → us-vs-them static, founder story, carousel, native post, listicle/advertorial,
   post-it note)? → **N** (new template). The same angle moved from static to video is a new concept too; Pellegrini
   tests an angle as a static, then duplicates it in video "so you know it's the angle that wins, not the format".
3. Is the whole ad about a different problem, moment or mechanism? → **N** (new angle). A different angle "is a new
   concept, not an iteration" (Blueprint live calls).
4. Is it rebuilt from someone else's ad? → **M**.
5. Otherwise only emphasis changed (hook, lead, visual, offer framing, education point, slice of the same avatar) → **I**;
   name the layer.
Terminology: Blueprint's slides call iterations "angles". Here "angle" means the door (why this person stops now); a new
door through the whole ad is a concept, while "same angle, different executions" (Gruns: "better poops" → "poop more",
"poop like you mean it", "normal poops") are iterations. A new moment used only as a new hook, with the same body, is an
iteration (message layer).

## 2. The six iteration layers (Blueprint list, with the metric each tends to move)

| Layer | Options (Blueprint) | Usually moves | Example (dog joint chew) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Message | headline swaps; hook rewrites; different emotional trigger (fear vs aspiration vs urgency); different promise frame (speed, ease, certainty, exclusivity) | hook rate (in the hook), CTR (in the promise) | "He stopped racing me up the stairs" → "He's the last one up now" |
| Mechanism | a different feature highlighted; reframe how it works; a different "why it works" story; isolate one component of a multi-part solution | CTR, CVR (belief) | isolate one ingredient's job instead of the whole formula |
| Avatar (slice) | founder vs beginner vs expert; price-sensitive vs convenience vs status; a different awareness stage (a different person = new concept, section 1) | hook rate (call-out), new pockets | same owner, solution-aware ("why the treats stopped working") |
| Education | teach a different belief; debunk a different objection; explain a different mistake; a different mental model | hold rate (lead), CTR | myth-bust lead: "it's not just age" |
| Offer | same core offer, different incentive; bonus stacking; risk-reversal emphasis; time-bound vs value-stacked framing ("keep the core offer the same if possible") | CTR, CVR, AOV | lead with the 60-day guarantee instead of the discount |
| Visual | same layout, different image; same template, different scene; same UGC structure, different actor; same motion pattern, different pacing | hook rate (first frame), hold (pacing) | same script, a different owner and dog |

"You don't need to change all of these. You could just change literally one bullet."

Self-concept and zone are message-layer levers: one message, three versions (actual, ideal, ought). "Not sure which
self? Always test it in 3 variations" (Pellegrini). The same holds for the zone (COZY, JOY, FATIGUE, PANIC) if it stays
coherent with awareness.

## 3. Distinct enough for Andromeda 2

- Meta reads the whole ad. "What Meta is looking at is every basically phrase"; a video is lead-in → hook → body → CTA,
  and swapping any component (a new body with the same lead-in/hook/CTA, or a new lead-in + hook on the old body) means
  "Meta will 100% see these as different" (Blueprint live calls). It is not the thumbnail or the first frame.
- Human test: "If I watched it, did I just watch the same thing twice? You might feel like you watched the first two
  seconds twice, but the rest of the body changes and that's all it needs."
- Near-duplicates (same audio and cut timing, only headline text and background colour changed) are "fairly similar"
  and get served to "extremely similar people". Small tweaks (background black → green) are not counted as an asset.
- Mirroring or reversing a shot: "very hacky… we just create new."
- Trading hooks between two top creatives produced nothing: the originals hold the social proof and Meta "might be
  seeing that hook is used already." Write new hooks; keep hook-swapping in the toolkit only for new ones.
- "Diversity of intent, not diversity of volume… 15 versions of [the same idea] can easily become noise; we want every
  test to be answering roughly a different question" (Selena).
- "Those little micro variations are not going to do too much for us… put 10 units of time into every creative instead
  of mass creative" (Blueprint live calls). AI is for multiplication of good ideas, not deception or filler.
- Reconciling "one element at a time" with "not just changing the headline": change one layer so the result is readable,
  but make it a new execution of that layer, big enough that a viewer notices.

## 4. Picking the layer from the funnel

Read `analyze`'s "Fix first" column (hook → hold → click → purchase vs the account's medians) and fix the first weak
stage only. Oliver's ladder: low hook rate → new hooks; low hold rate → new leads; low CTR → the close or desire is not
strong enough.

**Hook (hook rate):** 3-5 new hooks on the same body (Oliver); see section 6.
**Lead (hold rate):** keep the hook; write a new lead. Lead types (Oliver): value (tips, fun facts) · fear/pain
(agitate their nightmare) · myth-busting ("X is the real cause, right? WRONG!") · personal story (open loop on the
solution) · promise (dream outcome). Don't sell in the lead; earn 10-30 s of attention first (Gundry rule). In aware
markets, use an indirect lead: something loosely related to the promise, easy to consume, no belief to accept yet.
Pacing: a cut every 1-3 s, motion in every shot, one shot per line, open a loop early and pay it at ~60-70% of runtime.
**Back half (CTR, CVR):** "It probably is a good hook. You probably need more value in the back half." Keep the first
frames, inject an offer segment ("by the way, we're doing buy two, get one free right now", no fake timelines), then
the body. If the break is checkout → purchase with good earlier steps, the ad lacks a clear CTA or offer (Blueprint live
calls). Name the mechanism, anchor the proof, add risk reversal.
**Page (CVR, cost per IC):** same headline, angle and offer on the landing page; price reveal where intent is built;
pre-qualify in the ad. A new page is easiest to test as a new pack with a short floor (Blueprint live calls). Funnel
priority when ads seem stuck: product, then offer, then lander.
**Hook by market maturity** (Oliver): new markets → promise a benefit; mature → specific benefits with proof;
saturated → a new mechanism in the hook.

## 5. Iteration menus

**5a. Denney's iteration menu** (after finding the biggest variable)

| # | Move | Layer / label |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Take the biggest variable into a different format (image → video, into the hook of a top video, post-it note, testimonial → social proof) | N (new template) |
| 2 | Add-ons: social proof, social numbers, testimonial "golden nugget", press, before/after (category rules permitting), statistics, bullet points | message / mechanism |
| 3 | Turn winning images into a carousel; put the top performers in one carousel | N (template) |
| 4 | Add a headline bar | message |
| 5 | Swap the image (human vs non-human, different person, home vs street, plain product shot) | visual |
| 6 | Swap the message (a different human desire) | message |
| 7 | Videos: keep the format, try different problems | N (angle) |
| 8 | Videos: keep the format, try different agitators | education / message |
| 9 | Same script, different people | visual |
| 10 | Swap the hook: greenscreen, TikTok response bubble, split screen, oddly satisfying (reverse loop, texture play) | message / visual |
| 11 | Build on the story (e.g. a whole ad built around one review's "golden nugget") | education |
| 12 | Call out your audience | avatar slice |
| 13 | Test adding a pet; turn the best script into a post-it-note ad with voiceover (also works as a hook) | visual / N |

**5b. Nick's ways to milk a high-delivery winner** (Blueprint live calls; a few hours gave ~50 creatives, and one
resulting ad set did 3.43 ROAS on $1.1k in 7 days): add a voiceover (four in his example) · add music · recut it to
start on a new hook · make statics from it · test landing pages (home, product, collection) · price anchoring
(pre-select the expensive variant so the cheaper one feels cheap) · new copy · add it to an interest-winners ad set
(that's scaling, see `andromeda2-scaling`) · splice it with another winner · move a winning voiceover onto another video.

**5c. More moves from the Blueprint calls:** "You have a winner. Make another one… as soon as you can"; when to stop
iterating: "almost never", it doesn't have to beat the original; keep the hook and inject an offer; new bodies, hooks or
CTAs for awkward ads; on a long VSL change only the first 15 s-1 min; recreate winners in another colourway or variant
when stock runs out; have new UGC creators recreate past winners; ask proven creators to shoot a second, different
format (cheap or free); brief every creator with several hooks, call-outs and a shot list to get 20-30 assets instead
of four; three hooks per shoot = three ads; reproduce the best statics with AI; stills from the creators' shot lists;
a headline that wins in copy is a candidate hook for the next video ("copy as a leading indicator").

**5d. Evolve:** every iteration brief names its variable: messaging, hook, hold or image ("this idea is to test 3
different holds on this ad"). Great spend + poor CPA: "something about it got a lot of engagement, so make it more
conversion driven."

**5e. Selena's prompt:** "Produce 10 big swing variations, changing one element at a time" (hook, actor, format or
framing on the same angle). Then pick the ones you would bet on; iterations go out as a new pack.

**5f. Copy iterations:** accounts usually run on 5-15 primary texts and headlines in total. Run 3 completely different
ones at a time (not "buy clothes / shop clothes / best clothes"), read Breakdown → Text after two weeks and turn off
"optimized text per person" if the text breakdown is missing (Blueprint live calls). Nick tests 5 new primary texts + 5
headlines on high performers and combines the best. Iterations of a video can keep the winner's copy. Hand copy to
`andromeda2-ad-copy`.

**5g. 1-of-1 content** (Blueprint live calls): content only you can make, from wins, volume over time, your market
position and real customers ("the most authentic content… is real content. It is not reproducible. Period."). Re-shoot a
winning angle with a high-repeat customer who already wrote a review (call them, send product, give a 30-second guide:
"answer this question, say this"). Keep a wins log. It fits middle-to-bottom funnel best: "undeniable proof, ridiculous
objection handling".

## 6. Hook iteration patterns

A hook is three things that must agree and create tension: the audio (voiceover), the on-screen headline and the
visual (Oliver). "A great hook evokes curiosity while simultaneously calling out our ideal customer." Lead-in 1-3 s
("Okay." / "You wouldn't believe…"), hook 3-5 s, 2-3 sentences max (Blueprint).

**Hook types** (Oliver): curiosity · shock (a scandal, a crisis) · negative emotional trigger (their fears, in their
words) · promising a benefit · mini-pitch (who + what + mechanism + result in one line) · personal story of victory ("How
I got rid of 5 years of neck pain in 14 days…") · personal story of suffering ("My foot pain made me feel like an old
woman…").

**Templates from long-running VSLs:**
1. Real-cause myth-bust: "What really causes [problem]? It's not [suspect 1], [suspect 2] or even [suspect 3]."
2. Habit hijack: "Before you [common habit], watch this."
3. Viewer question: "[Expert], I'm embarrassed by my [vivid metaphor]. What should I do?"
4. Curious case: "The curious case of [person]. [Achievement]. But what people don't know is [struggle]."
5. Customer before/after in her words: "This was me before [method]… Here's me after." (category rules permitting)
6. Envied exception: "Have you ever noticed some [people] never seem to [suffer]?"
7. Prop + think again: "This is a [trusted product]. Think it's [healthy/safe]? Think again."
8. Relatable moment: "[Funny everyday scene showing the desired state]. But without [mechanism], [thing] is not [benefit]."
9. Validation: "So you're looking to [goal]. That's a great goal, and it's super achievable."
10. Founder confession: "My name is [name]. Just listen… We were a happy [family] until we weren't."
11. Third-party discovery: "I saw a clip of [person]… So I thought, how does she do that?"
12. Situation + news: "If [situation] and nothing works, I've got news for you."
Also: "Can't [root problem]? Do this [solution]!" (Kell) · "[Category] used to look like this. Now it looks like this."
(Blueprint: a hook that "every time I say it, it works").

**Format swaps for the same line** (Denney): greenscreen over the evidence; TikTok comment-reply bubble; split screen;
oddly satisfying reverse loop or texture; post-it note; headline bar.
**Visual hooks that are hard to argue with** (Pellegrini): a familiar neutral object, a real demo, 0-2 s of silence then
the voice, a reverse shot right after.
**Hook rules:** one villain or contradiction plus one concrete detail (a number, scene or object) in the first sentence;
never open with credentials; make the payoff easy and fast; speak to one person in their own words; open a question the
mechanism later answers by name. Compliance: describe the situation or tell it in the first person, never "Are you
[attribute]?". 70-80% of one member's best ads came from hooks lifted from their best organic posts (Blueprint live calls).

**One angle, six hooks** (worked; angle = the knee that wakes you at 3 a.m., side = Marc, 65, ex-marathoner; claims and
mechanism names are placeholders):
1. Myth-bust: "My knee didn't start waking me at 3 a.m. because I turned 65."
2. Story of suffering: "Four supplements in two years, and the 3 a.m. knee still won."
3. Envied exception: "My running partner is 68 and still does the Sunday loop. I wanted to know what he knew."
4. Prop + think again (visual: a jar of glucosamine): "Took this for a year. Think it reached my cartilage? Think again."
5. Habit hijack: "Before you buy another knee sleeve, watch this."
6. Used to / now: "Sunday mornings used to mean 10 km. Now they mean ice packs."
Six self-concept/zone variants of the same line also count as message-layer iterations; test 3-5, not all.

## 7. New-concept generators (from a winner)

| Source | How | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Winning avatar × new template | same side, new form factor | UGC winner → named-mechanism static, founder story, carousel, native post |
| Winning avatar's other problems (internal horizontal) | list everything this person struggles with, circle what the product touches | GLP-1 users → hair loss, metabolism, digestion |
| New avatar on the winning template | a different side of the buyer, same structure | the 45+ buyer gets their own round "for the problem and solution specifically for the 45+ year old" |
| Sub-problem / new mechanism / new name for the cause | go one level down | back pain → degenerative discs (Oliver) |
| New moment or room | the same person at another trigger point | 3 a.m. pain vs Saturday-morning ambition |
| New enemy, new proof frame, new awareness stage | psychology toolkit §10 | founder story, expert, customer count, comparison |
| Competitor imitation | concepts a competitor runs in many variations | Ad Library or a board tool; rebuild with your product and call-outs |
| 1-of-1 content | real customers, wins, your process | six-month check-in with a repeat buyer |
| Offer angle | bundle logic, gifting, seasonal | "people have two knees" |

Avatar exercise (Blueprint live calls): by hand, write the avatar and list all their problems, product-related or not;
circle the ones the product touches; each circled problem is a candidate angle. Most big brands run on only a few
avatars; at small spend pick one core avatar first.

## 8. Volume and mix

- **Count:** 1 new ad per week per $10k monthly spend, rounded up, floor 2 ($90k → 9/week; $100k → 10; $200k → 20)
  (Blueprint). At ~$100/day, 4-6 ads every other week instead of 2-4 every week is fine. A $10k/day account came out at
  ~25 iterations + 5 new concepts a week; a $2-3M/month account ships 100-150 creatives a week, mostly iterations.
- **Override:** "If you only have a capability to build three quality ads per week [at $90k/month], only make three
  quality ads." Don't go far above the count either: some ads then get no spend. Release a backlog as weekly packs.
- **Split by tier** (`scripts/a2.py testing-plan`): < $5k ~0% iterations; $5-25k 15%; $25-75k 35%; $75-150k 50%;
  $150-200k 65%; $200k+ 80%. Blueprint calculator anchors: $10k/month ≈ 13% proven / 87% new ("every few weeks, get an
  iteration batch of your top performing ads"); $100k ≈ 50/50; $200k+ caps at 80/20 "because you probably established
  your creative pillars".
- **State adjustments:** below target → "more, then new"; ROAS drifting down as spend rises → the pipeline is behind,
  slow vertical until it catches up; plateau with one dominant ad → more new concepts; reduced spend → iterate only "the
  absolute top, top, top stuff" plus a few home-run swings.
- **Cost-capped campaigns** (Blueprint 4-scenario matrix; details in `andromeda2-hard-scaling`):

| Cap (target CPA) hit? | Budget spent? | Creative action |
|---|---|---|
| Yes | Yes | raise budget; keep iterations flowing |
| No | Yes | iterate: the creative has signal |
| No | No | make new creative (and question whether caps fit at all) |
| Yes (cap = true target) | No | "iterate on creative more than anything… you're so close" |

- **Per concept:** 1-6 iterations per round; beyond six "gets a little crazy" unless spend is large. Build up over
  rounds: "these two ads worked… now I'm going to make five more… those five work… let's pour more gas in the fire."
- **Alternative view (Denney, tests per month by monthly budget):** < $5k 2-3; $5-30k 3-4; $30-250k 4-6; $250-500k 6-8;
  $500k+ 10+; 2-3 variations per test at small budgets, 4-6 at large.

## 9. Fatigue: signals and actions

| Signal (7-14 days vs the ad's own prior 14-30 days) | Reading |
|---|---|
| Prospecting 7-day frequency past ~3-3.5 (5 = expand with new concepts) | the pocket is saturating |
| Hook rate and CTR falling while CPM is flat | people have seen it |
| CPA rising / ROAS falling at stable spend | efficiency decaying |
| Meta shifting its spend share to other ads | the system has found better options |
| CPM +30% account-wide with no season | competition, not this ad |
| Everyone in the market running the same visual or message | market-level exhaustion (Evolve #14-15) |

Not fatigue: the dip after a budget raise (frequency, CPM and CPC rise, ROAS dips; hold the line if cost per ATC stays
flat), daily rotation between ads, one bad day, post-sale days. Big markets don't "exhaust" ("People are always going to
have babies"); new creative reviving performance is the signal to keep the weekly flow.
Actions: keep a winner at KPI on and unedited; ship its iterations; remove floors from its pack; below KPI follow the
pausing tree; paused → `andromeda2-zombie-campaigns`; if its comments are the asset, reuse the post ID.

## 10. Brief templates and pre-brief questions

**Mini-brief** (the skill's template, one per ad):
```
### <ad name>
Type: I of <parent> | N (avatar/template/angle) | M of <reference>
Side: A0X <name>, moment <…> · Awareness <…> · Self-concept <…> · Zone <…>
Layer changed: <one of six>: <what exactly>
Stays (locked DNA): <avatar, template, biggest variable, body/CTA…>
Hypothesis: because <evidence>, <change> will move <metric> from <a> to ≥ <b>
Hooks: 1) visual 0-3 s / spoken / on-screen text  2) …  3) …
Body and visual notes: <structure, footage patterns, cut every 1-3 s, one shot per line>
Proof: <real, anchored> · Offer + CTA: <…>
Copy seeds: <primary-text angle, 2-3 headline directions>
Sizes: 9:16 + 1:1, burned-in captions · Compliance: <…> · Hand-off: <skill>
```

**Evolve brief variants:** *Ideation* (type of ad; sizes; variations being tested: messaging / hook / hold / image; the
brief with concept, structure, scene-by-scene content, on-video messaging, editor instructions, editor notes; example ads;
asset folder; 2 primary texts + 2 headlines). *Imitation* puts "what is the ad + explanation of what we're imitating"
first. *Iteration* starts with "What is the ad we are iterating? Iterating DCT #, link" and its concept line reads like
"This idea is to test 3 different holds on this ad". Scene lines give the visual with source timestamps, the spoken or
text line, and editor instructions ("add a reverse effect to make the clip last longer than 2 seconds").

**UGC creator package** (Blueprint): several hooks, several call-outs, the few things they must say, a shot list for
video and stills; don't script them word for word; 20-30 assets per package.

**Pre-brief questions** (Evolve, ask before submitting a video brief):
1. Do the first 1-2 seconds hook the RIGHT user in and keep them hooked?
2. Is this an ad your potential customers would want to see?
3. Does the copy create a clear image in the user's mind?
4. Does the video feel native to the platform?
5. Does it contain a label for the type of person it is for (avatar call-out)?
6. Would it make someone smile or laugh?
7. Is it relatable?
8. Does it look like an ad? (It should not; "Winning Ad: ad doesn't look like an ad".)
9. Gut check: does it feel authentic?
10. Which learnings from previous winning ads have we applied?
11. Does it educate the user in a creative way on how to achieve their core desire?
12. Have you applied escalation to exaggerate the claim in a clear, relatable (and truthful) way?
13. Does it tell a story the avatar cares about, entertain them, or educate them with a new, believable mechanism?
14. Does it separate the brand from the competition?

## 11. Concept library, naming and hit rate

Keep one living file (Blueprint live calls: avatars named A01, A02…, a concept library of what is winning, brand book,
static and UGC guidelines; "the one I would also use onboarding a new team member").

**Avatars:** `| ID | Name | Moment(s) | Awareness | Self-concept | Zone | Status | Best concept |`
**Concepts:** `| ID | Avatar | Template | Angle | Mechanism | Status (testing / validated / retired) | Best ad | Iterations live | Hit rate |`
(Blueprint marks a validated concept green: "keep it simple, stupid".)
**Test log** (Evolve roadmap + Denney roadmap columns): `| Batch | Date | Ad name | Type | Parent | Layer / variable |
Hypothesis ("what gives you confidence this test will improve overall performance?") | Spend | CPA/ROAS | Hook | Hold |
CTR | Class | Learning (Ad Review Bible #) | Next action |`

**Hit rate by category** (Pellegrini's fictional example; use your own categories):

| Category | Launched | Winners | Hit rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ugly static | 24 | 5 | 21% |
| Mashup video | 18 | 2 | 11% |
| Static, named mechanism | 15 | 4 | 27% |
| Short VSL | 8 | 0 | 0% |
| Native post | 12 | 3 | 25% |

Ask: which category has a high hit rate, which a low one, which produces "condors" (banger ads)? "Going from 5% to 10%
winners doubles your scaling." Track hit rate **by layer** for iterations too (hook, lead, offer, visual…): it tells you
which layer to pull first on the next winner.

**Naming** (Blueprint: pack names carry audience type, avatar, concept and pack number; Denney: names encode format,
creator, hook and batch so reports can split them):
- Pack (ad set): `broad_<A0X>_<C0X>-<concept>_P<nn>`; pack numbers run across the campaign.
- Ad: `<A0X>_<C0X>_<I|N|M><n>-<layer>-<what>_<VID|STA|CAR>_<creator>_B<batch>`,
  e.g. `A01_C01_I3-hook-lastup_VID_maya_B18`, `A01_C14_N1-sidebyside_STA_B18`.
- Layer codes: `hook`, `msg`, `mech`, `edu`, `offer`, `page`, `visual`, `slice`.
