# Primary-text frameworks — templates and worked examples

Load at Step 4 (planning the variant matrix) and Step 6 (writing the body). Each framework is one *bet*: pick a different
framework for each primary text on the same ad, keep the side, promise and offer identical across them.

All brands below are fictional. Every number, name, quote and study in an example is a placeholder written as
`[REPLACE: …]` or marked "placeholder": replace it with the brand's real, provable data or delete the sentence.
First-person stories must be a real person's words (a customer review or interview used with consent, a creator who used
the product, or the founder) — see `copy-compliance.md` §6.

## 2. F1 Story / testimonial (long) — the 11-beat skeleton

**What it is.** A first-person "this was me" story compressed from the winning advertorials (C6): the reader recognises
the moment, watches someone like them fail with the usual fixes, learns the hidden cause, and sees a dated result.
Oliver's relatability rule: the narrator is "like me but WORSE" at the start. The product enters about a third of the
way in, never in the first line.

**Template**
```
1  [Hook ≤125 chars: mid-scene or mid-identity + one number + one sensory image]
2  [At first … But as the day goes on … Until …]  (2-3 one-line paragraphs)
3  [One social moment: what someone said / what they stopped doing] + [identity line: "I felt __ years older"]
4  [Tried __: failed because __.] [Tried __: __.] [Tried __: __.]   (one line each, max 3)
5  [Turning point: who told me — colleague / professional / "a video from someone like me"]
6  [Cause + mechanism in one plain sentence] + [one felt metaphor]
7  [Result with a number and a timeline — this person's real one]
8  [Social reversal: "Now __ asks me where I got them."]
9  [AOV seed: "I bought __ — one for __, one for __."]
10 [Risk reversal phrased as a promise]
11 [CTA naming the button + a real reason to act now]
   [— Name, role, verified buyer]  (attribution; consent on file)
```

**Worked example** — Shiftwell insoles (fictional), side "ward nurse on 12-hour shifts", problem aware, ought self,
FATIGUE low, lever mechanism.

> I work 12-hour shifts on a ward with 3 floors and one slow elevator. By hour 9 my feet feel like they're full of gravel.
>
> At first it was just the arches. Then the heels. By Friday it was my lower back on the drive home.
>
> One night my 8-year-old asked why I walk "like Grandpa" after work. I laughed. It wasn't funny.
>
> Gel insoles went flat in 2 weeks. Compression socks were hot and changed nothing. Custom orthotics cost
> [REPLACE: $400] and took 6 weeks to arrive.
>
> A charge nurse on my floor kept a spare pair of Shiftwell insoles in her locker. She said: "Your shoes aren't the
> problem. Your arch gives out around hour 6."
>
> That's the idea behind them: a firm arch shell that holds its shape for the whole shift, and a deep heel cup that keeps
> the heel from rolling. Like a kickstand for your foot.
>
> Shift 3, I stopped counting the hours. Week 2, I took the stairs instead of waiting for the elevator.
>
> Two more nurses on my floor wear them now. I bought a second pair for my weekend sneakers.
>
> — [REPLACE: Maria T., RN — real review, used with permission]
>
> 90 nights to try them. If they don't help, send them back worn and we refund you.
> Tap Shop Now and pick your shoe size — we match the arch height for you.

**Watch-outs.** Max three failed fixes (more reads as filler). Keep shame gentle and true; no body-shaming. The timeline
is the narrator's experience, not a promise to the reader.

## 3. F2 PAS with dimensionalised pain

**What it is.** Problem → Agitate → Solution (Oliver), where the agitation walks the three layers: physical → life
consequence → emotional consequence, in the market's own words, then the hidden cause (UMP) and the fix (UMS).

**Template**
```
[Problem as a moment, in their words — ≤125 chars]
[Life consequence: what it stops them doing / costs them]
[Emotional consequence: what it makes them feel]
[Why it keeps happening: UMP in one sentence + metaphor]
[Solution: product delivers UMS, so you can [desire in a place/time]]
[Proof sentence]
[Offer / risk reversal + CTA naming the button]
```

**Worked example** — Rainline hoodie (fictional), side "college student who walks to a 9 a.m. lecture" (an avatar from
the Blueprint Q&A: rain-soaked hoodie that "smells like a wet dog"), problem aware, actual self, FATIGUE low.

> A 10-minute walk to the 9 a.m. lecture, light drizzle, and now you're sitting in a wet hoodie for 90 minutes.
>
> It's cold through the whole class. By lunch it smells like a wet dog. It's still damp when you grab it tomorrow.
>
> Cotton fleece drinks water like a sponge — [REPLACE: a regular hoodie can hold X% of its weight in water].
>
> The Rainline Hoodie looks and feels like your favourite hoodie, but the outer knit sheds water, so drizzle beads off and
> you sit down dry.
>
> [REPLACE: 1,900] students wore one through last semester. [REPLACE: 92%] said they'd buy another.
>
> Free returns for 30 days. Tap Shop Now and pick your colour.

**Watch-outs.** PANIC-level agitation on a product-aware reader backfires (toolkit §8). In health categories the
consequence layer must not be a scare tactic (`copy-compliance.md` §4).

## 4. F3 Listicle ("3 reasons why")

**What it is.** Skimmable numbered reasons (Denney's listicle UGC; C6 "5 Reasons Why…" pages). Reason 1 carries the cause
and mechanism; reason 2 a desire scene; reason 3 proof or the differentiator; an optional bonus reason is the offer.

**Template**
```
[N] reasons [avatar] switch to [product] (#[k] is the one nobody mentions) — ≤125
1) [Cause + mechanism] → [proof]
2) [Desire scene: so you can …] → [proof]
3) [Social proof or differentiator] → [proof]
Bonus: [offer / guarantee]
[CTA naming the button]
```

**Worked example** — Glide Chews (fictional, Tamsin & Hound), side "owner of a senior dog who now stops at curbs",
solution aware, COZY low, lever education.

> 3 reasons senior-dog owners switch to Glide Chews (#1 explains the curb):
>
> 1) It targets the cushion, not just the cartilage. As dogs age, the fluid that cushions their joints thins out
> [REPLACE: source]. Glide Chews pair green-lipped mussel with hyaluronic acid to support it.
>
> 2) Dogs actually eat them. Soft, liver-flavoured, no pill to hide in cheese. [REPLACE: 9 in 10] owners in our survey
> said their dog took them as a treat.
>
> 3) Owners see it on the walk. "Week 3, he stopped pausing at the curb." — [REPLACE: real review, with consent]
>
> Bonus: 60 days to judge. If you see no difference, we refund you, even if the tub is empty.
>
> Tap Learn More to see how the cushion works.

## 5. F4 Us vs them

**What it is.** A side-by-side against the category default (Denney's comparison; Blueprint's us-vs-them template: the
"us" side is the mechanism, the "them" side is the education — what the default gets wrong). Compare against the
category, not a named competitor, and only with true, checkable differences.

**Template**
```
[Avatar call-out + the trade-off they're tired of — ≤125]
✗ [Category default 1] → [the problem it causes]
✗ [Category default 2] → [problem]
✓ [Our mechanism 1] → so you can [desire]
✓ [Our mechanism 2] → so you can [desire]
[Proof line]
[CTA naming the button]
```

**Worked example** — Tenfold press-on nails (fictional), side "mom who hasn't had 90 minutes for a salon since the baby"
(the Blueprint worked avatar), solution aware, ideal self, COZY low, lever mechanism.

> For moms who haven't had 90 minutes for a salon since the baby arrived.
>
> ✗ Salon gel: 90 minutes and [REPLACE: $65] every 2-3 weeks
> ✗ Cheap press-ons: thick, shiny-fake, popping off by Tuesday
> ✓ Tenfold: on in under 10 minutes during nap time
> ✓ An ultra-thin edge that blends into your nail line, so nobody asks "are those fake?"
> ✓ [REPLACE: up to 14] days of wear per set, reusable
>
> [REPLACE: 3,100] reviews, 4.7★. Tap Shop Now and pick your shape.

## 6. F5 Myth-bust

**What it is.** State the belief the market holds, contradict it, explain the real cause (Oliver's lead: "XYZ is the real
cause of [problem], right? WRONG!"; Denney's "Myth vs Truth"). In health categories write "Not quite" rather than
"WRONG!" and keep claims to "may", "one of the reasons" (`copy-compliance.md` §3).

**Template**
```
"[Common belief]," right? [Not quite.] — ≤125
[What actually happens: UMP + an everyday metaphor]
[Why the usual fix misses it]
[What works instead: UMS + product, so you can …]
[Proof]
[CTA naming the button]
```

**Worked example** — Glide Chews (fictional), solution aware, COZY low, lever education (bench text P3 in the SKILL.md
deck).

> "He's just getting old," right? Partly. But the stiffness you see at the curb has a cause most joint chews skip.
>
> Joints glide on a cushion of fluid. With age that fluid thins [REPLACE: source], like old oil in a hinge. The first
> steps on hard ground are the stiff ones, which is why the curb is where it shows.
>
> Most chews load up on glucosamine alone. Glide Chews add green-lipped mussel and hyaluronic acid to support the cushion
> itself [REPLACE: dose per chew + reference].
>
> [REPLACE: 4,212] owners, 4.8★. 60 days to see the difference or your money back.
>
> Tap Learn More to see the 2-minute explainer.

## 7. F6 Founder note

**What it is.** The founder speaks: the moment that started the brand, what the industry does, the hard thing they did
differently, and a first-person promise. Denney lists "long-form founder's copy" among the core copy tests; the
Blueprint calls founder ads "the most consistently good ads"; C6's Blissy page sells through an origin story.

**Template**
```
[I'm [name]. [Brand] exists because of [specific moment, date/place] — ≤125]
[What I tried / what the category does]
[What we did differently: mechanism + the hard part (time, cost, tests)]
[Proof: what customers tell us, with a number]
[A first-person promise: guarantee in plain words]
[CTA naming the button] — [first name]
```

**Worked example** — Glide Chews (fictional), problem aware, ought self, COZY low, lever mechanism.

> I'm [REPLACE: Tamsin]. Glide Chews exist because of one Tuesday: our 12-year-old lab couldn't jump into the car for his
> own vet visit.
>
> The vet said "it's his age." The chews we tried were mostly flavour and filler, and he'd spit half of them out.
>
> So we spent [REPLACE: 14 months] on one question: what keeps an old dog's joints gliding? We landed on the cushioning
> fluid, and a chew with green-lipped mussel and hyaluronic acid that dogs eat like a treat. [REPLACE: 14] recipes before
> ours would eat one.
>
> [REPLACE: 4,212] owners have reviewed them since. The note we get most: "He's waiting for me at the door again."
>
> My promise: give them 60 days. If you don't see a difference, email me and you get every cent back.
>
> Tap Learn More to read the full story. — [REPLACE: Tamsin]

## 8. F7 Offer stack

**What it is.** The deal, laid out so the most-aware reader can decide in one read. Offer = value exchange + timing +
risk profile (Blueprint). In sale periods, Pellegrini's quadruple offer (crossed-out price, a free product or kit, a
mystery gift or voucher, free shipping, plus an extra code) sells the feeling of winning: "they don't run for the
discount, they run to get there before their neighbour." Oliver's close order applies (social proof → urgency →
scarcity → risk reversal → CTA → bundle reason).

Rules: recompute target CPA/ROAS before any offer (`scripts/a2.py economics`; Evolve: "offers can be like drugs").
Give the bundle a reason. Scarcity and deadlines must be real and honoured. Prices end in .99 (Blueprint). Keep offer-led
copy to a minority of the account's copy (Blueprint Q&A: 20-30% of marketing calls out the offer).

**Template**
```
[The deal in plain words + who it's for — ≤125]
✓ [Value: was __ → now __]
✓ [Bundle with its reason: one for __, one for __]
✓ [Gift / bonus]
✓ [Free shipping / threshold]
[Timing: real end date or real stock limit]
[Risk: guarantee]
[CTA naming the button + "code auto-applied" only if the link applies it]
```

**Worked example** — Glide Chews Black Friday (fictional), most aware, JOY high, lever offer.

> Black Friday for senior dogs: the 90-day Glide Chews bundle is [REPLACE: $89.99] (was [REPLACE: $119.97]).
>
> ✓ 3 tubs = 90 days, the stretch most owners say it takes to judge [REPLACE: survey]
> ✓ Free travel tin for the car (walks don't always start at home)
> ✓ Free shipping, arrives in [REPLACE: 2-4] days
>
> Ends [REPLACE: Monday 1 Dec, 11:59 p.m. ET]. 60-day empty-tub guarantee.
>
> Tap Get Offer — the discount is already applied at checkout.

## 9. F8 Objection-killer

**What it is.** Lead with the objection in the customer's own words and answer it with proof. Evolve's Ad Review Bible
lists "mass objection" as a top reason ads fail; the Blueprint builds bottom-of-funnel statics purely from objections;
Kell's 10 + 1 universal objections are the checklist:

1. How do I know this is really for me? 2. What unique benefits do you bring? 3. Why should I believe your claims?
4. Will it work for ME? 5. How is this different from everything I've tried? 6. Why would I get results this time?
7. Why haven't I heard of this already? 8. How come my [expert] never told me? 9. Is this really a good deal?
10. What if I'm not ready to act today? +1. I don't have the money.

**Template**
```
"[Objection, first person, verbatim from reviews/comments]" — ≤125
[Answer with proof]
"[Objection 2]" → [answer]
"[Objection 3]" → [answer]
[Risk reversal]
[CTA naming the button]
```

**Worked example** — Tenfold press-on nails (fictional), product aware, actual self, COZY low.

> "Press-ons always pop off by day 3." It's the #1 thing moms told us before they tried Tenfold.
>
> Ours bond with a flexible base that bends with your nail, so they hold through dishes and bath time.
> [REPLACE: 87%] of reviewers report 10+ days of wear.
>
> "They'll look fake." → An ultra-thin edge sits flush with your nail line. Zoom into the reviews and look for the seam.
>
> "Removal will wreck my nails." → Warm water and the included stick, 5 minutes, no drill, no acetone soak.
>
> Not for you? Return the unused set within 30 days. Tap Shop Now.

## 10. F9 Native post

**What it is.** A plain three-line post that does not read as an ad (Pellegrini's quick win): comments bring the
customer's exact words, engagement lowers CPM, and good buyers come in with the traffic. His prompt, translated:
"Write 10 native Facebook posts of 3 lines in the voice of an ordinary customer. They tell the pain [X] in their words.
Zero ad words. End with a question that calls for a comment."

Compliance adaptation: the "ordinary customer" must be real — run it from the founder's voice, as a partnership ad with a
creator who uses the product, or with a real customer's words used with consent. Never seed comments from fake profiles
(the deck suggests it; Meta and the FTC treat it as fake reviews). Name the product in the headline so nothing is withheld.

**Template**
```
[Line 1: a recognisable moment, first person, no product]
[Line 2: the feeling or the small detail only insiders know]
[Line 3: a question that invites a story in the comments]
Headline: [product + avatar]
```

**Worked example** — Glide Chews founder voice (fictional), unaware, actual self, COZY low.

> Our lab is 12 and still insists on the full park loop.
> Some evenings he stops at the same curb and just looks up at me, like he's asking for a minute.
> What's the one thing your old dog still refuses to give up?
>
> Headline: Glide Chews for senior dogs

## 11. F10 Kindergarten pitch (6 lines)

**What it is.** Kell's "so simple a kindergartener could understand it" pitch turned into copy: problem → root problem →
root solution → product → physical win → emotional win. Kell's own example: "You're showing signs of aging → toxic
progerin breaks down the skin → Progeline reduces progerin by 22% → cream with Progeline → wrinkles go away." Write at a
5th-6th-grade level.

**Template**
```
[Problem, as a moment]
[Root problem: the named cause]
[Root solution: what fixes the cause]
[Product: the easy way to get it]
[Physical win]
[Emotional win] + [CTA naming the button]
```

**Worked example** — Shiftwell insoles (fictional), problem aware, ideal self, COZY low.

> Feet that ache by hour 9 of a shift.
> It usually starts when the arch gives way under hours of standing.
> Hold the arch up, and the ache has nothing to build on.
> Shiftwell insoles do that with a firm shell that keeps its shape for 12+ hours [REPLACE: lab or wear test].
> Hour 9 feels closer to hour 2.
> You drive home thinking about dinner, not your feet. Tap Shop Now.

## 12. F11 Hook + 2-sentence mechanism + proof + CTA (default medium text)

**What it is.** The tightest complete sell (Kell): hook line, the problem mechanism and solution mechanism in two
sentences, one proof line, one CTA. Use it when you need a single text that does everything, or as P2 when P1 is long.

**Template**
```
[Hook — ≤125]
[UMP sentence]. [UMS sentence, naming the product].
[Proof sentence: number + name/timeframe]
[CTA naming the button + reason now]
```

**Worked example** — Glide Chews (fictional), solution aware, ideal self, COZY low, lever mechanism.

## 13. F12 Proof-led: review stack, statistic, "As a ___"

**What it is.** Let the proof carry the ad. Three shapes:
- **Statistic** (Denney's roadmap example: statistics "signal a logical reason to buy" and won for 50+ buyers): one real
  survey or study figure, its source in plain words, one desire line.
- **Review stack**: three short real quotes, each answering a different worry, each attributed.
- **"As a ___"** (Denney's review/testimonial format): a real customer names their identity first, then the result.
Golden-nugget reviews (Denney) — the one review that reveals "a little too much" — can carry a whole ad.

**Template**
```
Statistic:   [N]% of [who, how many, when] said [specific outcome] [after routine/time]. ([source]) — so you can [desire].
Review stack: ✓ "[quote on objection 1]" — [name]  ✓ "[quote on result]" — [name]  ✓ "[quote on ease/taste/fit]" — [name]
"As a ___":  "As a [identity], I [specific before]. Now [specific after]." — [name]
+ [guarantee] + [CTA naming the button]
```

**Worked example** — Glide Chews review stack (fictional), product aware, JOY low (bench text P4 in the SKILL.md deck).

> What [REPLACE: 4,212] senior-dog owners say about Glide Chews:
> ✓ "He eats them like treats. The pill pocket days are over." — [REPLACE: name, verified buyer]
> ✓ "Week 3, he stopped pausing at the curb." — [REPLACE: name]
> ✓ "Our 13-year-old beagle does the stairs on her own again." — [REPLACE: name]
> 60 days to see it, or your money back. Tap Learn More.

## 14. F13 Belief builders (Evolve)

When the reader wants the outcome but doesn't believe it will happen, build belief instead of adding promises. Evolve:
"If you violate your ideal customer's established beliefs in the slightest, then nothing you promise them ... can save
your ad." Four methods:

**Yes-train** — a run of questions the reader answers "yes" to, then "you've experienced what millions of others have",
then the product. Template: `[Yes 1]? / [Yes 2]? / [Yes 3]? / So you [give up]? / Then you've felt what [many] have. /
That's why [product] [new direction].` Keep questions about situations, not personal attributes.

**Symptoms** — prove you've lived it. Template: `Every time I [moment] I [small, specific frustration]. [Fix 1] supposedly
[promise] / but all it ever does is [fail]. [Fix 2] [fails differently]. That's why we made [product]: [best of both].`
Evolve's hockey-sock example: thin socks "supposedly provide more grip / But all they ever do is rip"; thick ones make you
feel "like you're wearing clown shoes on the ice."

**Logic chain** — one statement leading into the next until the product is the only conclusion. Template:
`[How the thing works]. The more [X], the [Y]. But too much [X] causes [harm]. So to get [Y] without [harm], you need
the right amount of [X]. And to get the right amount, you must [measure/adjust]. That's why [product] [does it for you,
with proof].` (Evolve's hair-dryer example: heat dries hair, excess heat damages it, so the dryer cycles temperature
"based on 100,000 tests".)

**Authority flip** — acknowledge the belief bluntly, then replace it with stronger authority. Template: `Forget what
you've heard about [belief]. [Named authority / real study / the brand's own test of N] shows [new belief]. Here's what
that means for you: [desire].` Authority must be real and named.

**Worked example (yes-train)** — Mistwell humidifier (fictional), problem aware, ought self, COZY low:

> Cleaning the humidifier every weekend? Still finding pink slime in the base by Wednesday? Scrubbing parts you can't
> even reach? So you just stop running it, even with a baby in the room? Then you've hit what [REPLACE: thousands of
> parents tell us]. That's why Mistwell has no filter and a tank that goes in the dishwasher. Tap Learn More.

## 15. F14 Short form and the mini-pitch

**Short form** = one sentence or less (Denney): brand intro ("Meet ___"), product drops, discounts, dynamic product ads.
Quick and readable on Instagram, "which you have to consider for delivery." Templates:
- `Meet [product]: [what it does] for [avatar], [proof in 3 words].`
- `Back in stock: [product]. Last batch sold out in [real N] days.`
- `[Product], [one benefit], now [price.99] — [real end date].`
- DPA: `[Product name] — [one benefit]. [Price.99]. Free shipping over [threshold].`

**Mini-pitch** (Oliver): a whole ad in one sentence — who made it, the mechanism, the root cause, the timeframe and the
social proof. Template: `[Who] made a [category] that [UMS] to [fix UMP] in [timeframe], and [N real customers] [result].`
Only claim "going viral" or "taking the US by storm" if true.

**Worked example** — Glide Chews (fictional), product aware, ideal self, JOY low (P2 shape):

> Senior dog waiting at every curb? [REPLACE: 4,212] owners switched to Glide Chews for the old trot back. 60 days to see
> it, or your money back.

## 16. F15 Regulated offers (telehealth / prescription)

From the telehealth bonuses in the Denney bundle (Blanchard, 2024-25): "simple, direct messaging beat clever/cute copy";
"clever/mysterious ad copy" failed. Always include the consultation and prescription language; never put a medication
name in the headline, a price/discount, a pounds-lost number or a time-bound result (`copy-compliance.md` §5).

| Formula | Structure | Example (placeholders; adapt to the offer's approved-claims list) |
|---|---|---|
| Direct (best performer in the source) | headline: benefit + treatment type → body: what it is + who prescribes → ✓ bullets → CTA: free consultation → disclaimer | H: "Hair-loss treatment — free online consultation" · "Get a treatment plan from licensed physicians, online, delivered to your door. ✓ Board-certified doctors ✓ Personalised plan ✓ Ongoing support. See if you qualify. Prescription required if medically appropriate." |
| Social proof | headline: what others use → body: why it's popular → bullets → CTA: join them → disclaimer | H: "The treatment thousands are starting online" · "…Results vary. Prescription required." |
| Problem-solution | headline: the problem (no attribute "you") → body: the medical solution → bullets → CTA → disclaimer | not "Thinning hair at 30? Medical support can help" (a question still implies the attribute) but H: "Medical support for early hair thinning" |

## 17. Shared building blocks

**Close stack, in order (Oliver)** — pick 3-4 for a primary text: more social proof → urgency (real) → scarcity (real) →
risk reversal (sell the guarantee) → crossroads (two paths: stay as you are / the new way) → a direct CTA that names the
button → a bundle reason → a 1-2 sentence restatement.

**Transitions (Oliver)** — "You see…", "That means…", "So why does this matter?", "…because…", "This is why…", "So you
get…". Use one whenever new information starts; they keep the reader sliding.

**Future pacing to tonight** — the brain treats "you in 3 months" as a stranger (Pellegrini), so put the first win
close: "tonight's walk", "tomorrow's 7 a.m. school run", "your next 12-hour shift". Devices that compress well (Kell, C7):
- "What that REALLY means is…" — turns a feature into a lived moment.
- Named moment — "the morning he beats you to the door".
- Other people's reactions — "your charge nurse asks where you got them".
- A small triumph — "clicking the seat belt without asking for an extender" (C7).
- Timeline ladder — day 1 / week 3 / month 2, only from real data.
- Two realities (Kell) — "Reality 1: another winter of short walks. Reality 2: the full park loop." Keep it gentle in
  health copy.
Block the "later" escape: one tap, code auto-applied, a 2-minute quiz.

**AOV seeds (C6)** — a real reason to buy more than one: one per knee/shoe/room/car, one for work and one for home, a
gift, a supply that matches the time it takes to work ("3 tubs = 90 days"), stock-up before a season.

**CTA lines** — always name the button (Oliver: "tell them EXACTLY which button to click"); make them specific to the ad
("We're not going to write shit with AI", Blueprint Q&A). Kell's CTA types: identity CTA ("for every owner who wants
one more good year of walks"), gift CTA ("or send one to the friend whose dog is slowing down"), challenge CTA ("take
the 30-day curb test"), no-pressure CTA ("not ready? Save this post for after tonight's walk"). Avoid "this might be the
last time you see this ad" and similar unless literally true.

**Selling the gap** (Blueprint) — if the price is above the market ($29 vs $19-23), the copy must earn the extra $10
explicitly: the ingredient, the guarantee, the cost of the alternative. Or reframe against a more expensive way to get
the same desire (Evolve: at-home laser vs salon laser, not vs razors).

**Offer share** — offer-led copy is the minority: about 20-30% of the account's messaging; the rest is "surprise and
delight" (Blueprint Q&A). Evergreen offers can be framed without "20% off sitewide" (Blueprint): bundle value, gift,
free shipping.
