# Hook bank — the three-layer hook, categories, devices, compliance

Hooks are the cheapest high-leverage change in video: the same body with a new hook is a different ad to Meta ("Meta is
looking at every basically phrase… Meta will 100% see these as different", Blueprint live calls), a hook "can turn a VSL
from a loser to a winner" (Oliver), and Kell's media buyers own hook testing because hooks are "very easy micro changes".
Write 3-5 per body, each from a different category below.

## 1. The three layers and the rules

| Layer | Time | Job | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Micro-hook | 0-1 s | Pattern disrupt before any words land ("a cough… put the camera down… brush the makeup") | Blueprint |
| Spoken + on-screen hook | to ~5 s, 2-3 sentences max | Intrigue + value + who it's for; leads straight into the body; the on-screen headline carries it muted | Blueprint, Oliver |
| Visual hook | 0-3 s | The image that stops the thumb on its own, ideally hard to contest | Pellegrini, Denney |

Rules:
- **All three layers tell one idea.** Oliver's hook triad: voiceover, on-video headline and visual "should be related to one
  another and to the big idea… and should create tension, open loops, and create curiosity".
- **Signal the side in the first line or frame** — by name, behaviour, situation, aspiration or casting (Blueprint). "A
  great hook evokes curiosity while simultaneously calling out our ideal customer" (Oliver). A hook that calls the wrong
  audience wins thumb-stops and loses the lead.
- **One villain or contradiction + one concrete detail** (a number, a scene, an object) in the first sentence (VSL swipes).
- **Never open with credentials, a logo or a brand slate** (VSL swipes; Oliver). The product may appear (hands demo); the
  introduction comes later.
- **Open a question the mechanism answers by name** later in the body (VSL swipes) — and make sure the payoff over-delivers
  (Pellegrini's 522-day winner vs the loser whose "reveal disappoints").
- **Easy and fast payoff** ("one simple thing", "seconds a day") without promising medical speed.
- **Their words for the pain** ("toilet-paper skin", "seat-belt extender", "like I never went to bed"), but no
  second-person assertion of a personal attribute (§6).
- **Muted test**: the on-screen text alone must make the viewer want the next second.
- **Hook rate is ~1 second now** (Denney): the first frame and the first 3 words do most of the work. Kell's playbook: "On
  FB & YouTube you really only have 2 seconds."

## 2. Micro-hook devices (0-1 s)

- A sound before the image settles: leash clip, hinge squeak, pour, snap, cough, a laugh, a door slam.
- A physical interruption: putting the camera down, a hand entering frame, a prop thrust toward the lens, a drop.
- A spoken lead-in word: "Okay." · "Wait." · "You wouldn't believe…" · "So." (Blueprint lead-in)
- A mid-sentence start: the quote already in progress ("—he's just getting old.").
- The moment itself in motion: the dog stopping at the curb, the hand pulling the long sleeve down.
- Silence + the demo already happening (Pellegrini: silence 0-2 s, then the voice reveals).
- Reverse loop or oddly satisfying texture (Denney); a whip pan or snap zoom into the object.
- A text flash of 1-3 words in large type over the moment ("Not his age.").

## 4. Spoken + on-screen hook bank

Templates in `[ ]`. Examples are illustrations: every number, name, study and result must be replaced with the brand's
real, provable data. Fit = awareness · zone.

### A. Side call-outs ("that's me")
1. **Situation / behaviour call-out** — "If you've started [behaviour that betrays the side], watch this." · "If you've
   started lifting your dog into the car, watch what mine did on week four." · "Moms who don't have time for the salon but
   still want salon nails — these go on in 60 seconds." (Blueprint). Fit: problem aware · FATIGUE/COZY.
2. **Name the moment, not the label** — "Crush your day without the 2 p.m. crash." (Blueprint) · "For the 3 a.m. wake-ups
   that make 7 a.m. impossible." Fit: problem aware · FATIGUE.
3. **Behaviour switch** — "Why I switched from [pills] to [gummies]." Skips the convincing; talks to people already buying
   the category (Blueprint). Fit: solution aware · COZY.
4. **Identity in first person** — "As a [34GGG / night-shift nurse / dad of three]…" (Denney). Fit: problem/product · COZY.
5. **Identity question / reframe** — "Is there such a thing as a 'bad memory'?" (Kell's identity question) · "Are vegans
   healthy? French fries are vegan." (Organifi). Fit: unaware · JOY.

### B. Pain moment (FATIGUE; PANIC only problem-aware, low dose)
6. **Sensory first-person moment** — "I got tired of asking for a seat-belt extender." · "I fell flat on my back in a
   parking lot, still holding the balloons." (VSL swipes, customer stories) · "He stops at this curb now." Fit: problem aware.
7. **Personal story of suffering** — "[Pain] made me feel like [a prisoner in my own body]." (Oliver) — first person only.
8. **Loved-one quote** — "'[Line a partner/child/friend said]'" · "'You thought she was my daughter, didn't you?'" (VSL swipes) ·
   "My husband said 'babe, your eyes look bad.'" (Denney golden nugget). Fit: problem aware · FATIGUE.
9. **"We were fine until we weren't"** — "We were a happy family until we weren't." (Charity Water) — founder/story only.

### C. Contradiction / real cause (mechanism and education levers; solution aware)
10. **Real-cause myth-bust** — "What really causes [X]? Not [A], not [B], not even [C]." (VSL swipes) · "It's not his age. It's his
    hinges." · "Wrinkles aren't what makes you look older. This is." (VSL swipes)
11. **"Right? Nope."** — "[Common fix] helps [problem], right? Nope. What about [fix 2]? Nope." (Oliver/Dainely). Needs
    real evidence for the reversal.
12. **"Before you…, watch this"** — "Before you take another shot of [apple cider vinegar], watch this." · "Wait. Watch this
    before you touch your face." (VSL swipes)
13. **"Stop doing…"** (about the method, never the person) — "Stop using these for [ear cleaning]." (Tvidler) · "STOP doing
    [the thing they try] that doesn't [solve it]." (Kell coaching)
14. **Prop + "think again"** — "This is a bag of 'premium organic' [dog food]. Think it's healthy? Think again." (VSL swipes)
15. **Paradox** — "The healthiest diet in the world is loaded with [toxins]. So why does it work?" (VSL swipes)
16. **Belief flip** — "People think [learning piano] takes years. That's false. Here's how long it takes with this method."
    (Ridley, then show the progression)
17. **Then vs now** — "[Category] used to look like this. Now it looks like this." (Sam: "every time I say it, it works")
18. **"Can't…? Do this"** — "Can't [root-cause problem]? Do this [your solution]." (Kell coaching) · "Still [losing hair]?
    Do this for 2 seconds tonight." (student hook) — avoid "you"-attribute phrasing in health (§6).

### D. Curiosity / discovery
19. **Envied exception / tale of two** — "Have you ever noticed some women never seem to age?" · "Two sisters. Same habits.
    One looks 20 years younger." (VSL swipes, Kell). Use "look", "feel"; no anti-aging claims you can't support.
20. **Curious case** — "The curious case of [famous person]. [Achievement]. But what people don't know is…" (VSL swipes)
21. **Skeptic-turned-believer** — "I laughed when I heard [weird thing] helps [problem]. Then I looked at the studies."
    (Kell, VSL swipes) — only with real studies · "I rolled my eyes at a fourth [joint chew]."
22. **Weird object / everyday metaphor** — "Knowing what happened to this brown banana is the key to your [energy]." (Kell)
    · "This hinge is my dog's hips."
23. **Guess / quiz** — "Can you guess which of these [5 foods] [effect]?" · "[73]% of people get this wrong." (VSL swipes, real
    number only)
24. **Timer open loop** — "What's the best-kept secret to [skill]? I'll answer in 30 seconds. Start the clock." (Kell,
    Superior Singing) — then deliver on time.
25. **Brain tattoo** — a quotable aphorism: "An addiction to distraction will be the death of your creative production."
    (Robin Sharma, Kell's "highest lead on all of Mindvalley")
26. **Real discovery / news** — "[Real institution] researchers found [surprising mechanism]." (Flow Show "new discovery
    hook") — never invent the institution.
27. **Third-party discovery** — "I was scrolling and saw a clip of [person] doing [trend]. Turns out…" (VSL swipes, City Beauty)

### E. Result / desire first (brand-safe default; JOY/COZY; problem → product aware)
28. **Fascinating benefit with a time mechanism** — "After 21 days my teeth are the whitest they've ever been." (Snow;
    Kell: "one of the best ways for a brand to advertise"). Not for weight/disease timeframes.
29. **Golden-nugget reaction** — "25 minutes later my husband said my eyes looked MUCH better." (Denney) · "My neighbour asked
    if I'd gotten a new dog."
30. **Personal story of victory** — "How I got [my mornings back] after [5 years of neck stiffness]." (Oliver, softened)
31. **Ideal-self projection** — "Get back the [hairline you had at 25], without [a transplant]." (Pellegrini, ideal self)
32. **Actual-self validation** — "Your [wife] says your [hair] looks fine. She's right. But imagine if it were even better."
    (Pellegrini, actual self)
33. **Ought-self standard** — "You take care of your business, your family, your fitness. Time to take care of [X] too."
    (Pellegrini, ought self)
34. **Mini-pitch in one sentence** — "[Who] made [product] that [mechanism] — and it's [real social proof]." (Oliver)
35. **Before / after in own words** (only categories where Meta allows it; never weight, health, aging bodies) — "This was
    my [garage floor] before [method]. This is it now."

### F. Native / platform
36. **Comment reply** — "Replying to @[user]: '[real question]'" (Denney TikTok response)
37. **Rapid comment Q&A** — "Someone asked if [product] works on [X]. [X]? Yes." (VSL swipes, Gundry)
38. **Absurd / diverse** — "If you drop your dog…" vs "So many people carry their dog like this. We carry ours like
    here." (Sam: "every hook is really different… diverse hooks")
39. **Quick product-in-use** — "Yo, check these cargos out." → put them on, instantly (Sam: "a hook specific to that person")
40. **Unboxing / haul / "made me buy it"** formats (Denney format list)

### G. Offer-led (most aware)
41. "By the way, we're doing buy two, get one free right now." (Sam) — no fake deadlines.
42. "Take the [21]-day challenge." (Snow)
43. "Or how about buying this as a gift?" (Kell, Coolina flow)
44. "Only the first [50] orders get [100% cashback]." (Pellegrini BF urgency — a real, visible limit)
45. "60 days or your money back — even if the bag is empty." (VSL swipes: City Beauty put its guarantee in the hook)

## 5. Picker: awareness × zone → categories

| Awareness | Strongest categories | Zone pairing |
|---|---|---|
| Unaware | D (curious case, paradox, brain tattoo), A5, C17, street-interview questions | COZY, JOY |
| Problem aware | B (pain moment), A1-A2, C12-C13, E30 | FATIGUE (PANIC low dose, never shaming) |
| Solution aware | C (myth-bust, before-you, prop), A3, D21-D22, us-vs-them | COZY |
| Product aware | E (result first, golden nugget), F, testimonial montage openers | COZY, JOY |
| Most aware | G | JOY |

Sophistication 5 (jaded market): indirect and story hooks (D20, B9, founder) — hide the pitch (Oliver). Saturated
"Brian" (47 hair-loss ads this week, Pellegrini): COZY, no claims, a friend's recommendation tone.

## 6. Compliance rewrites (Meta personal-attributes and health rules; Kell's own compliance deck)

| Risky | Brand-safe |
|---|---|
| "Are you overweight?" / "Your belly fat…" | First person: "What finally worked after three diets didn't." · Situation: "For anyone who's tried every plan and still dreads the beach." |
| "Your knees are failing you" | "The knee that fires at 3 a.m. — here's what helped me." |
| "Do you have acne?" | "I used to cancel plans when my skin flared. Here's what changed." |
| "These 5 foods ruin your gut" (fear lead) | "How 5 everyday foods affect digestion" (Kell deck: impact, change; not ruin, kill, destroy) |
| "Lose 20 lbs in 30 days" | "How I stopped restarting my diet every Monday." (no pounds, no timeframes for weight) |
| "Doctors are lying to you about your pain" | "What most of us were told about [X] — and what [real study] found." |
| "Secret", "miracle", "breakthrough" | "key", "method", "way" (Kell deck) |
| "Cures / eliminates / reverses" | "helps", "supports", "promotes" — describe the experience and the mechanism |
| "The billion-dollar industry doesn't want you to see this" | A checkable reason: "most chews only add glucosamine" |
| "Ends tonight!" on an evergreen offer | Only real deadlines and real stock |
| Disease names in the body copy | Structure/function wording; no disease names even if "the doctor" says them (Kell deck) |

Branding timing (sources disagree): Oliver wants no branding or "salesy" elements before the product reveal so the ad
feels native; Kell's compliance deck wants the product named in the video for transparency. Default: no logo slate up
front, the product visible early (hands demo), its name spoken or shown by the reveal and always in the headline and
primary text; in health and supplement categories name it inside the hook window.

Also: never zoom on body parts; no before/after side by side for weight, health, aging or cosmetic results; close every
loop you open (the deck flags withheld information). Kell's Lead Bible swipes (cashews in the trash, "$400k/day" weight lead) break his
own deck — borrow the beat order, never the claims.

## 7. Where new hooks come from

- **Golden-nugget reviews** — mine Amazon reviews → own site → ad comments → organic comments; columns: pain, trigger,
  transformation, theme, golden nugget (Denney). One review can be the whole ad.
- **Your best organic content** — a Blueprint member: "70%, I'd say 80% of my best performing ads were stolen hooks from my
  best performing organic content."
- **YouTube** — search the side's problem, sort by most viewed, write down the first 3 sentences of the top videos and
  study thumbnails and titles (Kell coaching).
- **Ad Library long-runners** — long-running ads and concepts with many variations are proven (Blueprint flywheel;
  Pellegrini's day counts). Kell's weekly ritual: 10 new hooks from spying.
- **Customer calls** — call ~50 customers in 2-3 days; "what's the one thing this solves for you?" (Blueprint).
- **Prompt chain for raw emotional language** (Kell coaching): "What are 20 things [side] lies awake at night anxious
  about?" → "Rewrite it more emotional; things they wouldn't admit publicly." → "Tell me a story about [side] living
  this." Use as raw material only; verify against real reviews.
- **Hook discovery score** (Evolve): score each customer pain statement 1-5 on appeal, exclusivity, clarity, motivation;
  turn the winner into four forms: obvious-yes question, desired future state, first-person direct, if/then.
- Strip AI clichés ("skyrocket", "game-changer", "dent"); Sam: "no one log on to ChatGPT" for hooks — diverse beats
  generated.

## 8. Testing hooks

- 3-5 hooks per body, each a different category; keep the body identical so the hook is the variable (Oliver tests 3-5,
  his editors deliver 5 per VSL; creator packages carry 3+ hooks → 3+ ads, Blueprint).
- A new hook on a winning body is an **iteration** of the same concept; a new side or template is a new concept (doctrine R4).
- Don't transplant hooks between two live winners — the Blueprint tested it and "the swap doesn't actually help", partly
  because social proof (comments) stays on the originals. Write new hooks instead.
- Judge hooks on hook rate first, then hold rate and CTR: a hook that lifts thumb-stops but drops hold is calling the wrong
  person or giving the wrong impression of what follows (Oliver).
- Expect few winners: "If 1 out of 20 hooks performs like this, it's a BIG WIN!" (Kell playbook).
- Launch hook variants as a new pack, not into a live ad set (see `andromeda2-launch-testing`).
