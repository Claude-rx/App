Whole-Home Placement Guide

Where to put your 36 pouches

You have three packs: 36 pouches. This guide shows where each one goes in a typical house, so the scent barrier sits on the routes mice actually use.

The one rule: a pouch works at the gap, not in the middle of the room. Mice follow walls and squeeze in around pipes, under doors and through corners. Put the pouch there.

1. Find the entry points first (10 minutes)

  • Under the kitchen and bathroom sinks, where pipes go through the wall
  • Behind the stove, fridge and dishwasher
  • Garage door corners and the door into the house
  • Basement sill, utility lines, sump and furnace area
  • Dryer vent and laundry hookups
  • Attic hatch and eaves
  • Exterior doors with a gap under them
  • Anywhere you have seen droppings or chew marks

A mouse needs a gap about the size of a coin. If you can see daylight or feel a draft, treat it as an entry point.

Smaller house or no basement? Move those pouches to the kitchen and garage. Each pouch covers roughly 65 sq. ft.; along a wall, space them 8 to 10 feet apart.

3. Placing them

  1. Take the pouch out of the bag and place it whole. Do not cut it open.
  2. Put it at the gap or in the corner, on the floor or a shelf, not out in the open.
  3. Write the date on it with a marker. Each pouch works for up to 90 days.
  4. Keep it dry and away from direct heat. Do not block vents.
  5. Keep pouches where children and pets cannot chew on them.
  6. Reseal the bag with the pouches you have not placed yet.

4. What to expect

  • First 72 hours: most customers notice less activity.
  • By week two: the entry points are covered and the old trails are masked.
  • Day 90: replace the pouches. If you subscribed, your refill arrives on schedule.

Results vary with how severe the infestation is and how many entry points you have. Pouches are designed to repel mice; they are not a replacement for professional pest control in an established infestation.
