# Static format catalogue

Every working static format, with when to use it, the layout for both rooms, the on-image copy rules, the awareness stage and
lever it serves, how to iterate it, and the traps. A format is a **template**; it only becomes a concept when you put a side
into it (concept = avatar + template, Blueprint). Choose the side and the angle first, then the format.

## 0. Canvas, safe zones, hierarchy

- **Two rooms, two layouts.** Ship 1:1 (1080×1080) and 9:16 (1080×1920) for every static, designed separately — square and
  vertical are where ~99% of spend goes; 4:5 is no longer the standard; horizontal shows ~2-5% of the time, reuse the square
  (Blueprint). Upload the 9:16 as its own asset ("replace", not crop). No Flexible format (Meta crops "important parts"), no
  media-altering enhancements, no Meta image generation (Blueprint).
- **9:16 safe zones** (Meta placement guidance; overlays change, so confirm in the Ads Manager preview): Reels — keep text, logo
  and the product out of the top ~14% (≈270 px), the bottom ~35% (≈670 px) and ~6% at each side. Stories — top and bottom
  ~14% (≈250 px). Background imagery may run under the zones; meaning may not.
- **1:1:** ~60 px margin on every side; nothing critical in the corners (badges get clipped by some placements).
- **Carousel cards:** 1080×1080, 2-10 cards; card 1 is the hook and must work alone.
- **Hierarchy (max three text levels + logo):** L1 main line (≤ ~7 words, biggest, high contrast) → L2 support (≤ ~15 words)
  → L3 proof/offer chip (stars, number, guarantee, price). Logo small. Text-native formats (E1-E5, A2, C1, C6) may carry
  ~40 words if every line is short and readable.
- **Thumb test:** shrink the image to 25%. If you cannot get the main line and the focal subject in one glance, simplify.
  "Visual has too much going on", "too much text", "confuses what is sold" are top reasons statics fail (Evolve Ad Review Bible).

## 1. Picker

| Awareness (side) | Main lever | First-choice formats |
|---|---|---|
| Unaware | Education, identity, story | B2, D4, D6, E1, E4, F3 |
| Problem aware | Pain moment → hidden cause → desire | B1, D1, E3, E1, D5, B3 |
| Solution aware | Mechanism, enemy, value gap | A1, A2, D1, D2, A3, D4 |
| Product aware | Proof, objection handling | C1, C2, C3, C4, C5, C6, B4 |
| Most aware | Offer, timing, risk reversal | G1, G2, G3, G4, H3 |
| Existing customers | Buy again, new, restock, cross-sell | G5, H2, C4 |

- **Starter round (new account or new pixel):** one side, same message, four formats — us vs them, feature call-out (D2),
  testimonial (C1), headline ad (F1) — to learn which form the side reacts to; then test messages; then big swings (Denney:
  format first, then message, then swings; the platform hook is now ~1 second).
- **Hit-rate log:** tag every static with its format code and keep `format | launched | winners | hit rate`. Pellegrini's
  illustrative table ("exemple fictif — replace with your own categories"): ugly static 24 launched / 5 winners = 21%;
  named-mechanism static 15 / 4 = 27%; native post 12 / 3 = 25%; mashup video 11%; short VSL 0%. Going from 5% to 10%
  winners doubles your scaling — shift production toward the formats that hit **in this account**.
- **"Ugly" statics — current status: weak.** Blueprint's ~10 working templates (Moonlighters, ~100 brands, 2026) leave them
  out: they "fell off in the last six plus months because it's literally not working". Pellegrini's table still shows them
  hitting for some accounts. Default: do not build them as a category; if the account's own log shows them at or above its
  average hit rate, keep making them. Native lo-fi (E1-E5) is not the same thing — it borrows the feed's grammar, not bad design.
- **Longevity is the signal.** When you study competitors' statics, the ones running longest with the most variants are the
  proven templates (Blueprint: "longevity plus volume"). Steal the template; never the message or the visual everyone runs
  (exhausted visuals/messaging, Evolve).

## 2. A — Comparison

### A1. Us vs Them (two-column comparison)
- **Use when:** the side already tried the category and was let down. Solution/product aware. Levers: mechanism ("us" side)
  + education ("them" side = the gap: what the alternative gets wrong and how you solve it) (Blueprint).
- **Layout:** 1:1 — two columns; "us" left with the product in use in brand colour; "them" right with the generic alternative,
  desaturated; 3-4 call-out rows aligned across; verdict chip at the bottom. 9:16 — headline band under the top safe zone,
  columns in the middle 50%, verdict chip above the bottom 35%.
- **On-image copy:** header "[Product] vs. [the thing they already tried]" (≤ 7 words); call-outs ≤ 4 words each as paired
  opposites ("Springs back" / "Flat by month 3"). Call-outs come from THIS side's wants — busy mom: "on in 60 seconds",
  "salon-grade", "all-in-one kit"; enthusiast: "won't damage the nail". Generic call-outs mean you are "talking into the abyss".
- **Iterate (Blueprint's four images of one concept):** call-outs on a blank brand-colour background → the side showing the
  result to camera → the side using the product → product perfect vs alternative failing (chipping, flat, leaking). Then
  call-out swaps, then a carousel version (H4).
- **Watch-outs:** compare against a category ("regular foam beds", "drugstore strips"), not a named competitor's logo, unless
  every claim is provable. Telehealth/health: no comparison to other treatments (Blanchard).

### A2. Comparison table / checklist grid
- **Use when:** several alternatives compete (DIY, cheap version, professional service) — solution aware, mechanism + value.
- **Layout:** rows = 4-6 criteria the side cares about, columns = you + 2-3 alternatives; ✓/✗ or short words; your column
  highlighted. 9:16: keep ≤ 5 rows so the type stays large.
- **On-image copy:** title as the side's decision ("Before you buy another [category]"); criteria in their words (price per
  month, time per day, mess, returns). Example (advertorial swipes): Fire Blanket vs extinguisher — "Lightweight / Heavy metal
  tank · Easy cleanup / Powdery mess · Never expires / Expires".
- **Iterate:** reorder rows to lead with a different criterion; swap one alternative; add a price row.
- **Watch-outs:** every ✗ must be true for the whole category, not one bad product.

### A3. Sell-the-gap / price anchor
- **Use when:** you are priced above the market or price is the top objection. Solution/product aware. Lever: offer + mechanism.
  Rule: if you charge $29 and the market is $19-23, the ad must create the extra $10 of value explicitly (Blueprint). Or switch
  the comparison to a more expensive way of getting the same desire — bamboo pyjamas vs silk, at-home laser vs salon sessions
  (Evolve, product value).
- **Layout:** left the expensive path stacked up ("$450/session × 6"), right your one-time price; or cost per day/use; or
  "3 cheap ones = $240 / one that lasts = $149".
- **On-image copy:** main line names the gap ("Cheaper than the 3rd flat bed"); support gives the maths; chip = guarantee.
  Price ends in .99 (Blueprint: $29.99 beats $30.00).
- **Watch-outs:** use real prices you can source; no medication prices in telehealth ads (Blanchard).

## 3. B — Problem → transformation

### B1. Problem–solution split
- **Use when:** problem aware; the default "all ads are problem-solution in some way" (Blueprint). Angle: pain moment → desire.
- **Layout:** 1:1 — left = the pain moment (time, place, gesture — "2 a.m. on the kitchen tile"), right = the moment regained,
  same subject, same angle, warmer light. 9:16 — stacked: problem top, result bottom, headline in the seam.
- **On-image copy:** main line names the moment, not the feature ("He picks the cold tile. Again."); support links cause →
  solution in one line; optional label chips "Before [product]" / "Week 2".
- **Visual rule:** match light to emotion — dim, close, muted for the pain; bright, human, moving for the outcome. Never a
  smiling stock face on the pain side (psychology toolkit §9).
- **Iterate:** new moment (morning stiffness vs 3 a.m.), new person of the same side, new result scene.

### B2. Problem vs root-cause split (Oliver)
- **Use when:** unaware/problem aware and sophistication 4+ (the market has heard every claim). Lever: education.
- **Layout:** one side shows the problem in action; the other an "intriguing and strange" image of the root cause — a magnified
  fibre, a cross-section, a dehydrated cartilage illustration, a flattened foam core.
- **On-image copy:** "It's not [belief]. It's [named cause]." Keep the mechanism simple enough to get in 2 seconds.
- **Watch-outs:** the cause must be real or a fair simplification; label illustrations as illustrations.

### B3. Expectation vs reality split
- **Use when:** the side has been burned by online shopping or a category of cheap copies; apparel, home, beauty.
- **Layout:** "What you thought you bought" (the glossy model shot) vs "What you actually got" (the "dinky thing in a
  saran-wrapped bag"), then show what you actually deliver (Blueprint live calls).
- **On-image copy:** the two labels + one line on why yours is different.
- **Watch-outs:** the "reality" side shows a generic category item, never a competitor's identifiable product.

### B4. Before/after (policy-safe)
- **Use when:** the transformation is visible on an object, space or appearance where Meta allows it (cleaning, lawn, car,
  home, hair styling, some beauty). Product aware. A classic Denney format and add-on.
- **Layout:** same framing, same light, same angle, labelled with a real timeframe ("Day 1 / Day 30"); real customer photos with
  written consent, or your own documented test.
- **Never:** in weight loss, telehealth, health conditions or body-shape claims (Blanchard: "instant ban, no exceptions");
  idealised or AI-generated "results"; zoom-ins on body parts; scales with numbers; underwear/revealing clothing. Compliant
  substitute for health: lifestyle + product image ("healthy meal prep + product") or a dated progress object (Blanchard).

## 4. C — Proof and objections

### C1. Testimonial / review card
- **Use when:** product aware or bottom of funnel; also as a cold hook when the review is a "golden nugget" — a review that
  shows a prior pain or the transformation in a memorable, slightly-too-personal way (Denney: "25 minutes later my husband said
  my eyes looked MUCH better").
- **Layout options:** (a) review screenshot as it appears on the site/app; (b) the review text "exactly as is" set in a clean
  card with the product shot (Blueprint live calls: "doctor them up in Canva"); (c) pull-quote over a real customer photo;
  (d) wall of 3-6 short reviews. Stars, first name + initial or role ("night-shift nurse"), "verified buyer" only if true.
- **On-image copy:** the quote IS the main line — cut to the strongest ≤ 12 words with an ellipsis if needed, never reworded.
  Chip: rating + review count.
- **Iterate:** different review for a different side (the review that names the side's job or moment), different photo,
  quote → statistic (C2).
- **Watch-outs:** real reviews only, with permission for names/photos; no AI faces presented as customers; a screenshot of UGC
  with text slapped on is weak — use a proper still (Blueprint).

### C2. Statistic / number image
- **Use when:** you need a logical reason to buy inside an emotional purchase; it has won for brands targeting 50+ (Denney).
  Product aware; works as proof add-on.
- **Layout:** one big number (60-70% of the text weight), a 6-10-word line saying what it measures, macro product/texture shot
  (Denney's winner: "99% of women found their dream shade" / "93% reported no smudging" over a macro of the pen texture — top
  image of the month; comments asked for more colours → next iteration showed more colours). Red circle/underline on the number
  (Oliver).
- **On-image copy:** "[N]% of [who] [specific result] in [time]" + fine print with sample and date.
- **Iterate:** same stat into a video hook; new stat; new texture image.
- **Watch-outs:** every number comes from a real survey/test with n and date you can show; no "clinically proven" without the study.

### C3. Press / authority bar
- **Use when:** product aware; trust is the bottleneck (jewellery, supplements, high ticket). "Featured in Vogue / on Fox" is a
  trusted-brand lever among highest-delivery ads (Blueprint live calls).
- **Layout:** product hero + a strip of real logos ("As seen in"), or a named expert's quote with their photo and credential.
- **Watch-outs:** only outlets that actually covered you, only experts who agreed in writing; never invent "Dr." quotes or a
  publication; authority by role ("a 25-year firefighter") must be a real person.

### C4. Many-customers grid (compilation)
- **Use when:** the product must look right on many different people (apparel, beauty) or social proof by volume. Andromeda tends
  to show an ad to people resembling the person in it — a compilation of different customers widens who it reads as "for me"
  (Blueprint live calls).
- **Layout:** 4-9 real customer photos in a grid, product visible in each; one line ("[N] dog owners. Same bed.").
- **Iterate:** the grid filtered to one side (all nurses, all large dogs) = a sharper concept.

### C5. Proof-data screenshot / hard-to-argue demo still
- **Use when:** the result can be shown as data or a neutral, everyone-knows-it object test. Product aware; also strong cold.
- **Examples:** app screenshot "snoring 1h 48m → 20 min"; dashboard "18 mpg → 28.5 mpg" (advertorial swipes); egg on three
  insoles, sauce sliding off a treated shoe, scissors on a gutter guard (Pellegrini: hard-to-contest visual hooks, real demos).
- **Layout:** the proof object large, one caption, product in frame.
- **Watch-outs:** the data and the demo must be real and reproducible; disclose test conditions in the primary text.

### C6. FAQ / objection killer (bottom of funnel)
- **Use when:** the side knows you and one objection stands between them and the cart. Build statics for the bottom of the
  funnel: list every objection (prompt for 100), one static per objection, answer directly — price → "lasts 2× longer",
  durability → the test; "ads you can crank out in a day" (Blueprint live calls).
- **Layout:** recurring background (same scene every time — e.g. "crappy East-Coast lawns" for a lawn brand), the objection as a
  question in L1, the answer in ≤ 12 words in L2, proof chip. The recurring background makes 20 objections one coherent concept
  you can iterate.
- **On-image copy:** objection in the customer's words ("Is it uncomfortable?", "Will it fit a 90-lb dog?"); admit a minor flaw
  when true — honesty beats.

## 5. D — Mechanism and education

### D1. Named-mechanism diagram
- **Use when:** solution aware, sophistication 3-4; the side asks "why would it work this time?". Pellegrini's illustrative
  hit rate for named-mechanism statics is the highest in his table (27%); Frøya Organics' "arctic autophagy" ran 485 days as a
  static, then 569 days as a video.
- **Layout:** cause (UMP) → solution (UMS) in one picture: cross-section, 3 layers, before/after of the mechanism (not the
  body), arrow from cause to fix. Build in a design tool (vector), use AI only for the background or texture.
- **On-image copy:** "[UMP name]: [one-line cause]" / "[UMS name]: [what it does] in [time]". Link rule: A causes B, so C solves
  B — obvious in 2 seconds; a mechanism that needs a paragraph is too complex.
- **Iterate:** same mechanism, different side's moment; isolate one component; name → describe → feature the mechanism (Evolve).

### D2. "3 reasons why" / feature call-out (point-out)
- **Use when:** solution/product aware; a starter-round staple (Denney features point-out; Blueprint "three reasons why").
- **Layout:** product centred (≥ 1/3 of frame), 3-5 thin leader lines to call-outs; or numbered list beside the product.
- **On-image copy:** each call-out is a desire or a proof, not a spec ("Hips stay off the floor", not "6-inch foam"); title
  "3 reasons [side] switch to [category noun]".
- **Iterate:** swap the call-outs (Blueprint's call-out bank — "I can come up with a thousand call-outs"), swap the side's
  reasons, add a review chip.

### D3. How it works in 3 steps / infographic
- **Use when:** warming cold traffic to a new behaviour (telehealth, subscriptions, devices). Telehealth data: lower CTR, higher
  quality clicks; one "How it works in 3 steps" test was paused after 3 days (Blanchard) — use as a supporting ad, not a hero.
- **Layout:** three panels with icons/photos, one verb each ("Unzip. Place. Sleep.").

### D4. Myth vs truth
- **Use when:** a false belief keeps the side from buying ("it's just age", "all foam is the same"). Unaware → problem aware.
- **Layout:** two stacked cards, "Myth" crossed out, "Truth" in brand colour; or a 4-myth carousel (Ridge wallet pattern, Denney).
- **On-image copy:** the myth in the side's own words; the truth as a named cause.

### D5. Self-check list ("signs your…")
- **Use when:** problem aware; helps the side recognise the problem. List layout of 3-5 symptoms/situations (Oliver's
  self-diagnosis checklist).
- **Compliance:** describe situations or objects ("Signs your dog's bed has gone flat"), never assert a personal attribute or
  condition of the viewer ("Signs you have…").

### D6. Editorial / news-style image
- **Use when:** unaware; curiosity + education; advertorial traffic (Oliver: news-style hero image). Pair with the advertorial
  headline types (confession, "I wish I knew sooner", "before you…", "everyone's hyping") from the copy bank.
- **Layout:** photo + headline styled like a feature story; brand's own masthead.
- **Never:** imitate a real publication's masthead, byline or URL; fake "INSIDER" news brands; invented doctors.

## 6. E — Native and lo-fi

### E1. Native social post (text post)
- **Use when:** you want the feed's own grammar — Pellegrini's under-used quick win: comments give you the customer's exact
  words, engagement lowers CPM, good buyers come in. Illustrative hit rate 25%. Any awareness; best for problem-aware stories.
- **Layout:** a real post from the brand/founder page rendered at 1:1 (or a plain text post), 3 lines, no design, no logo bar.
- **On-image copy (Pellegrini's prompt, adapted):** "Write 10 native Facebook posts of 3 lines in the plain voice of [the
  founder / a team member / a real customer whose review we have permission to use], describing [pain] in their words. Zero
  advertising words. End with a question that invites a comment."
- **Never:** invent a customer, fake a real person's profile or verified badge, or seed comments from fake profiles (a tip in
  Pellegrini's deck that violates Meta's rules and the doctrine — do not use it).

### E2. Tweet / reply-bubble / comment reply
- **Use when:** answering a real question or objection publicly; "TikTok response bubble" and "TikTok made me buy it" are Denney
  formats. Mid/bottom funnel.
- **Layout:** the brand's own post/tweet card, or a real comment (with permission, name blurred if needed) in a reply bubble
  over a product photo answering it.
- **On-image copy:** the question verbatim; the answer in ≤ 15 words.

### E3. Post-it / handwritten note
- **Use when:** problem aware; intimacy, low intensity (COZY/FATIGUE). Denney format and iteration: "turn the best-performing
  script into a post-it-note ad" (also works as a video hook).
- **Layout:** a real sticky note on a fridge/mirror/laptop/dog crate, handwritten, 8-20 words; product in soft focus or absent.
- **On-image copy:** first person, the side's signature line ("Stop buying beds he won't sleep on").

### E4. Founder note
- **Use when:** trust and story; founder ads are "the most consistently good ads" (Blueprint live calls) — the static version
  is a founder photo + a short handwritten or typed note on why the product exists. Unaware → product aware.
- **Layout:** founder with the product in the real workplace (1-of-1 content nobody else can copy), note card overlay.
- **On-image copy:** one true origin line + one promise; signature.

### E5. Chat or notes-app screenshot
- **Use when:** recommendation from someone trusted is the trigger ("a friend told me about…" — advertorial swipes).
- **Layout:** iMessage/WhatsApp/notes UI with 3-5 short bubbles.
- **Watch-outs:** use a real exchange shared with permission, or make it obviously the brand speaking; never present a scripted
  conversation as a real customer's chat.

### E6. Ugly static
- Deliberately crude (MS-Paint arrows, phone photo, Comic Sans). Status: weak in 2026 per Blueprint; still hits for some in
  Pellegrini's table. Build only if the account's own hit-rate log supports it (see §1).

### E7. Meme
- **Use when:** the side has strong in-group identity (gym, gaming, parenting, dog owners) and the joke is about their moment.
  Blueprint names "a meme" as a template.
- **Layout:** a meme structure recreated with your own photography or licensed images; one joke, product present.
- **Watch-outs:** copyrighted images and celebrity faces need rights; humour must not punch down; test small, judge by spend.

## 7. F — Product and identity

### F1. Headline ad (bold statement)
- **Use when:** one strong line does the job; starter-round staple (Denney). Any awareness.
- **Layout:** big headline on brand colour or a bright stopping colour (Denney: bright colours stop the scroll), small product
  in the corner.
- **On-image copy:** one claim with a number or a named moment; nothing else but logo.

### F2. Product hero / texture macro
- **Use when:** the product's look/texture sells (beauty, food, apparel, materials). Pellegrini: product/texture close-up is a
  top-retention footage pattern; Denney: "up-close texture was super effective" on a winning stat ad.
- **Layout:** macro of the material doing its job (foam springing back, cream texture, fabric stretch); hands in frame
  (hands demo is the best-retaining pattern in Pellegrini's grid).
- **Copy:** one sensory line ("Springs back. Every morning.").

### F3. Identity scene (lifestyle + product)
- **Use when:** identity and aspiration decide the purchase (fashion "sells a vibe and a feeling" — Blueprint live calls);
  mid-funnel "lifestyle + product" in telehealth (Blanchard).
- **Layout:** the side in their world, the product in use; scenery, people and aesthetic must match the identity they buy into
  ("selling a luxury item in a trailer park" fails — Evolve). Include an emotional element where true: child, partner, the dog,
  others' gaze, safety (Pellegrini's highest-ROAS pattern).
- **Copy:** an actual/ideal/ought line (psychology toolkit §7).
- **Casting is targeting:** the person you show shapes who the ad is shown to (Blueprint live calls) — cast the side.

### F4. UGC haul / flat lay
- **Use when:** bundles, kits, multi-SKU, gifting, "what's in the box"; AOV angles. Named Superscale/Blueprint format, saved as a
  reusable prompt with a reference image.
- **Layout:** top-down shot of everything as if a customer just unpacked it on a bed/table, phone-camera look, hands optional,
  price tags or "included" labels.
- **Copy:** "Everything in the [bundle name]" + value total vs price (real values only).

### F5. Still from a winning video
- **Use when:** a video is winning — make statics from it (Blueprint live calls): the best frame + the video's winning hook as
  the main line. Cheap, already proven message, new format = new reach.
- **Layout:** full-bleed frame, hook line in L1 band, product visible.

## 8. G — Offer, trigger, retention

### G1. Offer stack / bundle
- **Use when:** most aware, or AOV is the lever. Offer = value exchange + timing + risk profile (Blueprint).
- **Layout:** product family shot, price strike, what's included as a stack, guarantee badge, delivery promise.
- **On-image copy:** a bundle needs a reason and a name ("people have two knees"; "one for upstairs, one for down"; Evolve:
  "Stylist Bundle — Hair Dryer + 2 Nozzles & Travel Bag | Regular $200 now $169.99"). Value-add offers (buy 2 get 1) beat
  discounts and can run all year (Blueprint live calls); keep offer-led creative to ~20-30% of the mix.
- **Watch-outs:** prices and values must be real; health/telehealth: no price or discount mentions in ads (Blanchard).

### G2. Quadruple Offer (BFCM)
- **Use when:** Black Friday / big sale; the buyer wants to win, not save (Pellegrini). Layout and rules in
  `angle-matrix-and-prompts.md` §8: visible strike price (€99 → €59) + free product (BOGO or full kit) + mystery gift or voucher
  + free shipping, bonus code optional; visible, credible limit.

### G3. Trigger statics
- **Use when:** a real event creates a reason to act — back in stock, discount, low stock, sale ending, price drop (Blueprint
  live calls: supporting ads). Most aware; retargeting/retention reach.
- **Layout:** product hero + one big status line ("Back in stock — sold out 8× this year" only if true) + date.
- **Watch-outs:** only true scarcity and real deadlines; take the ad down when the event ends.

### G4. Offer banner on a proven ad
- **Use when:** a sale starts — "put sale banners over existing best ads, and introduce some simple product image ads that
  mention the sale. Keep it simple" (Evolve). Fastest promo creative because the message is proven.
- **Layout:** the winning static unchanged + a banner strip (top under the safe zone, or bottom above it) with the offer and end
  date. Run in the separate sale campaign, as new ads (never edit the live winner).

### G5. Retention statics (existing customers)
- **Use when:** the retention campaign — new arrivals, bestsellers, restock alerts/"restocking event", catalogue of similar
  products, cross-sell of the hero's companion, themed/seasonal drops (Blueprint live calls).
- **Copy:** plain — "Hey, it's the August restocking event. People are simple." No persuasion needed; they already bought.

## 9. H — Carousels

Carousels count as their own template (a new concept when a side moves into one). Study winners card by card: "the order
and build of the frames is part of why it works" (Blueprint).

### H1. Story sequence
Card 1 pain moment hook → 2 hidden cause → 3 named mechanism → 4 proof → 5 offer/CTA. Each card readable alone; consistent
visual frame so the swipe feels continuous.

### H2. Listicle
"5 reasons [side] switch" — one reason per card, product in every card; last card the offer. Mirrors the advertorial listicle.

### H3. Best-of carousel
Put the account's top-performing statics into one carousel — "kind of like a super dynamic creative test" (Denney). A cheap
iteration of several winners; also good for retention.

### H4. Comparison or before/after carousel
Card pairs (them → us, or Day 1 → Day 30 where policy allows); or one criterion per card from A2.
