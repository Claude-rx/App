# Get Rid Of: buyer persona, root problem, root solution

Built on 16 Nov 2026 from 320 Amazon reviews of similar products (250 critical, 70 positive; full analysis in
`amazon-review-analysis.md`), the brand's own customer stories, and published sources (listed at the end).
The Amazon reviews are of other sellers' products. They tell us what this buyer has already lived through.
They are not reviews of Get Rid Of and are never shown as such.

## 1. Who buys

### Primary persona: "Third-Winter Karen" (the homeowner who already tried everything)

- **Age 40 to 68**, owns a house (often older, with a basement, crawl space or attached garage). Suburban or rural US.
- **Household:** partner, kids or grandkids visiting, a dog or a cat. Runs the home and buys the supplies.
- **Trigger:** droppings in the pantry or behind the stove, scratching in the wall at night, usually when the
  weather turns cold. "Getting cold outside so we'll see when the mice are looking for a cozy spot."
- **History:** has bought snap traps (19 of 45 mentions), peppermint spray or oil (10), and at least one bag of
  repellent pouches that did nothing. Refuses poison because of the pets.
- **Tech:** buys on a phone from a Facebook or in-app ad. Reads reviews first, the 3-star ones most.

### Secondary persona: "Storage Steve" (RV, camper, truck, cabin, shed)

- **Age 50 to 72**, owns something that sits closed for months. The stakes are a wiring harness, not a cereal box:
  "chewed up one of the engine wiring harness's at a cost of almost $600".
- Buys in fall, before storage. The happiest buyers in the whole dataset are this group: "No sign of rodents
  either place at the end of winter." They reorder every year (9 of 50 real positive reviews are repeat buyers).

### Anti-persona (do not sell to them; say so on the page)

- Groundhogs, moles, chipmunks, squirrels, raccoons in a yard: 14% of all critical reviews. It is for mice, in
  enclosed spaces.
- Someone with a heavy, established infestation who wants one product to end it by Friday.
- Open-air use: patios, gardens, deer blinds, a 30 by 40 ft open shop.

## 3. What they want (the real desire)

Not "a repellent". They want **the house back, without killing anything and without thinking about it again**:

1. **Prevention, not a body count.** "I really want to prevent rather than trap."
2. **Proof it is doing something.** The single buyer who counted droppings (25-30 a day down to 0-2) is the only
   one who sounds sure.
3. **To be told how many and where.** "Maybe I need to add more?" Reported use runs from one pouch for a whole
   house to a full pack in nine square feet.
4. **An honest answer about mice already inside.** The most-upvoted review in the dataset (108 helpful votes)
   is "Just don't expect a full-blown rodent eviction".
5. **Months of protection for the money.** "I did not think a month was my idea of rodent control."
6. **Time to judge.** Three buyers hit the 30-day return window before they knew.

## 5. The root problem

The mouse is not the problem. The **way in** is.

1. **A gap the size of a dime** is enough (Penn State Extension).
2. **Mice run on smell.** They have poor eyesight and rely on smell, touch and pheromones (NC State Extension).
   As they travel they leave urine and scent marks along baseboards and walls (University of Arizona Extension).
3. **They multiply faster than you trap them.** A female can have 5 to 10 litters a year and is mature at 6 to 8
   weeks (NC State Extension).
4. So a trap or a bait block removes **a** mouse and leaves the gap, the marked route and the food where they were.

Why other repellents fail on top of that: one or two oils, ten to twelve sachets for a whole house, dropped in
the middle of a room instead of at the gap, no plan for the mice already nesting inside, and no way to tell if
anything changed. Buyers end up judging by their own nose, which gets used to any smell within minutes.

## 6. The root solution

**Make the way in smell wrong, at the point where it starts, with enough pouches, for a whole season, and
deal with the mice already inside separately.**

| Root cause | What Get Rid Of does about it |
|---|---|
| The gap and the marked route | A pouch at each entry point and along the run (under the sink, door edges, garage track, pipe holes), not in the middle of the room |
| One weak smell | Four plant oils: peppermint, cinnamon, cedarwood, in a castor-oil base |
| Too few, wrong place | 12 pouches a pack, a room-by-room Placement Guide, and a house-size plan (the quiz after checkout) |
| Fades in days | Slow-release castor base, made to last up to 90 days per pouch; replace on a schedule, not by sniffing |
| Mice already inside | An honest 7-day plan: traps for the residents, pouches at the entries, steel wool in the gaps (CDC: seal up, trap up, clean up) |
| "How do I know?" | The dropping count: day 0, day 7, day 14 |
| Poison risk | No rodenticide. All four oils are on the EPA minimum-risk (25(b)) ingredient list |
| 30-day return windows | 90-day money-back guarantee |

### What we do not claim

- Not "100% working", not "kills", not "clears an infestation in 48 hours" in our own voice.
- Not the best in the world. We say what is different and let the guarantee carry the risk.
- Not for groundhogs, squirrels or open yards.
- "Up to 90 days" and "about 65 sq ft" are the brand's label figures; the page says replace sooner in hot or
  drafty spots (engine bays, garages).

## 7. Why honesty is the persuasion here

Buyers of this category reward the honest reviewer and punish the listing. So the page uses, on purpose:

| Principle | Where it is used |
|---|---|
| Pratfall effect | Reason 7: "A repellent alone will not evict a nest. Here is the plan that does." |
| Loss aversion | Intro and Reason 1: 5 to 10 litters a year; every week of waiting is another litter |
| Authority | "What the research says": Penn State, NC State, CDC, EPA, FTC, each linked |
| Social proof | The brand's customer stories and customer photos; the dropping-count review |
| Commitment and consistency | The 14-day dropping check; the house-size quiz after checkout |
| Regret aversion | 90 days to decide, against a 30-day return window |
| Contrast and anchoring | Exterminator visit and a $600 wiring harness against a pack of pouches |
| Unity | "You are not dirty and you are not unlucky. You were sold the wrong fix." |
| Hick's law | One call to action; pack choice is made on the checkout |
| Scarcity (real only) | The Early Black Friday offer and its real end date; no fake stock counters |

## 8. Voice

- **Tone:** direct, plain, a little angry on the reader's behalf. Short sentences.
- **Words that work:** "the way in", "at the gap", "no dead mice in the walls", "prevent", "count the droppings",
  "90 days to decide".
- **Words to avoid:** "eliminate", "exterminate", "100%", "instantly", "miracle", "all pests".
- **One line that stops the scroll:** "Still hearing them in the walls? The mouse isn't the problem. The way in is."
