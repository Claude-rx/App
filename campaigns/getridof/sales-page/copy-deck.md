## 07 · Explain the Problem Mechanism

### Why mice keep coming back: the Scent Trail Cycle

Mice barely use their eyes to get around your home. They follow their noses.

Every mouse that gets in leaves a trail of pheromones and urine behind it, an invisible highway from the gap under your door to the food in your pantry. Every new mouse picks up that trail and follows it straight in.

So when a trap catches one mouse, the highway stays open. The next mouse arrives, follows the same trail, and the cycle starts over. That's why "it worked for a week" is the most common story with traps.

To stop mice for good, you have to break the trail at the entry points, not just catch whichever mouse walks down it.
