THE ROOT CAUSE

The Slow Door: insulin knocks after every meal, and with age your cells answer more slowly.

After you eat, glucose from your meal enters your blood. Insulin then knocks on the doors of your muscle and liver cells to let it in, where it is burned or stored. When the cells answer quickly, the level comes back down smoothly.

Over the years, with less muscle, long hours of sitting, short sleep and stress, the cells answer more slowly. The body makes up for it by knocking louder, releasing more insulin, and for a long time the fasting number still looks fine. Then it starts to drift, a little higher at each checkup. Muscle is the biggest destination for glucose after a meal, so the hours you sit through after lunch and dinner are when the Slow Door shows most.

If anything new, persistent or worrying comes with it, see your doctor before any supplement.

What you are probably thinking, answered straight

These are the real doubts of people who buy berberine, from 389 Amazon reviews of two big brands, 200 of them critical. Some are fair. We answer them anyway.

Will it wreck my stomach?

It can, and we will not pretend otherwise. Stomach and gut effects are the commonest side effects of berberine: about a third of patients in one 3-month study had them. Take it with meals as the label says, start at one capsule for a week if you are sensitive, and stop if it does not settle. If it does not agree with you, the guarantee refunds your first bottle.

How much berberine is actually in it?

About 73 mg of berberine HCl per serving: 790 mg of extract at 8% plus 10 mg at 97%. We print the math so you can judge the real number, not a front-label headline.

Is it safe with my medication?

Maybe not, and that comes first. If you take blood sugar medicine including metformin, a blood thinner, cyclosporine or any prescription, ask your doctor before the first capsule. Berberine can add to some medicines and slow how the liver clears others. Never stop or change a prescribed medicine on your own.

Isn't berberine just internet hype?

The hype is new; the research is not. An umbrella review pooled 11 meta-analyses of randomized trials, and its authors also said the quality needs to improve. We show you both halves, and we never compare a capsule to a prescription drug.
