How to use it

Four sweeps, about 3 minutes

On clean, dry skin, in the morning, before your skincare and foundation.

  1. NeckSweep down from under the ear to the collarbone.
  2. Jaw and cheeksSweep from your chin along the jaw toward the ear.
  3. Under the eyesSweep out from the nose to the temples, very lightly.
  4. Neck againFinish with a few sweeps down the neck.

Repeat each sweep about 10 times, then do the other side. Keep it clean: a quick sweep on a cloth with a brush cleaner or 70% alcohol before each use, and a gentle wash of the tips once a month.

Gua sha needs oil and pressure; a roller covers one narrow line at a time. The brush gives the light, wide sweeps that a facial massage for puffiness uses.

Questions

When will I see a difference?

You may see a fresher, less puffy look right after your first 3 minutes. It’s temporary, so it works best every morning.

Does it work on mature skin?

Yes. The bristles bend instead of pulling, so they don’t drag thinner skin or fine lines. Keep the touch light, especially under the eyes.

Why is it called the Lymphatic Brush?

It’s named for the light lymphatic-massage strokes it’s designed for: the sweeps an aesthetician uses on a puffy morning face. It’s a cosmetic massage brush; we make no medical claim, and we don’t claim it drains anything.

Do I need oil or serum?

No. Use it on clean, dry skin. You can apply your skincare and your foundation right after.

Is it safe for sensitive skin?

The bristles are soft and the pressure stays light, so it’s gentle on most skin types. Avoid using it on irritated or broken skin, sunburn or active breakouts. If swelling doesn’t go away during the day, or you have a medical condition, check with your doctor first.
