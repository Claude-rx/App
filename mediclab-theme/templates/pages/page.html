The root cause

The Slow Door: insulin knocks after every meal, and with age your cells answer more slowly.

After you eat, glucose from your meal enters your blood. Insulin then knocks on the doors of your muscle and liver cells to let it in, where it is burned or stored. When the cells answer quickly, the level comes back down smoothly.

Over the years, with less muscle, long hours of sitting, short sleep and stress, the cells answer more slowly. The body makes up for it by knocking louder, releasing more insulin, and for a long time the fasting number still looks fine. Then it starts to drift, a little higher at each checkup. Muscle is the biggest destination for glucose after a meal, so the hours you sit through after lunch and dinner are when the Slow Door shows most.
