## Implications for the page

- **Headline:** name the mechanism and the age ("shade-adapting", "after 45"), promise the look she wants ("fresh,
  even, like your skin") and the one-minute routine; never "matches every skin tone" or "look 35".
- **Mechanism (sophistication 4):** a named root cause ("your skin changed, your foundation didn't": three changes
  after 45) and a named solution ("one adaptive shade in a serum base": encapsulated pigments + serum + actives +
  soft-focus powders).
- **Proof she trusts:** real reviews with photos and ages; the actives on the label; the ratings count; a guarantee
  in plain words. No doctors, no studies, no "clinically proven".
- **Objection killers:** the guarantee next to every button; "one-time purchase, no refills unless you ask"; "no SPF,
  wear your sunscreen"; the fish-allergy note by the button.
- **Offer:** quantity breaks with gifts she can picture (the blender brush, the mystery gift, the Lymphatic Brush),
  the $59 list price as the honest anchor, free shipping.
- **Urgency:** true only: this price and these gifts are only on this page (the store sells one bottle for $47).

## Claims rules the pages follow

Cosmetic claims only ("blurs the look of fine lines", "evens the look of skin tone"). Never: "matches every / all /
99 % of skin tones", any SPF or sun-protection claim, "reverses aging", "produces collagen", study percentages,
dermatologist or expert endorsements, scarcity counts, countdowns that reset, reviews of another product presented as
this one's. "Doesn't settle" stays hedged ("designed not to", "made not to") unless a buyer says it in her own review.
Refills are opt-in only, never pre-checked. PDRN is salmon-derived: the fish-allergy note sits by every button.
