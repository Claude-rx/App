# Andromeda 2 Video Scripts

Produces video ad scripts that speak to one side of one person in one recognisable moment: researched first, then written
beat by beat as a timed table (voiceover, captions, shots, editor notes) with 3-5 alternate hooks, a creator brief and a
scorecard. Andromeda 2 reads the whole video ("every phrase") and scores it against the viewer's current room, so a
specific moment, a named mechanism and sound-off, native footage win retrieval; hook rate and hold rate decide whether the
ad is watched long enough to sell.

## Before you start

Read `references/andromeda2-doctrine.md` every time (R1-R4 and the scorecard drive every script). From
`references/psychology-toolkit.md` read §1-2 (research, side card), §3-5 (desire, mechanism, proof), §6-9 (awareness,
self-concept, Mood × Intensity, dimensionalising pain) and §12 (compliance). Open `references/metrics-and-diagnosis.md` §4
only when the user brings performance data for an existing video.

Gather, asking only for what is missing and cannot be inferred:
- Product, price/AOV, the real offer and guarantee, the landing page the ad sends to.
- Who it is for: an existing side card (A01…) or raw research — reviews, comments, support tickets, Reddit, competitor ads.
- Proof that exists: review count and rating, customer numbers, real studies, named experts, demos, customer stories.
- Category risk: health, supplements, weight, skin, telehealth, finance → strict compliance.
- Production reality: real customers willing to film, creators, founder on camera, AI avatars, an editor.
- What is live and winning, so the new script answers a different question (and its hook/hold/CTR if known).
- Format and length wanted; if none, choose in step 4.

When the user gives little, proceed: build the side card from the product page and reviews, mark assumptions
`[ASSUMPTION]` and every unverified number `[PLACEHOLDER — replace with real, provable data]`. Never invent studies,
doctors, publications, review counts, customers or results presented as real.

## Workflow

1. **Research the side, then name the moment.** Fill the side card (toolkit §2) and collect verbatim phrases and
   golden-nugget reviews (Denney: a review that "reveals a little too much" can carry a whole ad). Then write the moment:
   place, time, what they just did, app and placement, sound on or off, the trigger ("the curb on the 6:40 p.m. walk", "the
   bathroom mirror at 7 a.m."). Why: retrieval routes ads to expert views of a person in a context; "vet approved, free
   shipping" has thousands of AI twins, "the guy whose retriever hesitates at the curb" has none (Pawliw; doctrine R1-R3).

2. **Write the kindergarten pitch and the mechanism.** Seven one-liners (Kell): Problem → Emotional problem (and "what's
   even worse than that?") → Root problem (UMP) → Root solution (UMS) → Ingredients/techniques → Physical wins → Emotional
   wins. Then two sentences: "[Problem] isn't caused by [belief]; it's [UMP] — [everyday analogy]. [Product] delivers
   [UMS], so you can [moment regained]." It must pass the link test — A causes B, so C solves B, obvious in 2 seconds
   (Pellegrini). If a founder or formulator exists, ask Kell's four questions first: the core problem, the real reason it
   couldn't be solved, the mechanism that overcame it, how the product delivers it (Kell calls assuming you know "how it works"
   one of the most common mistakes of his career). Why: "why would it work this time?" is the question every tried-and-failed buyer brings.

3. **Set the psychology of this one script.** Pick one awareness stage, one self-concept (actual/ideal/ought), one Mood ×
   Intensity zone, one selling lever (offer, mechanism or education, multiplied by the avatar) and 1-2 core desires, and
   write them in the header. Keep them coherent: FATIGUE suits problem-aware; PANIC on product-aware is a red flag.
   Why: Pellegrini's audit finds losing scripts have none of these set — "a script any brand could sign".

4. **Choose format and length.** Concept = side + template (Blueprint); label the script `concept` or `iteration` against
   what is live. Use the catalogue in `references/script-formats.md` (beat sheets, timings, awareness and lever fit).
   Defaults:
   - Cold first-touch: problem-solution or UGC testimonial, 30-60 s (Moonlighters' highest-delivery first-touch ads:
     52.8% video, 55.2% problem-solution, median 34 s, range 8 s-1:45, Blueprint).
   - Higher ticket or a mechanism that needs a story: 90 s-3 min mini-VSL; $1,000+ products: long form leads (Blueprint).
   - Low ticket: several short ads (15-30 s), not one long one.
   - Product-aware and retargeting: testimonial montage, objection-killing 1-of-1 content, offer clip.
   - Jaded market (sophistication 5): founder story, indirect lead, story-first formats.
   Word budget at ~2.9 spoken words per second (measured on proven VSLs): 15 s ≈ 40-45 words, 30 s ≈ 85, 60 s ≈ 170,
   90 s ≈ 260. Over budget means cutting an idea, not talking faster.

5. **Build the emotional raw material before writing lines** (`references/emotion-and-retention-devices.md`):
   - Pain as one sensory moment: place + physical action + object in hand + body sensation + what others see or say + the
     inner thought in quotes; dimensionalised physical → life → emotional, in their words.
   - Dream outcome as 2-3 concrete scenes; translate each feature with "What that REALLY means is…" (Kell/Mindvalley).
   - Future pacing close in time — "tonight", "this Saturday", the first use (Pellegrini: the brain treats "you in 3
     months" as a stranger).
   - One identity line for the chosen self-concept; one real proof line ready for every claim.
   Why: depth is what Andromeda can read and look-alike AI ads cannot copy, and every scene becomes a shot.

6. **Write the body on the format's beat sheet.** One idea per script (Kell: "laser focused on 1 idea"); grade 5-6
   reading level (Kell FK < 7, Oliver < 6th grade); short spoken lines. Then:
   - Open a loop in the hook and pay it off at ~60-70% of runtime; open the next loop before closing the last
     (Blueprint: "open loop A. Before loop A closes, loop B opens").
   - Proof right after each new claim (Oliver: dream outcome, mechanism, benefits).
   - Desire, not feature: the payoff is the regained moment, shown happening on screen (Blueprint "payoff").
   - The reveal must over-deliver the hook — Pellegrini's 522-day winner changed only the tension; the loser's "reveal
     disappoints".
   - CTA = offer (value + timing + risk) in one line for ads under 60 s; price ladders, bonuses and FAQs live on the page.

7. **Write 3-5 hooks for the body** (`references/hook-bank.md`). Each hook has three layers: micro-hook (0-1 s pattern
   disrupt), spoken + on-screen hook (2-3 sentences max, Blueprint), visual hook. Each alternative comes from a different
   category (pain moment, myth-bust, golden-nugget quote, demo, call-out, skeptic…), never a reword. The first line or
   frame signals the side; no credentials, logo or brand slate first. Why: hooks are the cheapest high-leverage
   iteration — Meta treats a new hook on the same body as a different ad (Blueprint live calls), Oliver tests 3-5 per body
   because "a VSL can be turned from a loser to a winner with the right hook", and Kell counts 1 winning hook in 20 as "a
   big win".

8. **Direct the picture and the sound.** For each line write the shot, its footage pattern and an editor note. Give
   editors Pellegrini's rules verbatim: never more than 3 s on one shot; movement in every shot; one footage per
   statement ("if the voice says Marc bends down, show Marc bending down"). Front-load the patterns that held on his
   accounts — hands demo, texture close-up, no face before the action, side-by-side, POV, an emotional element, cuts every
   1-3 s (table in script-formats §6). Match footage to the line's emotion: dark, close, slow for pain; bright, moving,
   human after the product appears; never smiling stock under a pain line (Oliver). Design for sound off: burned-in
   captions that match the audio exactly, large, few words per line (Kell), an on-screen headline that carries the hook
   muted, big text on key numbers. Licensed music only (a Blueprint client was fined $5,000 for trending audio). Deliver
   9:16 and 1:1 with the action centre-safe so the crop works.

9. **Write the creator brief and production notes** (script-formats §7-8). Internal side: concept, template/inspiration,
   the side's wants and needs, the call-outs to land. Creator side: hooks, must-say call-outs, shot list, specs. Don't
   over-brief — pick creators for tonality and let them talk; 3+ hook takes and raw clips turn one shoot into 20-30 assets
   (Blueprint). Casting is targeting: Andromeda shows an ad more to people who resemble the person in it (Blueprint), so
   cast the side realistically and attainably (Kell: 40+ buyers "HATE seeing brands using younger models"); compilations
   widen reach. Real customers and founders first (founder ads are "the most consistently good", Blueprint). AI avatars
   only at high quality, never presented as a real customer, disclosed where the law requires (New York). AI product
   accuracy: exact packaging images, stated dimensions, product fixed in place, no orbit shots on hard shapes.

10. **Compliance pass** — toolkit §12 plus video specifics: no second-person personal-attribute claims ("Are you
    overweight?") — use first-person story, third person or the situation; no zooming on body parts; no side-by-side
    before/after for health, weight, aging or cosmetic results; supplements say "supports/helps" and describe the
    experience and the mechanism, never a cure; no disease names; no fake scarcity, countdowns or "back in stock"; real
    testimonials only (an actor reading a customer's words is a dramatisation and is labelled; never invent the "regular
    guy" story); street-interview subjects are real with releases. Kell's swiped leads contradict Kell's own compliance
    deck — ship the brand-safe version every time: lead with the fascinating benefit and the end outcome, not the shameful
    negative (Kell on Snow).

11. **Score and fix** with the quality bar below; ship at 15+/20 with compliance at 2.

12. **Deliver.** Save to `ads/<product-slug>/video-scripts-<YYYY-MM-DD>.md` (relative to the working directory) unless the
    user names a path; summarise in chat: the concept(s), the strongest hook, the score, the placeholders to fill. Several
    scripts in one request should differ by side or template (several concepts), each with its own 3-5 hooks.

**Rewrite mode** (an existing video with data): run `scripts/a2.py analyze` or follow metrics §4 and fix only the first weak
stage — weak hook rate → new hooks, same body; weak hold rate → new lead (agitate, myth-bust, story from the climax) and
faster cuts; weak CTR with good watch metrics → rewrite desire, mechanism, proof and offer (Oliver's ladder). Planning a
whole batch of iterations from winners belongs to `andromeda2-creative-iteration`.

## Quality bar

Score the doctrine scorecard (`references/andromeda2-doctrine.md` §5, 0/1/2 per line): ship at 15+, rework at 11-14,
kill the idea under 11; any 0 on compliance blocks shipping. Then check:
- **Pellegrini's five questions** answered in the header: who, Mood × Intensity, awareness, self-concept, how we prove.
- **Hook**: stops the scroll muted in 0-2 s; the side is signalled in the first line or frame; three layers congruent;
  3-5 alternates from different categories.
- **Depth**: one sensory pain moment; dream outcome as scenes; future pacing close in time; one identity line.
- **Mechanism**: UMP and UMS named, linked, one analogy, one visual.
- **Proof**: after every claim; numbers, names and timeframes real or flagged as placeholders.
- **Structure**: one idea; within the word budget; grade 5-6; the hook's loop paid off at ~60-70%; the payoff shows the
  regained moment and over-delivers the hook; CTA = offer + timing + risk.
- **Picture**: every shot ≤ 3 s with movement; one footage per statement; footage matches the line's emotion; no talking
  head before the action in the hook.
- **Rooms**: reads complete with captions only; 9:16 + 1:1 safe; licensed music.
- **Production**: casting is the side, realistic and attainable; AI disclosed where required, never posing as a customer;
  product accurate in every frame.
- **Distinct intent**: labelled concept or iteration; answers a question no live ad answers.
