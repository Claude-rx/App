## 1. The research pass

Answer these for each side before writing (Pellegrini's 12 questions, extended with Pawliw's desires / experiences /
behaviours / emotions). Use the customer's own words — reviews, Reddit threads, comments, support tickets, competitor
comment sections, Amazon 1-3★ reviews of competing products, TikTok/YouTube comments.

1. **Pain moments of the day** — when, where, which gesture ("the knee that fires at 3 a.m.", "checking my temples in every shop window").
2. **What they believe** about the problem and about what works ("it's just age").
3. **Deep hope / unspoken desire.**
4. **Dream outcome** — plus the time and effort they would accept.
5. **What they tried** — liked and disliked, and why it failed.
6. **Horror stories** — worst moments, the embarrassing ones.
7. **Who they blame** — the named external enemy (supplements that promise and do nothing, the vet who shrugs…).
8. **Why it used to be less of a problem.**
9. **Fear at 1 year and 5 years** if nothing changes (often a hidden fear: "not being able to carry my grandson" → wheelchair).
10. **Why they would believe it this time** (what a mechanism must explain).
11. **Awareness stage** (section 6).
12. **Self-concept** — actual / ideal / ought (section 7).

Plus: the exact phrases they use ("I've tried everything, nothing works"), the objections, the buying triggers
(the moment they finally search for a fix), and the behaviours that betray the side (what they do, buy, watch).

## 2. The side card

One card per side. A side is not an age bracket: the 26-year-old man and the 68-year-old grandmother whose dog hesitates at
the curb share a side. Name sides `A01`, `A02`… and keep a living library.

```
A0X — <short memorable name, e.g. "Marc, the ex-marathoner who won't accept 'it's age'">
Moment(s): <when/where the side comes out, what they just did, device/placement guess>
Situation: <life context that makes it matter now>
Pain in their words: "<verbatim phrase>"
Belief about the problem: "<…>"
Tried & failed: <products/methods + why they failed>
Enemy: <named>
Desire (the moment they want back): <concrete scene>
Dream outcome + acceptable effort/time: <…>
Fear if nothing changes (1y / 5y): <…>
Self-concept: actual "<…>" / ideal "<…>" / ought "<…>"
Awareness: unaware | problem | solution | product | most aware
Best zone (section 8): COZY | JOY | FATIGUE | PANIC
Signature line: "<the sentence they would say>"
```

Worked example (Pellegrini): **Marc, 65.** Pain — the knee that fires at 3 a.m. Belief — "it's part of age". Enemy —
supplements that promise and do nothing. Fear at 5 years — not being able to carry his grandson (unconscious: wheelchair).
Hidden desire — ride his bike again, dance with his wife. Self-concept — actual "I'm still active", ideal "back on the
bike", ought "I must stay independent". Signature line — "I've tried everything, nothing works." Retired, still active,
ex-marathoner, tried 4 supplements in 2 years.

One audience holds many sides (Pellegrini's hair-loss example, the same morning): Mark 35 (married, 2 kids, receding
front for 6 months, tired not panicked), Tom 28 (single, gym 5×/week, vanity, checks his temples in every mirror), David 45
(father of 3 teens, dramatic loss in 12 months, does not understand why), Steve 50 (married 25 years, life is good, buys
what serves him), Brian 32 (saturated: saw 47 hair-loss ads this week, anti-ad instinct). To reach all five you need five
psychologically distinct ads.

## 3. Desire: sell the moment, not the feature

People buy the moments the problem steals from them. Convert every feature into a life scene.

| Feature | Desire (the moment regained) |
|---|---|
| "Relieves joint pain" | Bending down to tie your grandson's laces without wincing · golf all weekend without ending on the couch Monday · sleeping through the night without the 3 a.m. knee · gardening two hours straight |

Formula: *[product] so you can [concrete action] [in a concrete place/time] without [the old pain/compromise].*
Future-self caution: the brain treats "you in 3 months" as a stranger. Shrink the distance ("this afternoon", "tonight"),
block the "tomorrow" escape (2-minute quiz, one-click add to cart), and show the product solving today's problem.
"Sell to the person who is tired, busy and hurting now."

## 4. Mechanism: name the cause, name the solution

A mechanism answers "why would it work this time?" — the question every tried-and-failed buyer asks.

- **UMP (problem mechanism):** the real root cause, named, and the reason nothing else worked.
  Not "joint pain" but "**Joint Drought**: the 3 layers of cartilage dehydrate".
- **UMS (solution mechanism):** the unique way the product fixes that cause, named.
  Not "anti-inflammatory" but "**Triple-Zone Rehydration**: rehydrates the 3 layers in 14 days".
- **Link rule:** A causes B, so C solves B — obvious in two seconds. Keep both simple; a mechanism that needs a diagram
  in a 30-second ad is too complex and will not be believed.
- Sales-message formula: *"Your [problem] isn't caused by [common belief]; it's caused by [UMP]. [UMS] fixes [UMP], and
  [product] delivers [UMS], so you can [desire]."*
- Use a vivid metaphor or analogy so they see and feel it (poison → antidote; "the weight of your upper body crushes the
  little nerves in your lower back").
- Test a mechanism like an angle: static first, then video (Pellegrini: Frøya Organics "arctic autophagy" ran 485 days as a
  static and 569 days as a video).
- Truth: the cause must be real or a fair simplification of a real one, and the solution must actually do it.

## 5. Proof that anchors

Floating claims ("100% natural", "expert approved", "fast") are ignored. Anchor with:
1. **Precise numbers** — 7 days, 2,847 people, 83% satisfaction. Never "fast": always "xx days".
2. **Names** — Dr. Bernard Martin; University of Grenoble study. Never "experts".
3. **Timeframes** — visible results on day 7, peak at day 30. A dated curve, not "quickly".
The bigger the promise, the more specific the proof. Put proof right after every new claim (dream outcome, mechanism,
benefit). Proof types: testimonials in the customer's words, numbers of customers, studies, named authorities,
demonstrations (the egg that doesn't break, the sauce that slides off), before/after (policy-safe), press, guarantees.

## 6. Awareness and sophistication

**Awareness (Schwartz)** decides how direct the ad can be.

| Stage | They know | Open with | Example opening |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unaware | Not the problem | A story, a surprising fact, an identity mirror | "Why do 40-year-olds wake at 3 a.m. more than teenagers?" |
| Problem aware | The problem, not solutions | The pain in their words, agitated; then the hidden cause | "Your knee doesn't hurt because of age." |
| Solution aware | Solutions exist, not yours | Why the usual solutions fail (UMP) → new mechanism | "Glucosamine can't reach dehydrated cartilage." |
| Product aware | Your brand | Proof, comparison, offer, objection killers | "2,847 reviews later: what people say on day 7." |
| Most aware | Ready | The deal, urgency, risk reversal | "BOGO ends at midnight — 60-day refund, keep the bottle." |

**Sophistication** decides what the claim must carry: 1 = a direct claim works; 2 = make it bigger and specific
("in 4 weeks"); 3 = add a solution mechanism (how); 4 = add a problem mechanism (a new root cause only you solve);
5 = the market is jaded — sell through story, identity and indirect leads; hide the pitch.
Gauge it from competitors: claims + product-first stories = stage 1-2; mechanisms in their ads = 3-5; Reddit full of
named products = stage 3-4.

## 7. Self-concept: actual, ideal, ought

People do not buy a product; they buy a version of themselves. Pick which version the ad speaks to, and when unsure test
all three as separate ads.

| Self | Who | Feeling | Line that fits | Example (hair loss) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Actual | Who I am today | Validation, comfort, pride, relief | "That's me." | "Your wife says your hair looks fine as it is. She's right. But imagine if it were even better." |
| Ideal | Who I want to become | Growth, hope, inspiration | "What if it were finally possible?" | "Get back the hairline you had at 25, without a transplant." |
| Ought | Who I think I should be | Duty, standards, guilt, pressure | "I really should…" | "You take care of your business, your family, your fitness. Time to take care of your hair too." |

## 8. Mood × intensity: the four emotional zones

Choose the opening emotion (mood +/−) and its volume (low/high). "Intensity buys attention, not the sale."

| | Low intensity | High intensity |
|---|---|---|
| **Mood +** | **COZY** — reassuring like a friend. "A friend told me about these tablets. Thicker hair after 3 months. I'm sticking with it." Fits Steve (stop shouting at me). | **JOY** — aspirational hype. "REGROW YOUR HAIRLINE. 10,000 MEN SAW THE DIFFERENCE IN 60 DAYS." Fits Tom (in shopping mode). |
| **Mood −** | **FATIGUE** — commiseration, "sick of X". "Sick of checking your temples in every shop window?" Fits Mark (wants to be understood without drama). | **PANIC** — fear urgency. "You lose 100 hairs a day. After 40 they don't grow back on their own." Fits David (needs a shock). |

Fit the zone to awareness: PANIC on a product-aware audience is a red flag (they need proof and a deal, not a shock);
JOY/most-aware and FATIGUE/problem-aware are natural pairs. Note the zone in every brief so tests are readable.

## 9. Dimensionalising pain (physical → life → emotional)

Make the problem real now, in three layers, using their vocabulary:
1. **Physical** — the simple symptom (back pain).
2. **Life consequence** — what it stops them doing (can't lift the grandkid, cancels golf, pays for treatments that don't work,
   told by specialists what they can't do).
3. **Emotional consequence** — what it makes them feel (trapped in their own body, old before their time, a burden,
   humiliated, invisible).
Then mirror it in the dream outcome: physical relief → the activity back → the feeling back (free, capable, proud, attractive).
Rule for video: match the footage to the emotion of each line (dark, close, slow for pain; bright, moving, human for the
outcome). Never use smiling stock footage under a pain line.

## 10. Angle generators

Ways to find new doors into the same product (each produces a new concept, not an iteration):
- **New side** of the same buyer (gym Tom vs dad David) or a new user of the same product (nurses vs mechanics vs people
  with obesity for the same insole).
- **New moment** (3 a.m. pain, Monday-morning stiffness, the wedding photo, the beach).
- **New sub-problem** under the main one (back pain → degenerative discs → sciatica).
- **New mechanism** or a new name for the cause.
- **New enemy** (the industry, the old method, a named ingredient).
- **New proof frame** (founder story, expert, customer count, comparison, demo).
- **New awareness stage** (same message told to the unaware via story, to the aware via offer).
- **New self-concept / zone** (actual vs ideal vs ought; COZY vs PANIC).
- **Sell the gap** when priced above the market: make the extra value explicit ($29 vs $19-23 → show the $10).
- **Offer angles** (bundle logic: "people have two knees"), gifting, seasonal moments.
- **Mine competitors** (long-running ads = proven angles; gaps = messages nobody covers) and **mine comments** on your own
  winners for the next angle.

## 11. Selling levers and quick frameworks

**Three ways to sell × the avatar multiplier** (Blueprint): every ad pulls at least one lever, aimed at one avatar.
- **Offer** = value exchange + timing + risk profile ("guaranteed Valentine's delivery; if she hates it, money back").
- **Mechanism** = the features and *how* it works — the causal explanation that makes the promise believable; give it a
  name ("the M3 method" beats "a three-step process").
- **Education** = "Oh… THAT's the real problem": a belief update that makes the product the logical conclusion.
- **Avatar** = "This is talking to me": identity + context + language. Seven ways to signal it — call it out by name; one
  product, many avatars; don't name it, imply it; name the symptom/situation ("crush your day without the 2 p.m. crash");
  name the behaviour ("why switch from pills to gummies" skips the convincing); the aspiration; the pain or ceiling.
  Self-test: can I say who this is for in one sentence, and does the first line/frame signal that person?

**Desire, identity, belief** (Evolve / Schwartz): desire decides the content (you channel existing desire, you can't create
it; strength = intensity × scope); identity decides the visuals (who they want to be seen as); belief decides how the ad is
built — via a Yes-train, symptoms they recognise, authority, or logic. Aware-level ads convert but rarely scale;
problem-aware and unaware ads scale.

**16 basic desires** (Reiss, via Denney) — pick the 1-2 your buyers act on: acceptance, curiosity, eating, family, honor,
idealism, independence, order, physical activity, power, romance, saving, social contact, social status, tranquility,
vengeance. Most used on Meta: acceptance, family, physical activity, power, romance, social contact, status, tranquility.

**Kindergarten-simple pitch** (Kell) — write these seven one-liners before any script or copy: Problem → Emotional problem →
Root problem (UMP) → Root solution (UMS) → Ingredients/techniques → Physical wins → Emotional wins. If you cannot explain the
product in two sentences (hidden cause + how it fixes it), the ad will not be understood. Write at a 5th-6th-grade level.

**Hook discovery score** (Evolve CRO) — score each customer pain statement 1-5 on Appeal, Exclusivity, Clarity, Motivation
(max 20); turn the winner into four hook forms: obvious-yes question, desired future state, first-person direct, if/then.

## 12. Compliance guardrails (Meta policy)

- No personal-attribute assertions in the second person: not "Are you overweight?" / "Your diabetes…"; say "For people who
  want…", tell a first-person story, or describe the situation.
- No unprovable health/medical cure claims; supplements describe the experience and the mechanism, not "cures X".
- Before/after: realistic, not idealised body-shaming; follow the category rules (weight loss, cosmetic procedures).
- Numbers, studies, names and reviews must be real and provable. Fictional placeholders in templates must be replaced
  with the brand's real data before launch.
- Special ad categories (credit, housing, employment, social issues) must be declared.
- AI people: disclose where the law requires it (e.g. New York); never present an AI person as a real customer.
- Health, weight-loss and telehealth offers are reviewed much harder. Avoid: before/after images, pounds-lost numbers,
  time-bound outcome promises ("lose 20 lbs in 30 days"), "miracle / breakthrough / secret", medication names in headlines,
  scales showing numbers, zoom-ins on body parts, all caps and "!!!", fake scarcity. Include "if medically appropriate /
  prescription required" language where it applies. Brand-safe framing (Kell, Snow): lead with the fascinating benefit and
  the end outcome rather than the shameful negative.
- Never fabricate authority: no fake "news" publications, no invented doctors or studies, no scripted fake comments, no
  static "ends today" timers that never end. Borrow the structure and emotional language of old advertorials, not their fakes.
