## 1. Angle families — the question each static answers

An angle is the door into the message: why this person stops, now (Pellegrini: "Pourquoi ça me concerne ?"). Each static must
answer one question for one side. Write the question in the viewer's voice before choosing a format.

| Angle family | The viewer's question it answers | Typical awareness | Lever (Blueprint) | Strong formats | Main-line starter |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pain moment | "Is that me at [time/place]?" | Unaware → problem | Avatar | B1, E1, E3, F3 | "[Time]. [Place]. [Gesture]. Again." |
| Desire / dream outcome | "What do I get back?" | Problem → product | Avatar | B1 (right half), F3, C1, F1 | "[Action] again. [When]." |
| Hidden cause (UMP) / education | "Wait — THAT's the real problem?" | Problem → solution | Education | B2, D1, D4, D6 | "It's not [belief]. It's [UMP]." |
| Solution mechanism (UMS) | "Why would this work when the others didn't?" | Solution | Mechanism | D1, D2, A1 | "[UMS]: [result] in [time]." |
| Enemy | "What let me down — and is this different?" | Solution | Education | A1, A2, B3, D4 | "Why your [category] [failed] by [time]." |
| Proof | "Did it work for someone like me?" | Product | Mechanism (belief) | C1-C5 | "'[verbatim]' — [name, role]" |
| Objection | "But what about [price / fit / effort / mess]?" | Product → most | Offer / mechanism | C6, A3, A2, E2 | "[Objection]? [5-word answer]." |
| Offer | "What do I get if I act now?" | Most aware | Offer | G1-G4 | "[Offer] · [reason] · [real deadline]" |
| Identity / self-concept | "Is this who I am / want to be / should be?" | Any | Avatar | F3, E4, F1, E1 | actual / ideal / ought line |
| Value gap | "Why pay more than [market price]?" | Solution → product | Offer + mechanism | A3, A2 | "$[x] per [use] vs [alternative]." |
| Trigger moment | "Why does this matter this week?" | Any | Offer (timing) | G3, F3, B1 | "[Season / life event]: [moment]." |

**Mood × Intensity is a modifier, not an angle** — every static gets a zone (COZY, JOY, FATIGUE, PANIC; psychology toolkit §8)
and the zone sets the look (§5). Fit zone to awareness: PANIC on a product-aware side is a red flag; FATIGUE pairs with
problem-aware, JOY with most-aware. **Self-concept** (actual / ideal / ought) is chosen per static; if unsure, make the same
message three times, one per self (Pellegrini: "teste-le toujours en 3 variations").

Seven ways to signal the side on an image (Blueprint): call it out by name; one product, many sides; imply it without naming;
name the symptom or situation ("the 2 p.m. crash"); name the behaviour ("why switch from pills to gummies" skips the
convincing); the aspiration; the pain or ceiling. Name a situation or behaviour, never a personal attribute (§10).

## 2. Building the matrix

1. **Rows = sides** (A01, A02… from the side cards). Two or three sides per batch is plenty; big brands run few avatars
   (Blueprint live calls: "one, max two" at small budgets).
2. **Columns = angle families** from §1 that the product can honestly support.
3. **Fill each cell with one line:** the viewer's question + the specific material that answers it (the moment, the verbatim
   phrase, the number, the mechanism name, the review). An empty cell = missing research, not a reason to write a generic ad.
4. **Mark each cell:** awareness · zone · self-concept · best 1-2 formats (picker in `static-formats.md` §1) · TOF or BOF.
5. **Cross out cells already answered by a live ad** (same side + same question) — those become iterations, not concepts.
6. **Pick the batch** with the balance rules in §3.

The advertorial swipes show the cheapest source of new concepts: keep the mechanism and offer, change the side + trigger moment +
shame scene (XStance re-skinned one page for ER nurse, diesel mechanic, dog walker, mom-teacher). For each cell you can also
vary: trigger moment · shame scene · enemy · mechanism metaphor · proof type · offer frame.

So do I." | "Will he even use it?" — [N]% use it night 1 | "Why two, why now?" — one upstairs, one down |

## 3. Balancing a batch · batch recipes

**Balance rules (check before writing a word of copy):**
- Every static answers a different question — for the viewer, not just in wording. A pack of 8 ads saying the same thing is one
  ad (doctrine R4). "Diversity of intent, not diversity of volume" (Superscale, Blueprint live calls).
- Same side + same angle in two formats only on purpose — to learn whether the angle or the format wins (Pellegrini: test the
  angle as a static, duplicate it as a video).
- Cover ≥ 2 awareness stages and ≥ 3 of the four levers (offer, mechanism, education, avatar).
- Use ≥ 2 zones per side; never PANIC for product-aware.
- Default mix for a general batch: about half top-of-funnel angle tests (pain, desire, cause, mechanism, enemy) and half
  bottom-of-funnel (proof, objection, offer). Statics often earn their keep at the bottom — "you might be pleasantly surprised…
  they might fall a little into the middle as well" (Blueprint live calls) — but top-of-funnel is where new reach comes from.
- Iterations vs concepts: iterations have a high hit rate and sustain; new concepts have a low hit rate ("it might take 100 ads
  to have a hit") but unlock spend — do both. Below target: "more, then new" (iterate what is above target first). At scale,
  most of a round is iterations (Blueprint live calls).
- Statics complement video; a statics-only account gives up "60% or 70% of impressions" (Blueprint live calls). If the placement
  breakdown shows Reels/Stories near 1% of spend, the gap is vertical creative — ship real 9:16 layouts.

**Batch recipes:**

| Goal | Recipe |
|---|---|
| New account / new pixel | 1 side, 1 message, 4 formats: us vs them, feature call-out, testimonial, headline ad (Denney rapid format test). Then message tests on the winning format. |
| Angle test (Pellegrini) | 1 side × 4-6 angle families in 1-2 proven formats; the winning angle gets a video twin. |
| Avatar expansion (Blueprint) | The winning template × 2-3 new sides = 2-3 new concepts. |
| Mechanism launch | D1 diagram + A1 us vs them + C2 stat for the solution-aware side; B2 root-cause split for the problem-aware side. |
| BOF objection engine | 1 side × top 5-10 objections × one recurring background (C6). Ready in a day. |
| Winner iteration | 1 concept × 3-6 iterations, one layer each (§7). New pack. |
| Promo (Evolve BFCM five) | Offer graphic · new product photo + offer · best image + offer banner · best video + offer banner · new video + offer. |
| Retention | New arrival · restock · bestseller · cross-sell · buy-again (§9). |

**Volume:** one new ad per week per $10k monthly spend, floor 2 (doctrine; `scripts/a2.py testing-plan`); Blueprint's "IV line" —
"if every account could just have five static images a week… you'll survive" (not a magic number). Release backlogs as weekly
packs, not all at once. Quality over count: "Just put 10 units of time into every creative instead of mass creative."

## 4. On-image copy — rules and pattern bank

**Rules**
1. **Main line ≤ ~7 words.** It carries the side and the hook. The image is read in about a second (Denney: hook ~1 s).
2. **Start with what they get or the moment they recognise** — not the brand. "When you start with what THEY get… they are more
   likely to stick" (Oliver). Brand name rarely in the main line; the category noun + "this" is the advertorial habit.
   Exception — health categories: name the product in the ad's headline field for transparency (Kell).
3. **Specific beats clever:** a time, a place, a number, a named mechanism, a verbatim phrase. "Simple, direct messaging beat
   clever/cute copy" (Blanchard). Every number real and provable.
4. **Support line ≤ ~15 words:** the proof, the cause → fix link, or the offer. Proof right after the claim.
5. **Sell the moment, not the spec** — *[product] so you can [action] [place/time] without [old pain]* (toolkit §3).
6. **Read it out loud** (Evolve) and cut every word that does not change meaning. Sentence case; no ALL CAPS, no "!!!".
7. **Customer words over brand words.** Pull from reviews, comments, Reddit; keep golden-nugget phrasing intact.
8. **No personal-attribute callouts** ("Overweight?", "Your acne"): use first person, the situation, the behaviour, or the object.
9. **Match the landing page** headline and offer (Oliver: a mismatch = high bounce).

**Pattern bank** (fill-ins; examples are illustrative). Evolve's 32 headline patterns (Schwartz) are the source of most of these.

"Read this before [habit] again" · "[N] reasons [side] swear by this" · stat badge "95% felt relief from day one" (only with
real data). Authority by role must be a real person; avoid "Doctor reveals" unless a real doctor said it on the record.

## 5. Visual direction

- **One focal subject.** Product or the person's moment, never both competing. CRO note from Evolve: "Seeing 2 things in the
  picture → which one is the humidifier?" Product recognisable at thumbnail size (≥ ~1/4 of the frame in product-led formats).
- **Casting is targeting.** Andromeda tends to show an ad to people who resemble the person in it (Blueprint live calls) — cast
  the side (age, life stage, setting), not a model. For broad appeal use a multi-person compilation (C4). Cast attainable talent
  matching the buyer's age (Kell).
- **Identity match.** People, scenery and aesthetic must be the world the side wants to belong to (Evolve #8).
- **Elements that hold attention** (Pellegrini's footage patterns, translated to stills): hands using the product · texture
  close-up · side-by-side comparison · POV (the viewer's own hands/eyes) · an emotional element (child, partner, pet, others'
  gaze, safety) · a hard-to-argue demo with a neutral object (egg, sauce, scissors).
- **Zone → look:**

| Zone | Light & colour | Subject & framing | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| COZY | warm, soft, natural daylight; muted brand palette | close, at home, relaxed body language | friendly sentence case, handwriting allowed |
| JOY | bright, saturated, high key | motion, wide smile, outdoors/social | bold, big numbers, offer chips |
| FATIGUE | dim, slightly cool, real (not dramatic) | the tired detail: the hand on the lower back, the 2 a.m. clock | plain, quiet, first person |
| PANIC | high contrast, a single red accent | the stark consequence, no gore | short and factual — compliance first (§10) |

- **Hierarchy:** L1 main line → L2 support → L3 chip → logo (canvas specs and safe zones: `static-formats.md` §0).
- **Exhausted visuals:** scan the category in the Ad Library; if every competitor runs the same white-background packshot or the
  same smiling couple, that look is exhausted (Evolve #14) — choose a scene the feed hasn't seen.
- **Not like an ad:** "Winning ad — ad doesn't look like an ad" (Evolve roadmap learning); native grammar beats banner grammar.

## 6. AI image prompts

**Tools.** Nano Banana is Superscale's default static model; GPT Image 2 is "very strong" for some cases (Blueprint live calls).
Try both on the first concept and keep whichever holds the product. Do not use Meta's in-platform image generation ("not up to
snuff" — Blueprint). Real photography and real customer content stay the gold standard for proof formats; AI is "for
multiplication, not deception".

**Product accuracy (Blueprint live calls)**
1. Attach reference images: close-ups of the packaging, label, logo, cover texture and the product from the exact angle you want.
   Visual references beat descriptions.
2. State exact dimensions and proportions ("the bottle is 2 inches tall with a quarter-inch pump"; "bed 36 × 28 × 6 in").
3. Keep the product fixed in one position and scene; avoid orbit/rotated views of hard shapes.
4. Say it explicitly: "the [product] must look exactly like reference image 1 — same shape, colour, label, logo placement".
5. If the model still distorts it, generate the scene only and composite the real packshot in a design tool.
6. Prompt "a million times over until it's really good"; AI slop hurts trust in the brand.

**Text.** Default: generate the image with clean negative space and set the copy in a design tool (Canva/Figma). It keeps call-out
and headline swaps (the cheapest iterations) to minutes and guarantees exact spelling. If the model renders text, proof every
character against the brief.

**People.** Never present an AI person as a real customer, reviewer or expert; disclose AI people where the law requires it
(e.g. New York); the Moonlighters do not run 100%-AI performers there (Blueprint live calls).

**Prompt framework** — fill every field:
```
[Format + aspect ratio] e.g. "Square 1:1 photo-realistic split image" / "Vertical 9:16"
[Scene / moment]        time, place, light, what just happened (the side's moment)
[Focal subject]         who/what, action, expression, position in frame
[Product]               "exactly as reference image 1", exact dimensions, fixed position and angle, label facing camera
[Casting]               the side: age range, life stage, setting, attainable look
[Zone look]             light and palette from the zone table (§5)
[Composition]           where the negative space for L1/L2 goes; 9:16: nothing important in top 14% / bottom 35%
[Style]                 native phone photo / editorial / studio macro / flat lay
[Text]                  "no text" (default) or the exact strings
[Avoid]                 extra products, extra logos, distorted hands/paws, warped label, stock-photo smiles on pain scenes
```

**Templates** (replace the brackets; attach references every time):
- *Problem–solution split (B1):* "Square 1:1 photo-realistic split image with a thin white vertical divider. LEFT: [pain moment —
  time, place, light], [subject] [tense action], muted cool tones. RIGHT: the same [subject] in [regained moment], warm daylight,
  [product exactly as reference image 1, dimensions, fixed angle] clearly visible. Plain uncluttered top fifth on both halves for a
  headline. Native phone-camera look. No text."
- *Root-cause / mechanism background (B2, D1):* "Clean macro cross-section of [material/body-neutral object] showing [cause] on the
  left and [fix] on the right, neutral background, scientific-illustration lighting, generous empty space above. No text." —
  then draw arrows and labels in the design tool.
- *Us vs them (A1):* "Square 1:1, two-column comparison scene. Left: [product exactly as reference] in use by [side], brand colour
  [hex] accents. Right: a generic unbranded [category item] [failing in a specific, true way], desaturated grey. Same camera
  height both sides. Empty bands for four rows of call-outs. No text, no competitor logos."
- *Testimonial card background (C1):* "Soft-focus [real home setting of the side], [product exactly as reference] on the right
  third, warm natural light, empty left half for a quote card." Quote set verbatim in the design tool; real customer photo if
  you have one.
- *Identity scene (F3):* "Candid photo of [side: age, life stage] [doing the desired activity] in [their world], [product] in use,
  [emotional element], [zone light]. Looks like a friend's photo, not an ad."
- *Texture macro / hands demo (F2):* "Extreme close-up of hands [pressing/applying/stretching] [product material exactly as
  reference image 2], [texture behaviour], shallow depth of field, [palette]."
- *UGC haul / flat lay (F4):* "Top-down phone photo of [bundle contents, each exactly as references 1-4] just unpacked on [a
  rumpled bed / kitchen table], torn mailer and tissue paper, natural window light, slightly imperfect framing."
- *Recurring objection background (C6):* "[Recurring scene — e.g. a patchy East-Coast lawn in late summer], same camera angle and
  light for every image in the set, empty top half for a question and answer."
- *Vertical 9:16 versions:* restate the scene with "vertical 9:16; keep faces, product and the headline area between 15% and
  65% of the height; the bottom third is floor/background only".

**Batch prompts** (once the single image is right): "Using concept C03 (A02 × us vs them) and the attached references, produce
10 big-swing variations changing one element at a time: background, person, product angle, the 'them' failure, call-out set"
(Superscale). "These are my objections [list]; make one static per objection using this background and this layout" (Blueprint
live calls). Save the winning prompt + reference images as a reusable template ("UGC haul static" was saved as a skill).

**QA every generated image:** product shape, colour, label, logo placement vs reference · hands, fingers, paws, faces · no extra
products or logos · text spelled exactly · realistic light and shadows · nothing in the 9:16 dead zones · would a customer
recognise the product in the box when it arrives? If not, it is a misleading ad.

## 7. Iterating a static

Full loop (winner detection, briefs, cadence): `andromeda2-creative-iteration`. What is specific to statics:

**Before iterating, find the variable that won** (Denney): launch each concept with ≥ 2-3 variants. If all variants got spend
and hit CPA, the format is working → make more of the format. If one variant took the spend, compare imagery vs message: if the
images were near-identical, the message won → test that message on other statics and other formats.

**Layers** (Blueprint's six, mapped to a static) — change one per iteration:
| Layer | Static iteration |
|---|---|
| Message | new main line, new emotional trigger (fear vs aspiration vs urgency), new promise frame (speed, ease, certainty, exclusivity) |
| Mechanism | a different feature in the call-outs, a different "why it works", isolate one component |
| Avatar slice | same side, different awareness stage or buyer type (price-sensitive vs status-driven). A different side = a new concept |
| Education | a different myth, mistake or belief |
| Offer | same core offer, different incentive; risk reversal emphasis; time-bound vs value-stacked |
| Visual | same layout, new image: human vs no human, different person, home vs street, plain product; same template, new scene |

**Denney's menu, static-relevant items:** take the winning variable into other formats (image → video hook, → post-it note,
testimonial → social-proof static) · add-ons: social proof, review count, golden-nugget review, press, statistic, bullet
points, before/after where allowed · turn it into a carousel (or put all top performers in one) · add a headline bar · swap the
image · swap the message to a different human desire · call out the audience · add a pet · build on the story of a golden
review.

**Blueprint's image iterations:** "image changes" or "call-out changes" — e.g. the us-vs-them sequence in `static-formats.md` A1.

**Rules:** 1-6 iterations per concept per round; beyond six "gets a little crazy" unless spend is large (Blueprint). A colour or
background swap (black → green) is not counted as a new asset (Blueprint live calls) — an iteration must change what is
emphasised. Iterations keep the winner's copy unless copy is the layer being tested. Launch iterations as a new pack, never
inside the live pack, never by editing the live ad. When a static's angle wins, brief the same angle as a video
(`andromeda2-video-scripts`) — Pellegrini tests angles static-first for exactly this reason. Keep iterating a true winner
"almost never" stops; the exception is a winner whose value is its comments/social proof (Blueprint live calls).

**Reading a static's performance:** statics have no hook/hold rate. Read CPM (relevance and engagement), link CTR (main line +
visual + desire), CPC, cost per ATC/IC, CPP, and spend share inside the pack (`references/metrics-and-diagnosis.md`;
`scripts/a2.py analyze`). High CTR with weak conversion = clickbait or wrong side — tighten the call-out; low CTR with fine CPM
= the main line or the desire is off.

## 8. Seasonal and promo statics

**Black Friday triumph psychology (Pellegrini).** BF buyers "don't run for the discount; they run to get there before their
neighbour". They want the adrenaline of winning, not saving. A deal exclusive to 50 people is worth more than an open deal, even
a cheaper one. The more visible and credible the limit, the stronger the triumph. Your job: give the best feeling of victory.
On the image: show the limit (real number, real date), the haul they win, and the "you got in" moment — not just a percentage.

**Quadruple Offer (Pellegrini)** — one static, four layers, each with its own job:
1. Visible strike-through price (€99 → €59) — the anchor.
2. A free product: BOGO or the complete kit.
3. A mystery gift or voucher — surprise and curiosity at checkout.
4. Free shipping — removes the last brake.
Bonus: an extra promo code on top. Layout: product family hero; price strike as L1; a 3-item "+ free" stack as L2; limit and
deadline chip; free-shipping strip. Check margins first (Evolve: recompute ROAS/CPA targets with the profit calculator before any
offer).

**Urgency that works (Pellegrini) — only when true:** a visible, credible limit · genuinely limited stock (Frøya: "sold out 8
times, limited stock") · a bonus that disappears tomorrow (Serenium) · a short window, 24 h (Megelin: "price drop for 24 hours
only") · 100% cashback for the first 50 orders. Never a static "ends today" that never ends.

**Promo creative set (Evolve BFCM):** offer graphic · new product photo + offer · best-performing image + offer banner ·
best-performing ad + offer banner · new video + offer. "Put sale banners over existing best ads… keep it simple."

**Q4 ladder (Evolve, illustrative):** Halloween 20% → VIP early access + limited stock (lead gen) → pre-BF 25% → VIP night 30% +
special bundles → BFCM 30% → holiday 25% → New Year 25%; expect a pre-BFCM dip (~Nov 11), a BFCM hangover (~Dec 3) and a
shipping-deadline dip (~Dec 17); January: "new you" angles. Plan the creative by mid-November.

**Evergreen offers:** value-add beats discount — buy 2 get 1 can run all year and costs less than 20% off; "find evergreen offers
that give people 20% off sitewide without saying 20% off sitewide"; keep direct offer call-outs to ~20-30% of creative
(Blueprint live calls). Sale statics go in a separate sale campaign so evergreen keeps running when the sale ends
(`andromeda2-launch-testing`).

## 9. Trigger, bottom-of-funnel objection and retention statics

- **Trigger statics (supporting ads):** back in stock, discount, low stock, sale ending, price drop (Blueprint live calls). One
  status line, product hero, date. True only; pause when the event ends.
- **Objection engine:** prompt for every objection (aim for 100), group to the top 10 per side, one static each on a recurring
  background (C6). "If price is an objection, explain that your product lasts two times longer." These are the fastest statics
  to make and often fall into mid-funnel too.
- **Statics from winning videos:** best frame + the winning hook as L1 (F5); also a post-it-note version of the winning script.
- **Retention (existing customers, separate campaign):** new arrivals, bestsellers, restock alerts / "restocking event", catalogue
  of similar products, cross-sell of the companion product, themed drops (BarkBox: a new theme every month). Copy plain: "buy
  again". 1-of-1 content — real customers' photos, repurchase stats, the "six months later" product — is strongest here.
  Manage by frequency (existing customers: 7-day 2-4, 30-day ≤ 15 — `references/metrics-and-diagnosis.md` §8).

## 10. Image compliance checklist

Run on every static (psychology toolkit §12 is the master list):
- **No personal-attribute assertions** on the image ("Overweight?", "Your diabetes", "Signs you have…"); first person, situation,
  behaviour or object instead. Second-person condition callouts are the riskiest pattern in the advertorial swipes.
- **Before/after:** never for weight loss, telehealth, health conditions or body shape; elsewhere realistic, same angle and light,
  real customer with consent, dated; no AI "results".
- **Body imagery:** no zoom-ins on body parts, no scales with numbers, no underwear/revealing clothing, no shocking or
  sensational images (Blanchard, Kell). Health brands: lead with the fascinating benefit and the end outcome, not the shameful
  negative.
- **Health claims:** describe the experience and the mechanism, never "cures"; no time-bound outcome promises ("lose 20 lbs in 30
  days"); no "miracle/breakthrough/secret", no ALL CAPS or "!!!"; medication names not in headlines; add "if medically
  appropriate / prescription required" where it applies; no price or discount mentions and no comparison to other treatments in
  telehealth (Blanchard).
- **Proof:** every number, review, rating, press logo, expert and "as seen in" is real and documented; placeholders replaced
  before launch; no invented studies, doctors or publications; no fake news mastheads.
- **People:** AI people never shown as customers or experts; disclose AI where required; celebrity likeness only with rights.
- **Scarcity and deadlines:** only true; no perpetual countdowns.
- **Native formats:** no fake profiles, fake comments, fake verified badges, or invented customer chats.
- **Misleading UI:** no fake play buttons, notification badges or buttons that do nothing; no misleading thumbnails.
