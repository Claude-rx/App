# Script formats — catalogue, beat sheets, timing, production

Open this after the side card, kindergarten pitch and mechanism exist. Pick one format per script; a format is the
*template* half of "concept = side + template" (Blueprint). Timings assume ~2.9 spoken words per second, measured on
proven VSLs ("VSL swipes": 14 proven long-form VSL scripts analysed); UGC talkers run slightly faster, so check by reading aloud.

## 1. Length ladder

| Length | Words | Beats that survive | Use for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 s | 40-45 | Hook (0-3 s) → named cause + analogy (3-8) → one felt moment or one proof number (8-12) → ease + risk reversal CTA (12-15) | Demo, POV, offer clip, hook-heavy iterations, low-ticket |
| 30 s | ~85 | Hook → one sensory pain moment → absolution + real cause → reveal + ease → one proof → future image + CTA | Default cold test band is 30-60 s (Blueprint brief for weak first-touch) |
| 45-60 s | 130-170 | + qualify/promise, the enemy, UMS in one line, two future images, risk reversal, CTA calling back to the hook | First-touch problem-solution and UGC (Moonlighters first-touch median 34 s, range 8 s-1:45) |
| 90 s | ~260 | + "who I am" with a twist, a 20-30 s story arc (rock bottom → dismissal → discovery), the do-it-yourself flip, a mini crossroads | Founder story, higher price, mechanism that needs a story |
| Mini-VSL 2-3 min | 350-520 | Full spine: hook, lead, problem mechanism, solution mechanism, product, instructions, proof stack, offer, guarantee, crossroads, 2 CTAs | Complex mechanism, $60+ products, problem-aware health/gadget offers |
| Long-VSL lead | 30 s-2 min front end | Hook + a lead whose only job is to sell watching the full VSL / landing story | Feeding a 5-45 min VSL or a long advertorial |

Drop from every ad under 60 s (VSL swipes): price ladders, bonuses, ingredient-by-ingredient detail, FAQs, long quest stories,
multi-pack logic. They live on the landing page. Offer-led variants keep one beat: `[price] / [per-day reframe] / [guarantee]`.

Where things land:
- **Hook loop paid off** at ~60-70% of runtime (VSL swipes compression guide). Open the next loop before closing the last one.
- **Credentials** never before the first curiosity line (VSL swipes: every script except one founder confession).
- **Product**: in long VSLs the name arrives 40-70% in (VSL swipes); in ads under 60 s show it in use by ~30-50% — Sam's
  problem-solution goes "straight into how your product is the quick, easy, best solution" after the problem; Snow and
  Organifi introduce it even earlier.
- **Offer** after the viewer is past halfway (Oliver: "more than 50% of the way through"); **guarantee** within one beat
  of the offer (VSL swipes: in every script that has one).
- **Close reuses the hook's pain words** and calls back to the opening image (VSL swipes).
- **The most exciting part is the first 10-25%** (Oliver); after it, every sentence needs a reason to keep watching.

Sam's component timing (Blueprint live calls): lead-in 1-3 s ("Okay." / "You wouldn't believe…" — a pre-hook), hook
3-5 s, body "however long it needs to be", CTA 3-5 s ("maybe up to 10 seconds if you're aggressive").

## 2. The universal skeleton

Blueprint ("95 plus percent" of good video ads, "100% ripped from thousands of advertisers"):

| Component | Time | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Micro-hook | 0-1 s | Pattern disrupt: "a cough… put the camera down… brush the makeup for a second." |
| Hook | to ~5 s, **2-3 sentences max** | "Fast, very aggressive, very bold": why this person should watch, the value they'll get; leads straight into the body. |
| Lead-in / body (act 2) | as needed | Problems, why, what was happening before; stack obstacles the viewer relates to; the "second thoughts" (why other options let them down); the crisis = what happens if it stays unsolved. |
| Payoff | short | "Exactly what you did to get the results" — then show the result happening on screen (the hat ad cuts to strangers saying "whoa, cool hat"). |
| Offer + CTA | 3-5 s | Value exchange + timing + risk (Blueprint offer anatomy). |

Three acts mapped to an ad (Blueprint phone-case example): enticing incident = hook ("if you drop your phone every day, I'm
going to show you something that makes it indestructible") → second thoughts ("not like the bulky cases you've seen") →
act 2 obstacles (dropping it, AppleCare, being unreliable) → crisis (broken phone → miss the meeting → lose the job) →
resolution, fast (the perfect result + offer + CTA). Resolution "happens very quickly"; everything else is lead-up.

Open loops: "open loop A. Before loop A closes, loop B opens. Then we close loop A…" — always one question unanswered.

| Format | Best awareness | Lever | Zones | Lengths | Funnel role |

| Demo | Solution / product aware | Mechanism | JOY, COZY | 15-30 s | Hook-rate engine; proof |

| Street interview | Unaware, problem aware | Education, curiosity | JOY, COZY | 30-60 s | Top of funnel reach |
| Us vs them | Solution aware | Mechanism, offer ("sell the gap") | COZY, JOY | 15-45 s | Switching buyers |
| Myth vs truth / listicle / how-to / comment reply | Unaware → solution aware | Education | COZY | 30-60 s | Belief shift, indirect |
| Testimonial montage | Product / most aware | Proof | COZY, JOY | 30-60 s | Mid-bottom funnel, objection killing |
| Mini-VSL | Problem / solution aware | Mechanism + education | FATIGUE → JOY | 90 s-3 min | High ticket, complex mechanism |

Price: longer ads for higher ticket, "multiple short ads" for low ticket; for $1,000+ products long form likely leads
(Blueprint). A 5-10 minute top-of-funnel ad is fine when you need highly qualified cold viewers (Blueprint).

## 4. The formats

Each beat sheet gives the primary length; compress with §1. Footage pattern codes refer to §6.

### 4.1 UGC testimonial

Denney's core UGC script ("the core fundamental script of any UGC ad that converts"): **Hook → Agitate the hook → Product
intro → Tutorial (or benefits/testimonial) → Social proof → CTA.** Break every UGC idea into these blocks before writing.
She spends ~50% of the effort on the hook. Opener that "works particularly well": "As a [identity]…" (first person —
the creator owns the attribute, so it is not a personal-attribute callout).

Snow (1:38, 5.7M views) — the closest long template to a 45-60 s brand ad: fascinating benefit with a time mechanism
("After 21 days my teeth are the whitest they have ever been") → personal story (what always stopped her) → how it works
in 3 easy steps → emotional benefit + social proof ("never been more confident… over 1 million smiles") → rapid
testimonial montage → challenge CTA ("take the 21 Day Challenge"). Kell: leading with the fascinating benefit "is always one
of the best ways for a brand to advertise"; focus on the end outcome, not the shameful negative.

**45 s beat sheet**

| Time | Beat | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00-0:01 | Micro-hook | The moment itself, a prop, a sound |
| 0:01-0:05 | Hook (2 sentences) | Result-first, pain moment or golden-nugget quote |
| 0:05-0:14 | Agitate | One sensory pain scene + what it cost them (life layer) + one emotional line |
| 0:14-0:20 | Tried & failed + real cause | "I tried [A], [B]. Turns out it was [UMP]." |
| 0:20-0:28 | Product intro + how it works | UMS in plain words; hands demo; "takes [30 seconds]" |
| 0:28-0:33 | Proof | A real number, a review card, a third-party reaction |
| 0:33-0:40 | Payoff + dream scene | The moment regained, shown; "What that really means is…" |
| 0:40-0:45 | CTA | Offer + risk reversal, in her voice |

Obvi collagen brief pattern (Denney): alternative-to-expensive-solution hook ("I used this instead of getting botox and
hair extensions") → relatable spend ("I've spent $1000s… can ya relate") → failed solutions → "until I found this" →
simple routine ("one scoop in my morning matcha") → objection handled (no whey, no sugar) → timeframe result → third-party
compliment ("my boyfriend even said…") → quirk ("looks like kids' cereal") → soft CTA.

Best source: real customers (1-of-1 content, §8). Run a polished and an unpolished version (Blueprint).
Pitfall: the creator reading a brand brochure — Pellegrini's losing script ("formula is 100% natural, expert approved").

### 4.2 Problem-solution

Sam's structure for first-touch video (Blueprint "Ads That Scale"):
1. The problem in the first seconds "in really graphic detail" ("Don't use soft or generalist language").
2. Straight into why the product is "the quick, easy, best solution".
3. Trust: data, UGC testimonials, results shown (before/after only where the category allows). "No one wants to be sold snake oil."
4. CTA with offer/risk reversal ("30 days free returns if it doesn't work").
Shorthand: "Hook + avatar call-out together. Maybe a second hook connected to calling out the problem. Meat, meat, meat,
CTA." It should not feel like an ad — the winners "feel real".

Canopy humidifier (Denney, verbatim core): "I know the benefits of a humidifier… However, whenever I buy the cheap ones
online, they always get really dirty. And there was no way that breathing in all of that dust… was good for my skin. Meet
Canopy. This is the first humidifier for skincare…"

**30 s beat sheet**

| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0:00-0:03 | Hook + side call-out (situation or behaviour) |
| 0:03-0:09 | Problem in one graphic, sensory moment |
| 0:09-0:14 | Why the usual fix fails → named cause (UMP) |
| 0:14-0:21 | Product as the easy fix; UMS in one line; hands demo |
| 0:21-0:26 | One proof (number / review / visible result) |
| 0:26-0:30 | Dream image + offer + risk reversal |

TOF universal formula (Blueprint 1-of-1 training): "Clearly hit what the problem is. Position your product as the solution.
Then showcase offer. And proof." Self-check from Sam: are we stating the real problem, positioning the product as the only
unique solution, with a CTA and ideally an offer? "Most people don't. Even the biggest brands don't always do this right."

### 4.3 Founder story

When: a founder willing to be on camera — "the most consistently good ads… period" (Blueprint); later add an influencer
alongside ("F plus I"), not instead. Use for unaware and saturated markets (sophistication 5: story hides the pitch).

**90 s beat sheet** (built from Kell's VSL GOD author-story questions and Charity Water's confession)

| Time | Beat | Line starter |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00-0:05 | Hook: the crisis moment or the industry contrast | "[Category] used to look like this. Now it looks like this." (Sam's hook that "works every time") |
| 0:05-0:12 | Who I am, in one sentence, with a twist | "Even though I'm best known for [X], I've spent [N] years on [Y]." (VSL swipes) |
| 0:12-0:30 | The crisis that started it (sensory, dated) | "[Year], I was [scene]. [What someone said]." |
| 0:30-0:42 | The crazy idea / what I tried / what failed | "So I tried [A]. Then [B]. Nothing held." |
| 0:42-0:55 | The discovery = the mechanism (named + analogy) | "Turns out it wasn't [belief]. It was [UMP]." |
| 0:55-1:05 | First result moment ("Where were you? How did it feel?") + the first other person helped | "The first person I tested it on was…" |
| 1:05-1:15 | Proof at scale (real numbers, real reviews) | "Since then [N] people…" |
| 1:15-1:25 | Moral motive + dream outcome for the viewer | "I made this so [moment] doesn't have to…" |
| 1:25-1:30 | Offer + guarantee + sign-off with name | "I'm [name]. Try it for [N] days." |

60 s version: hook → one-line who I am → crisis (10 s) → discovery/mechanism (15 s) → proof (8 s) → dream + CTA.
Footage: the founder's own world — sourcing, packing orders, calling customers, the workshop (1-of-1 "business shape",
Blueprint). Hook rule: action before face (§6) — open on the hands/the product/the place, voice over, then the face.
Pitfall: credentials first; a talking head with no B-roll for more than 3 s.

### 4.4 Demo

The demo is the visual hook and the proof at once. Pellegrini's "hard-to-contest" visual hooks: a neutral object everyone
knows (egg, sauce, scissors); the demo is real; **silence 0-2 s, visual demo, then the voice reveals**; bonus: a reverse
scene right after the hook. Long-running examples: egg on three soles (Stepprs, 645 and 503 days), sauce slides off
(Crep Protect, 115 days), scissors on the gutter (Zima Dental, 1,328 days).

**15-30 s beat sheet**

| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0:00-0:02 | Silent demo of the result on a familiar object (hands, close-up, no face) |
| 0:02-0:05 | Voice reveals what they just saw + who it's for |
| 0:05-0:12 | Why it works (UMS in one line, the analogy) — show it again, slower or reversed |
| 0:12-0:20 | Second demo on the viewer's real use case (their moment) |
| 0:20-0:26 | Objection handled visually (washable, any size, any surface) |
| 0:26-0:30 | Offer + risk reversal |

Kell Flow Show demo flows: Foam Cleaner (incredible product demo hook → big benefits & features → handle objections → buy
now for discount); GloLabs (text-on-screen product demo → client testimonial with benefits → "holy shit" information →
benefits → mechanism). Gamify progress (Kell on Ridley piano): show day 1 vs month 1 vs year 1 as levels.
Pitfall: a demo the viewer can dispute (cuts during the key moment, CGI look) — keep it in one continuous shot.

### 4.5 POV

Camera = the viewer's eyes. "On se voit faire" (Pellegrini: POV 0-10 s; Mini Microscopes ran 362 days). Denney's POV
product-focused shot: no voiceover, limited human element, "easier for a person to 'see themselves' with your product";
can run on text plus a text-to-speech voice.

**20 s beat sheet**: POV of the side's exact moment (0-3 s, on-screen text names it: "POV: the curb where he stops") →
the problem in action (3-7) → product in hand, used (7-12) → the same moment, now solved (12-17) → text CTA + guarantee (17-20).
Captions carry everything; design for sound off.

### 4.6 Street interview

Sam's example: a deodorant brand interviewing attractive women on the street and showing it to men — the side is the
viewer, not the interviewee. Builds reach with variety of faces (Andromeda matches people in the ad to viewers, §8).

**45 s beat sheet**: a question the side secretly cares about (0-4 s; ask about opinions or situations, never the
interviewee's body or health) → 3-4 fast honest answers (4-15) → the reveal or blind test of the product (15-25) → genuine
reactions (25-33) → one-line mechanism or proof (33-39) → CTA (39-45).
Compliance: real passers-by with signed releases; reactions to a test must be genuine; never present casted actors as the
public or as customers; no personal-attribute questions on camera.
Footage: handheld, mic in shot, cut every 1-2 s between answers.

### 4.7 Us vs them

Side-by-side comparison sits at 3-10 s in Pellegrini's pattern data (Stepprs, 388 days): "proof hard to contest". "Sell
against the competition" appears in about 12 of Kell's 31 winning flows. Leaf razor (Denney, verbatim core): "Okay, let me
show you. Here's a popular disposable brand… Does a pretty good job. No big complaints. Let's bring in the Leaf razor…
the blade refills are 95% cheaper."

**30 s beat sheet**: big promise vs the usual option (0-3) → side-by-side test on the side's real job (3-12) → the reason
(UMS) in one line (12-18) → the gap made explicit — what the extra $ buys if you are priced above market ("sell the gap",
Blueprint) (18-24) → guarantee + CTA (24-30).
Variants: "what you thought you bought vs what you actually got" (Blueprint, apparel); The Ridge flow (big promise vs the
competition → features → unique mechanism → guarantee → no-brainer outro).
Compliance: comparisons must be true and substantiated; prefer a category ("a popular disposable") over naming a
competitor; health/telehealth: no comparisons to other treatments.

### 4.8 Education formats

The education lever: "Oh… THAT's the real problem" — a belief update that makes the product the logical conclusion
(Blueprint). From Denney's 10 UGC strategies:
- **Myth vs truth**: "Four myths on modern wallets. Myth number one…" (Ridge). Beats: myth → truth + proof → next myth →
  product as the truth embodied → CTA.
- **Listicle**: "3 reasons why", "3 money tips" (KOHO). Number the structure on screen; the product is the last reason or
  "best of all, you can do all of this with one app".
- **How-to / tutorial**: authority intro → "bookmark this video" → tips 1-2-3 (one is the product) → visible result → code.
- **Comment reply / TikTok response**: answer the most common — "or even the most unhinged" — question in the comment
  bubble. Rapid Q&A in comment style (Gundry: "Someone commented, 'Does olive oil help prevent snoring?' Snoring? Fact.").
- **Greenscreen**: creator over press, a study, a review or another top performer.
Oliver's myth-bust lead fits all of them: "XYZ is the real cause of [problem], right? WRONG!"

### 4.9 Testimonial montage / compilation

- UGC compilation (Blueprint): best hook from UGC 1, then UGC 2, then UGC 3 — "different people wearing different
  products"; compilations widen who Andromeda matches (§8) and read as proof in numbers (Denney: compilations performing
  better can mean people need more social proof).
- St Jude sandwich (2:00, 58M views, Kell): testimonial, testimonial, CTA, testimonial, testimonial, CTA + bonus,
  testimonial, testimonial. "Organize them into a list like this. And you'll be able to move the world."
- Rapid one-liners (Kell, Yoga Burn): "I start seeing results like right away." "I gained 4 inches by the end of it."
- Role: mid to bottom funnel, "undeniable proof… ridiculous objection handling" (Blueprint 1-of-1).
**30 s beat sheet**: strongest golden-nugget line as hook (0-3) → 4-6 clips of 3-4 s, each a different objection or
moment (3-24) → number of customers / rating on screen (24-27) → CTA (27-30). Real customers only.

### 4.10 Mini-VSL (60-180 s)

AceMend's 11-point structure (Oliver: ran 1.5+ years, "use this exact script… for any low-ticket (less than $500) offer"):
1 hook (call-out + reason behind the problem) → 2 problem → 3 problem mechanism → 4 solution mechanism → 5 alternatives
(temporary, miss the real root cause) → 6 authority + proof → 7 product mini-pitch (what it is, what it does, how it
delivers the UMS) → 8 instructions (fast, easy, vivid physical benefits) → 9 social proof stat → 10 testimonials in the
market's words → 11 offer/close (guarantee + reason, big emotional promise, crossroads, CTA).
Fill-ins: "If you suffer from [problem] it's because of [real root cause], which is caused by [common activities]." ·
"This is [product], a [physical description]. It uses [UMS] to [how it works/benefit] + [features]."

**120 s beat sheet**

| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0:00-0:05 | Hook: callout + real cause teased |
| 0:05-0:20 | Lead: myth-bust or pain story; open loop on the cause |
| 0:20-0:40 | Problem mechanism + analogy + one real study/number |
| 0:40-0:50 | Why the usual fixes fail (cost, relapse) |
| 0:50-1:05 | Solution mechanism → product reveal, hands demo |
| 1:05-1:15 | How to use (seconds a day) + first-use moment |
| 1:15-1:30 | Proof: stat + one testimonial with a timeline |
| 1:30-1:40 | CTA #1 with offer and guarantee (early CTA — zuPOO repeats its CTA at ~1/3 and the end) |
| 1:40-1:52 | Dream scenes + brand-safe crossroads |
| 1:52-2:00 | CTA #2, calling back to the hook |

90 s: merge 0:40-0:50 into the mechanism; one testimonial; one CTA. 3 min (RemedyCup, "$5 million" 3-min ad, "very
indirect… not aggressive in its promises"): add a scale-of-problem beat, a hero (frustrated professional) and
feature-by-feature proof. Organifi Pure (2 min) shows the minimum: visual contrast hook → agitate → product intro →
mechanism named by an acronym → outcome → risk-free CTA.
Footage: documentary feel — clips, UGC, simple 2D/3D mechanism animation, red circles and graphs on numbers; "it should
not have branding or 'salesy' elements" before the product appears (Oliver); after the product appears, "only happy,
positive, high-quality clips. The emotions you evoke here will be attached to your product."

### 4.11 Long-VSL lead (30 s-2 min)

Kell's architecture: Hook (first 3-6 s) → Lead (30 s-2 min "mini-VSL" whose job is "to sell you on watching the whole
video") → VSL (20-45 min). "By consistently creating dozens and dozens of leads, we can keep a VSL lasting for years";
shoot 10-20 leads at a time. Sam: a 15-minute VSL where only the first 15 s-1 min changes per test — "I love this."
Gundry rule (Oliver): "DO NOT sell in their leads" — capture attention with interesting information for 30 s.

Lead types (Kell's Lead Bible + Oliver), brand-safe defaults:
| Lead | Formula | Brand-safe default |
|---|---|---|
| Value | "Here's an incredible way to solve your problem, but it's not as good as this next way" | 1-3 genuinely useful tips → "But there's something far more powerful." |
| Mistake (Kell's "fear") | "Here's a big mistake you can make with your goal, try this instead" | Name the method, never the viewer's flaw; no "ruin/kill/destroy" |
| Story | "This person went from zero to hero by learning what we're about to show you" | Real person, real outcome |
| Comparison | Two objects side by side: "Can you tell the difference?" (chicken 1957 vs 2005) | Objects, not bodies |
| Dream comparison | "They have a magical secret to solve their problems, and so do we" | Envied-exception hook (some people never seem to…) |
| Important question | "What's the deal with this? Is it the real problem?" → "Actually it's both!" → "It's really WHICH…" | Use answerthepublic-style real questions |
| Myth-bust (Oliver) | "[X] is the cause, right? WRONG!" | Real study behind the reversal |
| Indirect (Oliver) | Talk about something loosely related (nose strips for an anti-snoring product); nothing to believe yet | For aware and saturated markets: raises view rate "without sacrificing quality of traffic" |

Pivot line (recurs nearly verbatim across Kell's leads): "But the real issue isn't [X]. There's something bigger going on
that you need to know about." Watch-CTAs: "I've made a free video to explain it all"; "Stick around for the next 60
seconds"; "We made a presentation for people like you". Kell's lead target: ~10% still watching at 60 s (2020-era
benchmark; judge against your own baseline).

### 4.12 Offer clip and offer injection

- Rescue an ad that hooks and clicks but breaks between checkout and purchase: keep the first frames, inject an offer
  segment, then the body (Sam). Or end with the person on camera: "by the way, we're doing buy two, get one free right now."
  "Don't put like timelines on it" unless a real deadline exists.
- Most-aware offer clip (8-20 s): the deal → why now (real reason) → risk reversal → one-tap CTA.
- Sales periods (Pellegrini, Black Friday "psychology of triumph" — they want to win, not save): the quadruple offer
  (visible strikethrough price, a free product/BOGO, a mystery gift or voucher, free shipping) with a visible, credible
  limit that is true (real stock, a bonus that really disappears, a real 24-hour window, "100% cashback for the first 50").

## 6. Visual layer

### 6.1 Footage patterns (Pellegrini, his accounts' winners vs losers; ROAS of ads carrying the pattern)

| Pattern | Where | Why it holds | ROAS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hands demo | 0-3 s | The brain follows the action | 2.14 |
| Product / texture close-up | 0-3 s | Immediate visual proof | 1.98 |
| No face before 15 s | 0-15 s | Action beats a head | 2.3 |
| Side-by-side comparison | 3-10 s | Proof hard to contest | 1.9 |
| POV, subjective camera | 0-10 s | "We see ourselves doing it" | 1.7 |
| Emotional element | 0-10 s | A child, a deep desire, the gaze of others, safety | 2.4 |
| Fast cuts | Throughout | A new shot every 1-3 s | 2.5 |

His agent's reading of one account: face close-up in the hook → low retention; bust shot → high; hands demo → maximum.
These are correlations; test one pattern at a time. To find your own account's patterns, run his footage-analysis
routine: last 90 days, top 30 spenders above target ROAS vs top 30 below ROAS 1; per video, frames for the first 15 s +
transcript; tag footage (the 7 patterns, first-shot length, shots in first 15 s), psychology (angle, side, mechanism,
awareness, self-concept, zone) and script (does the hook open a loop; desire or feature; anchored or floating proof);
keep patterns present in 5+ creatives; output 3 patterns to impose on editors and 3 tests for this week.

### 6.2 Editor brief (give verbatim to human and AI editors)

> Cut every 2-3 s at most. Movement in every shot. One footage per statement: if the voice says "Marc bends down", show
> Marc bending down. No static moment. (Pellegrini)

Plus:
- **Emotion congruence** (Oliver): pain lines → dark, somber, close, slow; never stock footage of people smiling under a
  pain line ("this type of incongruence breaks interest… and causes drop-off"). Outcome lines → bright, moving, human.
  After the product appears, only happy, high-quality clips.
- **Mechanism** → simple 2D/3D animation or a physical analogy prop; big on-screen text, red circles and graphs on numbers.
- **Transitions**: simple cuts and fades keep engagement (Oliver); avoid effects that read as an ad template.
- **SFX**: a sound on the micro-hook (leash clip, squeak, pour, snap) and on the payoff moment.
- **Music**: licensed only — TikTok Creative Center commercial library or a stock library (a Blueprint client was fined
  $5,000 for trending label audio, more than once). Low under the pain, lift on the payoff.
- **B-roll purposes** (Kell's shoot list, grouped): credibility (lab, research, awards, real press), in demand (busy desk,
  people waiting), the customer journey (the "normal appointment", the use moment), contrast (shaking head at the old
  fix), reaction shots (speechless, surprised), warmth (handshakes, open body language).
- Don't expect creators to edit like editors: hire a real editor and send them this brief (Blueprint). Ask the editor for
  5 hook variants per body (Oliver).

### 6.3 Sound-off and rooms

- Burned-in captions always: large, few words on screen, matching the audio exactly (Kell: most people watch muted; older
  viewers need big fonts).
- The on-screen headline carries the hook on its own; read the ad with captions only — it must still sell.
- Keep captions and headlines inside Meta's current Reels/Stories safe zones (Denney's QA uses a safe-zone guide); the UI
  covers the top and bottom bands and the right-side buttons.
- Deliver 9:16 (Reels, Stories) and 1:1 (Feed); frame the key action centre-safe so the 1:1 crop works. 4:5 is no longer
  the standard (doctrine R2; Kell's older playbook still said 4:5).
- Reels-native beats polished: creator-shot, native text, real rooms. "A VSL running in-feed… should look like an
  interesting video, a mini-documentary, or educational content" (Oliver).
- Versions for rooms: for a strong concept write variants for different trigger moments (3 a.m. pain vs Saturday ambition,
  doctrine R2). Denney's age breakdowns: images and long-form video skew 45+; Reels and Stories 18-35.

## 7. Creator brief template

Two parts (Blueprint); "you must start with the top, the internal pieces… the avatar needs to be the focus." Don't
over-brief: "Choose them for their tonality… They know how to make viral content. Let them rip." Give multiple hooks,
multiple call-outs and a shot list and one package yields "20 to 30 assets", not four.

```
INTERNAL
Core concept:      [template] × [side]   e.g. "UGC in car — press-ons for moms who don't have time but still want great nails"
Template / inspo:  [links to reference videos]
Side:              wants, needs, outcomes, the moment, words they use
Call-outs to land: mechanism [UMS name + 1 line] · education point · offer · proof (real numbers)
Concept or iteration of: [live ad name]

CREATOR
Hooks (say first, 3-6 takes, 1-2 s pause before each):
  1. [...]   2. [...]   3. [...]
Must-say call-outs (your words are fine; these points must be there):
  - [...]
Story prompts (answer in your own words, ~30-60 s): the moment it was worst · what you tried · the day you noticed a change · what you can do now
Shot list:
  - Testimonial x1 (selfie or tripod, eye level, window light)
  - The moment: [exact scene, e.g. "dog stopping at the curb, POV low"]
  - Product in use x3 (no VO) · product close-ups x5 (no VO) · unboxing x1 (no VO, no edit)
  - Payoff scene: [the regained moment, e.g. "dog jumping into the car"]
  - Stills: [e.g. smiling with product, product in a cool spot]
Specs: 9:16 · natural light on face · no filters or colour grade · 1-3 s extra at start and end of every clip ·
  AC/fans off · no logos on clothing · clean background · no music (we add licensed music) ·
  raw clips via [Drive] within [5] days of receiving product · 20+ raw clips including the testimonial
Do not say: [cures/treats/miracle] · [before/after body claims] · questions about the viewer's body or health ·
  results we can't prove · competitor names
```
Address the side in the hook ("If you're a mom with no time…") or by casting (show the mom). With an expensive creator,
ask for a mock version (a call or quick phone video) before the shoot. Same proven creators can shoot "a second piece that
is different" for little extra, which is the cheapest way to get a new template (Blueprint).
Sourcing: Meta Creator Marketplace first (save to list AND email AND message — "always do both"; ~10k followers is fine),
Billo, Instagram DMs. When a creator works, use them a lot and keep them close — "they will not last forever".

## 8. Production

**Casting is targeting.** Sam: "if you show a guy wearing this product, they're going to show the ad more to guys… This is
just how Andromeda works." Cast the side, realistically and attainably — Kell's "landmine": "always use actors/actresses
that reflect a realistic and obtainable goal"; his 40-85 skincare buyers "HATE seeing brands using younger models", so
show 40+ women who look great for their age. Equally, don't make the viewer feel old ("don't show me old people to sell to
me" — a 40-year-old about back pain): cast at the side's age or slightly aspirational, never unattainable. Use
multi-person compilations to widen reach. Choose for tonality over looks.

**Real customers (1-of-1 content).** "Only content that you could produce in your unique situation" — results, volume over
time, your position, real customers (Blueprint). Keep a wins log; call high-repeat reviewers, interview them on Zoom,
send product, "give her whatever she needs to produce this content". People freeze without structure: give a shape
("answer this question, say this, say this… keep it to 30 seconds"). Kell's "rock bottom story" casting pays real
customers for real stories ("submissions without a story will be instantly denied").

**Founder / expert interview** (Kell's VSL GOD questions — after each answer ask "say that again in [7/20/30] seconds"):
Who are you in one sentence? · What was the crisis moment? · What was the crazy idea? · What did you try? · The first amazing
result — where were you, what were you doing, how did it feel? · Who didn't believe you? · Who was the first other person it
helped? · What's the root cause you discovered, in one sentence? · The solution, in one sentence? · Your best real proof?
Mechanism check (Kell): the core problem · the real reason it couldn't be solved · the mechanism that overcame it · how the
product delivers it.

**Spokesperson / UGC spec** (Kell): a 60-second script, phone selfie, good light on the face, normal clothes, home
background (bathroom, living room, office), a teleprompter app so it doesn't look read.

**UGC vs AI.** Real UGC "is not gone… super functional, very strong"; AI is "a very small percentage" of the Moonlighters'
video spend. An AI avatar of a real founder (with consent) performs; "AI slop… is going to hurt trust of the brand."
"AI ads are not used for deception, they're used for multiplication" (Sam). Rules: never present an AI person as a real
customer or a real testimonial; disclose where the law requires (New York requires disclosure of synthetic performers —
the Moonlighters don't run 100% AI people there); keep AI for B-roll, mechanism visuals, versions and hook variants.

**Product accuracy in AI video** (Blueprint AI flywheel): upload close-up packaging images as references; state
dimensions ("the bottle is two inches tall with a quarter-inch pump"); for hard shapes avoid orbit shots and keep the
product fixed in one position; attach the exact image ("the serum bottle looks exactly like this"). Visual references beat
descriptive text. Reject any frame where the label, colour or proportions drift.
